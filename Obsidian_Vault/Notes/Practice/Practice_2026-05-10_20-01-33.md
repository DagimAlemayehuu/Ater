## 🎯 THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | SCENARIO
In an economy where the central bank has set a reserve requirement of 10% for commercial banks, if a commercial bank receives a $100 million deposit and currently has $500 million in reserves, what happens to the bank's excess reserves when it makes a $50 million loan to a customer?


---


## 🔐 SOLUTION KEY (DO NOT PEEK)

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers and technical logic</summary>

#### Q1 Logic
- **Definitive Answer:** `The bank's excess reserves decrease by $50 million, but then increase by $5 million due to the money creation process, resulting in a net decrease of $45 million.`
- **Mechanism:** Initially, with $500 million in reserves and a 10% reserve requirement on deposits, the bank's required reserves on the new $100 million deposit are $10 million. Thus, it has $500 million - $10 million = $490 million in excess reserves before making the loan. When it makes a $50 million loan, it reduces its excess reserves by $50 million to $440 million. However, the $50 million loan increases the money supply and eventually deposits back into the banking system. Assuming the loan proceeds are deposited back into the banking system (for simplicity, let's say into the same bank), the bank now has $100 million (initial deposit) + $50 million (loan proceeds) = $150 million in deposits. The required reserves on $150 million at 10% are $15 million. So, the bank needs to hold $15 million in required reserves against these deposits. It currently has $500 million - $50 million (loan) = $450 million in reserves. Thus, its required reserves are $15 million, and it has $450 million - $15 million = $435 million in excess reserves. The initial analysis missed accounting for the full impact of money creation and reserve requirements accurately. Correctly, after the loan, the bank's reserves are $450 million, and it must hold 10% of $150 million ($15 million) in required reserves, leaving $435 million in excess reserves. The accurate point is the bank's ability to lend and the money multiplier effect on reserves. The decrease in excess reserves due to lending $50 million directly affects its liquidity but the system perturbation trace involves understanding the money multiplier effect which in a simplified view would adjust required reserves and excess reserves dynamically.
