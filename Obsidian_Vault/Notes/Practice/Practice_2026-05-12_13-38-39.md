## THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | MCQ
In a developing economy, what happens to the demand for domestic goods when the exchange rate depreciates, assuming all else remains constant?

- [ ] **A)** The demand for domestic goods decreases because imports become more expensive.
- [ ] **B)** The demand for domestic goods increases because domestic goods become cheaper for foreign buyers.
- [ ] **C)** The demand for domestic goods remains unchanged as exchange rate fluctuations only affect services.
- [ ] **D)** The demand for domestic goods decreases because domestic consumers switch to imported goods.

---


## SOLUTION KEY

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers</summary>

#### Q1
- **Answer:** `B`
- **Explanation:** When the exchange rate depreciates, domestic goods become cheaper for foreign buyers, which typically increases the demand for domestic goods from foreign markets. This is a fundamental concept in international trade and economics, reflecting how changes in exchange rates can impact the competitiveness of a country's exports.
