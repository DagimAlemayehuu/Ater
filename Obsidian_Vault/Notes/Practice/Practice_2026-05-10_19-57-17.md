## 🎯 THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | TRUE FALSE
In the context of development economics, if a government increases its spending on infrastructure, and this increase is financed through higher taxes, then the aggregate demand in the economy will remain unchanged.

- [ ] True
- [ ] False

---


## 🔐 SOLUTION KEY (DO NOT PEEK)

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers and technical logic</summary>

#### Q1 Logic
- **Definitive Answer:** `True`
- **Mechanism:** According to the principles of macroeconomics, an increase in government spending financed by higher taxes represents an increase in government expenditure (G) which is a component of aggregate demand (AD). However, the increase in taxes will reduce disposable income, and thus consumption (C), another component of AD. The net effect on aggregate demand depends on the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) and the multiplier effect. However, in a simple analysis assuming the tax increase directly offsets the spending increase without changing the overall level of aggregate demand, the statement could be considered true. This scenario assumes that the increase in government spending is exactly balanced by the decrease in private consumption due to higher taxes, leading to no net change in aggregate demand.
