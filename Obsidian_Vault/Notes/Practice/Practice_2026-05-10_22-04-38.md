## THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | MCQ
In a developing economy, if the government increases its spending on infrastructure, what happens to the aggregate demand and the country's economic growth rate?

- [ ] **A)** Aggregate demand decreases, and the economic growth rate slows down
- [ ] **B)** Aggregate demand increases, and the economic growth rate accelerates
- [ ] **C)** Aggregate demand remains unchanged, and the economic growth rate remains steady
- [ ] **D)** Aggregate demand decreases, but the economic growth rate accelerates

---


## SOLUTION KEY

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers</summary>

#### Q1
- **Answer:** `B`
- **Explanation:** When the government increases its spending on infrastructure, it directly injects more money into the economy, which increases aggregate demand. This increase in aggregate demand can lead to an increase in production, employment, and ultimately, the country's economic growth rate. This is based on the Keynesian economic theory, which suggests that government intervention through fiscal policy can help stimulate economic growth during periods of economic downturn.
