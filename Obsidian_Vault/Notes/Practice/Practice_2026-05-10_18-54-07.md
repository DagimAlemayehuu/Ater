## 🎯 THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | TRACE
In a developing country, the government increases the money supply by 10% to stimulate economic growth. If the initial increase in money supply leads to a 5% rise in aggregate demand, and assuming a marginal propensity to consume (MPC) of 0.8, what happens to the level of investment in the economy?


---


## 🔐 SOLUTION KEY (DO NOT PEEK)

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers and technical logic</summary>

#### Q1 Logic
- **Definitive Answer:** `B`
- **Mechanism:** The increase in aggregate demand and production leads to an increase in income levels. With an MPC of 0.8, consumption increases, but the remaining 0.2 of the increased income (marginal propensity to save) could potentially be channeled into savings and investment. The rise in aggregate demand can lead to an increase in investment as businesses seek to increase production capacity to meet the higher demand, assuming that the increase in money supply does not lead to significantly higher interest rates that would crowd out investment.
