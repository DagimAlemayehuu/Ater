## 1. Mental Model

A student, planning to buy oranges for the week, checks the market price and decides how many oranges to purchase. If the price is high, they might buy fewer oranges or consider alternative fruits. Over time, their demand for oranges may change as their budget or preferences shift. The student's decision is influenced by their eligibility to buy oranges at a given price, which is determined by their willingness and ability to pay.

## 2. Causal Mechanism

The concept of Time And Eligibility is closely related to the [[Demand_Curve]], which represents the relationship between the quantity of a commodity demanded by an individual at different prices per time period. The demand for a good or service, such as oranges, is defined as the quantity that consumers are willing and able to buy at a given market price in a given time period, ceteris paribus. This concept is essential in understanding how the market reaches [[Market_Equilibrium]], where the quantity of goods supplied equals the quantity demanded. Changes in eligibility, such as a student's budget constraint, can cause a change in equilibrium, shifting the Demand Curve to the left or right.

### Key Takeaways:

- The demand for a good or service is influenced by the consumer's eligibility to buy it at a given price.
- A change in eligibility can cause a change in the quantity demanded, shifting the Demand Curve.
- Understanding Time And Eligibility is crucial in analyzing how markets reach Market Equilibrium.
- The elasticity of demand, including Price Elasticity Of Demand and Arc Elasticity Of Demand, can be affected by changes in eligibility.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of Time And Eligibility assumes that consumers have perfect information about the market price and their own budget constraints. However, in reality, consumers may face uncertainty or information asymmetry, which can affect their eligibility to buy a good or service. Additionally, changes in eligibility may not always lead to changes in demand, as consumers may have limited substitutes or face [[Substitutes_And_Eligibility]] constraints. Furthermore, the concept of Time And Eligibility may not be applicable in all markets, such as those with [[Inferior_Goods]] or [[Normal_Goods]], where demand behaves differently.

## 5. Walkthrough

**Step 1:** The demand for a good or service is influenced by several factors, including its price, consumer income, price of substitutes, taste and preferences, and eligibility.

**Step 2:** A change in the price of the good (P) results in a movement along the demand curve, while changes in other factors (Y, P_s, T, E) shift the demand curve.

**Step 3:** Eligibility (E) affects the demand curve by changing the consumer's ability or willingness to buy the good at a given price.

**Step 4:** Understanding these shifters is crucial in analyzing how markets reach market equilibrium.
