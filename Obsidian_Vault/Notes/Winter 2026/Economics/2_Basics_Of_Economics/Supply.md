## 1. Mental Model

A farmer, John, has been growing wheat on his land for years. One day, he invests in a new, more efficient tractor that allows him to cultivate and harvest his land faster and with less labor. As a result, John is able to produce more wheat than he did before. This scenario illustrates how a change in technology can impact the supply of a good.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The concept of supply is directly influenced by a firm's ability to produce and deliver goods to the market. A [[Technological_Advancement]] can enable a firm to produce more, thereby increasing supply. For instance, with the introduction of a new, more efficient tractor, John, the farmer, can produce more wheat. The time a producer has to respond to price changes also affects supply; the more time a producer has, the more elastic the supply. Additionally, producers with spare or excess production capacity can quickly respond to price changes, resulting in a more elastic supply, assuming variable factors are readily available. This is closely related to the [[Law_Of_Supply]], which describes the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity supplied.

### Key Takeaways:

- A technological advancement can increase a firm's supply by enabling it to produce more.
- The time a producer has to respond to price changes affects the elasticity of supply.
- Producers with excess capacity can respond more quickly to price changes.
- Understanding supply is crucial for analyzing [[Market_Equilibrium]], where the supply and demand curves intersect.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of supply assumes that producers have the ability to adjust their production levels in response to price changes. However, in reality, there may be limitations to this ability, such as time constraints (see [[Determinants_Of_Elasticity_Of_Supply]]) or lack of access to variable factors. Additionally, the relationship between supply and price may not always be straightforward, particularly in markets with [[Substitute_Goods]] or [[Inferior_Goods]]. Furthermore, external factors, such as changes in government policies or weather conditions, can also impact supply. The concept of supply also relies on the assumption that producers are rational and aim to maximize profits.

## 5. Walkthrough

**Step 1:** The introduction of a new, more efficient tractor represents a technological advancement.

**Step 2:** This advancement increases Farmer John's efficiency, allowing him to produce more wheat.

**Step 3:** As a result, Farmer John's supply of wheat increases, contributing to a greater overall supply in the market.

**Step 4:** The increased supply helps meet market demand, moving the market towards equilibrium.

**Step 5:** The technological advancement also reduces Farmer John's production costs, leading to increased profitability.

**Step 6:** The increased profitability encourages further investment in technology, potentially leading to additional advancements.
