## 1. Mental Model

When a severe cold snap hits Florida, citrus farmers struggle to meet the demand for fresh oranges. As a result, the price of oranges surges. Consumers, seeking alternatives to oranges, turn to apples, which become more attractive due to their relatively lower price. This scenario illustrates how changes in market conditions can affect the demand for substitute goods.

## 2. Causal Mechanism

The concept of substitutes and eligibility is closely related to how changes in market conditions affect the demand for goods. When the price of a good increases, consumers may seek out substitute goods, which are products that can satisfy the same needs or wants as the original good. For instance, if the price of coffee increases, consumers may switch to tea as a substitute. This behavior is reflected in the [[Demand_Curve]], which shows how the quantity demanded of a good changes in response to changes in its price. The Price Elasticity Of Demand measures the responsiveness of the quantity demanded to changes in price, and it is influenced by the availability of substitutes. Goods with close substitutes tend to have more elastic demand, as consumers can easily switch to alternative products. [[Substitute_Goods]] play a crucial role in determining market outcomes, as they provide consumers with options when prices change.

### Key Takeaways:

- When the price of a good increases, the demand for its substitute goods also increases.
- The availability of substitutes affects the Price Elasticity Of Demand, with goods having close substitutes tend to have more elastic demand.
- Understanding substitutes and eligibility is essential for analyzing how changes in market conditions affect market outcomes, such as price and quantity traded.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of substitutes and eligibility assumes that consumers have access to information about alternative products and can easily switch between them. However, in reality, consumers may face limitations in their ability to switch to substitute goods, such as lack of information or high switching costs. Additionally, the availability of substitutes may change over time, affecting the [[Market_Equilibrium]]. Furthermore, the presence of substitutes can also influence the [[Law_Of_Supply]], as firms may adjust their production levels in response to changes in demand for substitute goods. The concept also relies on the assumption that goods are homogeneous, which may not always be the case in reality.
