## 1. Mental Model

The farmer's market in a small town is bustling with activity as the summer harvest approaches. Tomatoes are in high demand, and farmers are eager to supply them. As the price of tomatoes increases, one farmer, John, decides to expand his production to meet the growing demand. He invests in new irrigation systems and hires additional labor to increase his tomato yield. This scenario illustrates how the elasticity of supply comes into play as farmers like John respond to changes in market prices.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The elasticity of supply, denoted by $E_S$, measures the responsiveness of the quantity supplied of a good to a change in its price. It is calculated using the formula $E_S = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} * \frac{P}{Q}$, where $\Delta Q$ and $\Delta P$ represent changes in quantity and price, respectively. The elasticity of supply can be classified into several categories, including elastic, inelastic, unitary elastic, perfectly elastic, and perfectly inelastic. The availability of infrastructure facilities, such as irrigation systems, can significantly impact the elasticity of supply, making it more elastic if such facilities are readily available [[Determinants_Of_Elasticity_Of_Supply]]. For instance, if the price of tomatoes rises, farmers with access to existing irrigation systems can quickly increase production, making the supply more elastic. Additionally, changes in marginal cost of production also influence the elasticity of supply [[Law_Of_Supply]].

### Key Takeaways:

- The elasticity of supply is influenced by the availability of infrastructure facilities, such as irrigation systems.
- Changes in marginal cost of production also affect the elasticity of supply.
- Understanding elasticity of supply is crucial in analyzing market behavior and making informed decisions about resource allocation [[Market_Equilibrium]].

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of elasticity of supply assumes that producers have the ability to adjust their production levels in response to price changes. However, in reality, producers may face limitations such as fixed production capacities, limited access to resources, or technological constraints. For example, a farmer may not be able to increase production immediately due to weather conditions or limited availability of labor. Furthermore, the elasticity of supply may vary over time, with producers being more responsive to price changes in the long run than in the short run [[Time_And_Eligibility]]. These limitations highlight the importance of considering the specific context and constraints faced by producers when analyzing the elasticity of supply. The concept also assumes that producers have perfect knowledge of market conditions, which may not always be the case.

## 4. Elasticity of Supply Classification

$E_S = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} * \frac{P}{Q}$

| Elasticity  | Description | Example |
|--------------|-------------|---------|
| Elastic      | $E_S > 1$   | Farmers quickly increase tomato production in response to price rise. |
| Inelastic    | $E_S < 1$   | Farmers struggle to increase tomato production due to limited resources. |
| Unitary Elastic| $E_S = 1$  | Tomato production increases proportionally with price rise. |
| Perfectly Elastic| $E_S = \infty$ | Farmers can increase tomato production without limit at a given price. |
| Perfectly Inelastic| $E_S = 0$   | Tomato production remains unchanged despite price changes. |

## 5. Walkthrough

**Step 4:** Elastic supply ($E_S > 1$) means that a small price change leads to a large change in quantity supplied.

**Step 5:** Inelastic supply ($E_S < 1$) means that a large price change leads to a small change in quantity supplied.

**Step 6:** Unitary elastic supply ($E_S = 1$) means that a price change leads to a proportional change in quantity supplied.

**Step 7:** Perfectly elastic supply ($E_S = \infty$) means that suppliers can increase quantity supplied without limit at a given price.

**Step 8:** Perfectly inelastic supply ($E_S = 0$) means that quantity supplied remains unchanged despite price changes.
