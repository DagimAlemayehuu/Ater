## 1. Mental Model

In a small town, a popular coffee shop called "The Daily Grind" has recently opened, offering a wide variety of coffee blends. The shop's owners also sell their own brand of coffee beans, which customers can purchase to brew at home. As a result, the demand for coffee beans in the town increases, illustrating how the demand for one good can be influenced by the availability of another related good. This scenario demonstrates the concept of complementary goods, where the consumption of one good is enhanced by the consumption of another. The success of "The Daily Grind" depends on the availability of both coffee and complementary goods like sugar, creamer, and pastries.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Complementary goods are products or services that are consumed together to satisfy a particular want or need. According to the theory of demand, when the price of one good decreases, the demand for its complementary good increases, and vice versa. For instance, [[Market_Demand]] for printers and ink cartridges are interrelated, as a decrease in the price of printers would likely increase the demand for ink cartridges. This relationship between complementary goods is crucial in understanding [[Market_Equilibrium]], as changes in the price or availability of one good can affect the [[Demand_Curve]] of its complementary good. The demand for complementary goods is also influenced by [[Substitute_Goods]], as the availability of substitutes can impact the demand for both goods.

### Key Takeaways:

- A decrease in the price of one good can increase the demand for its complementary good.
- Complementary goods are often consumed together to satisfy a particular want or need.
- Understanding the relationship between complementary goods is essential in analyzing Market Equilibrium and Demand Curve.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

However, the concept of complementary goods assumes that the goods are consumed together, which may not always be the case. For example, a consumer may purchase a coffee maker but not necessarily buy coffee beans from the same brand. Additionally, the availability of [[Substitute_Goods]] can impact the demand for complementary goods, making it challenging to predict the effects of a price change. Furthermore, the relationship between complementary goods can be influenced by various factors, such as changes in consumer preferences or [[Technological_Advancement]], which can affect the demand for one or both goods. The concept of complementary goods also relies on the assumption that consumers have a stable [[Demand_Function]], which may not always be the case.
