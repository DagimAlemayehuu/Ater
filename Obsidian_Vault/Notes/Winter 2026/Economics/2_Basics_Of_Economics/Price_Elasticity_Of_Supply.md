## 1. Mental Model

The farmers in California are expecting a drought. As the news breaks, the price of wheat futures increases. In response, farmers who have the ability to switch to more drought-resistant crops or have access to irrigation systems can quickly increase their supply of wheat. However, those without such infrastructure are unable to respond as effectively. This scenario illustrates how the availability of infrastructure facilities affects the price elasticity of supply.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The price elasticity of supply measures the degree of responsiveness of supply to a change in price, calculated as $E_S = \frac{\% \ change \ in \ quantity \ supplied}{\% \ change \ in \ price}$. This concept can be calculated using both point and arc methods, with the arc elasticity formula given by $E_S = \frac{Q_1-Q_0}{Q_0+Q_1} \div \frac{P_1-P_0}{P_0+P_1} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} * \frac{P_0+P_1}{Q_0+Q_1}$. The elasticity of supply can be classified into several categories, including elastic, inelastic, unitary elastic, perfectly elastic, or perfectly inelastic. The availability of infrastructure facilities plays a crucial role in determining the elasticity of supply, as seen in the example of the California farmers. ### Key Takeaways:

- The availability of infrastructure facilities can make the elasticity of supply more elastic, as it enables suppliers to respond more effectively to changes in price.
- A key nuance is that the elasticity of supply can vary depending on the time period considered, with longer time periods often allowing for more flexibility in supply responses.
- Understanding the price elasticity of supply is crucial for businesses and policymakers, as it helps them anticipate how changes in market conditions will affect the quantity supplied of a good.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of price elasticity of supply assumes that suppliers have the ability to adjust their production levels in response to changes in price. However, in reality, there may be limitations to this ability, such as constraints on resources or technological capabilities. Additionally, the elasticity of supply may be affected by factors such as government policies, weather conditions, or global events, which can impact the ability of suppliers to respond to changes in price. Furthermore, the concept of price elasticity of supply may not be well-suited to markets with [[Market_Demand]] that is highly uncertain or subject to sudden shocks. The price elasticity of supply also does not account for external factors such as changes in [[Substitute_Goods]] prices. Therefore, it should be applied with caution in real-world scenarios.
