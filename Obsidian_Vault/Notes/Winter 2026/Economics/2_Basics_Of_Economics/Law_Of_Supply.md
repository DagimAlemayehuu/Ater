## 1. Mental Model

The manager of a local coffee shop, anticipating a rise in demand during the holiday season, considers increasing the price of a cup of coffee. Currently, the shop sells 100 cups a day at $2 each. If the manager raises the price to $2.50, the shop can supply 120 cups a day due to increased production efforts. This scenario illustrates how the quantity supplied responds to a change in price.

## 2. Causal Mechanism

The Law of Supply states that, ceteris paribus, as the price of a good increases, the quantity supplied also increases. This relationship can be measured by the price elasticity of supply, $E_S$, which is calculated as $E_S = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$. Alternatively, the arc elasticity of supply can be expressed as $E_S = \frac{Q_1 - Q_0}{Q_0 + Q_1} \div \frac{P_1 - P_0}{P_0 + P_1} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P_0 + P_1}{Q_0 + Q_1}$. The supply can be elastic, inelastic, unitary elastic, perfectly elastic, or perfectly inelastic. A technological advancement enables a firm to produce and supply more in the market, affecting the elasticity of supply [[Technological_Advancement]]. The more time a producer has to respond to price changes, the more elastic the supply [[Time_And_Eligibility]].

### Key Takeaways:

- The price elasticity of supply can be calculated using the formula $E_S = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$.
- A change in technology can increase the elasticity of supply.
- The time available to respond to price changes affects the elasticity of supply.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The Law of Supply assumes that the price change is the only factor affecting the quantity supplied, which may not hold in real-world scenarios where other factors like changes in production costs or government policies can also play a role. Additionally, the concept of elasticity of supply may not apply directly in markets with rigid price controls or in situations where producers have limited ability to adjust production in the short term. Furthermore, the calculation of elasticity can be complicated by the presence of multiple products or varying production capacities [[Determinants_Of_Elasticity_Of_Supply]].
