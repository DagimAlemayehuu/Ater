## 1. Mental Model

At a local farmer's market, on a sunny Saturday morning, consumers gather to buy fresh produce. The price of apples is $3 per pound, and 100 people are willing to buy 2 pounds each. If the price drops to $2, 120 people want to buy 3 pounds each. This scenario illustrates how the quantity demanded of a good changes with its price, reflecting the market demand curve.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The market demand curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity demanded by consumers in the market. According to the theory of demand, as the price of a good increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa. This concept is closely related to the [[Demand_Function]], which expresses the quantity demanded as a function of the price. The market demand curve is derived from the aggregation of individual demand curves and is influenced by factors such as [[Substitute_Goods]], [[Normal_Goods]], and [[Inferior_Goods]]. For instance, if the price of apples increases, consumers may switch to buying pears, which are Substitute Goods.

### Key Takeaways:

- The price of apples is $3 per pound in the given scenario.
- A change in consumers' income can shift the market demand curve for Normal Goods.
- Understanding the market demand curve is crucial for businesses to make informed decisions about production and pricing.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The market demand curve assumes that consumers have perfect information about the market and that there are no external factors affecting demand. However, in reality, factors such as [[Technological_Advancement]] and changes in consumer preferences can influence demand. Additionally, the market demand curve may not hold for [[Inferior_Goods]], which have a negative relationship between income and quantity demanded. The concept also assumes that the market is in [[Market_Equilibrium]], where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.

**Step 1:** The initial scenario shows that at a price of $3 per pound of apples, 100 people are willing to buy 2 pounds each.

**Step 2:** This results in a total quantity demanded of 100 * 2 = 200 pounds.

**Step 3:** When the price drops to $2 per pound, 120 people want to buy 3 pounds each.

**Step 4:** This results in a total quantity demanded of 120 * 3 = 360 pounds.

**Step 5:** The market demand curve illustrates how the quantity demanded changes with the price, showing an inverse relationship.
