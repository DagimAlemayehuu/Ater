## 1. Mental Model
Imagine a simple farm where the quantity of resources, such as land and labor, increases. This increase in resources allows the farm to produce more crops. Now, imagine that the farm also gets new, more efficient farming equipment, which is an advance in technology. With this new equipment, the farm can produce even more crops using the same amount of resources. This is similar to how economic growth occurs in an economy, where an increase in resources or advances in technology leads to an increase in the total output level.

## 2. Causal Mechanism
Economic growth, defined as an increase in the total output level of an economy, occurs under two primary conditions: an increase in the quantity or quality of Economic Resources or advances in Technology. The quantity of economic resources can increase through various means, such as an expansion of the workforce or an increase in the availability of raw materials. On the other hand, advances in technology improve the efficiency of production processes, enabling more output to be produced with the same amount of resources. For instance, a technological innovation in manufacturing can allow for the production of more goods without an equivalent increase in labor or materials. These conditions are fundamental to understanding how economies can grow and develop over time, as they directly influence the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]] of an economy. By increasing the quantity or quality of resources or advancing technology, an economy can shift its production possibilities frontier outward, indicating an increase in its productive capacity and, consequently, economic growth.

### Key Takeaways:
- Economic growth occurs when there is an increase in the quantity or quality of economic resources or advances in technology.
- An increase in the quantity of economic resources means there are more resources available for production.
- Advances in technology improve the efficiency of production, allowing for more output with the same resources.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases
The theory assumes that technology does not change during the year, which might not hold in reality as technological advancements can occur rapidly. Additionally, the theory does not account for external factors such as political instability, natural disasters, or global economic trends that can impact economic growth. The theory also assumes that the increase in resources and technology directly translates to economic growth, without considering potential issues such as resource allocation, distribution of wealth, and environmental impact. Furthermore, the theory does not provide a detailed explanation of how the increase in resources and technology leads to economic growth, and it does not consider the potential diminishing returns on investment in resources and technology. Lastly, the theory does not account for the role of human capital, such as education and skills, in economic growth.

## 5. Walkthrough

2. Step 2: The farm increases its land by 20 units and labor by 2 units. New total: 120 units of land and 12 units of labor.
3. Step 3: With the new, more efficient farming equipment, the farm's output per unit of land increases by 10% and per unit of labor by 5%.
4. Step 4: Calculate the new output: 120 * 1.1 = 132, and 12 * 1.05 = 12.6.
5. Step 5: Assuming the output is directly proportional to land and labor, the new total output is 132 * 12.6 = 1663.2 crops.
6. Step 6: The farm's total output level has increased from 1000 to 1663.2 crops due to the increase in resources and advances in technology.
7. Step 7: This demonstrates how economic growth occurs through an increase in resources or advances in technology, leading to an increase in the total output level.
