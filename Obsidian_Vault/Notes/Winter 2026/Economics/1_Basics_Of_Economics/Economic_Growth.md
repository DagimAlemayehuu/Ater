## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small island economy that produces only two goods: fish and coconuts. The islanders have a certain amount of resources, such as fishing nets and coconut trees, and a certain level of technology, such as fishing techniques and coconut harvesting methods. If the islanders discover a new fishing technique that allows them to catch more fish with the same amount of effort, this would represent an advance in technology and could lead to economic growth. Similarly, if the islanders find a way to increase the yield of their coconut trees, this would also represent an increase in the quality of resources and could lead to economic growth.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Economic growth is an increase in the total output level of an economy. It occurs when one or both of two conditions are met: an increase in the quantity or quality of [[Limited_Resources]] or advances in Technology. The Production Possibilities Frontier (ppf) shows the various combinations of output that the economy can produce given the available factors of production and technology. For example, if an economy has a large workforce and a significant amount of capital, it can produce a large quantity of goods and services. If the economy experiences technological progress, it can produce more goods and services with the same amount of resources. This is why economic growth is closely related to the efficient use of Limited Resources and the Production Possibilities Frontier (ppf). [[Economic_Systems]] that promote efficient allocation of resources and encourage technological progress tend to experience more economic growth.

### Key Takeaways:

- Economic growth occurs when there is an increase in the quantity or quality of economic resources or advances in technology.
- The production possibilities frontier shows the various combinations of output that the economy can produce given the available factors of production and technology.
- Economic growth is closely related to the Production Possibilities Frontier (ppf) and the efficient use of Limited Resources.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

One limitation of the concept of economic growth is that it assumes that the economy can always move towards a more efficient use of resources. However, in reality, there may be [[Market_Failure]] or other external factors that can limit the economy's ability to achieve this. For example, if there are significant externalities or information asymmetries, the market may not allocate resources efficiently, leading to potential limitations in economic growth. Additionally, the concept of economic growth also assumes that technological progress is always possible and that it will always lead to an increase in output. However, in reality, there may be limits to technological progress or it may not always be possible to implement new technologies, which can limit economic growth.
