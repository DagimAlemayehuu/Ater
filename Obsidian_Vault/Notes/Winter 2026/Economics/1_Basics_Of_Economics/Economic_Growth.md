## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Imagine you have a lemonade stand, and you want to make more lemonade to sell to your friends. Economic growth is like when your lemonade stand starts making more lemonade, not just because you're working harder, but because you have better tools or more helpers. This means you can sell more lemonade and make more people happy.

## 2. How the Economics Actually Work

Economic growth happens when an economy can produce more goods and services. This can occur when there are more resources available, like more helpers for your lemonade stand, or when the resources you have become more efficient, like a machine that makes lemonade faster. The [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier_(ppf)]] shows the different combinations of goods and services that can be produced given the resources and technology available. Economic growth is also related to [[Efficiency]] and [[Resource_Allocation]], as the economy needs to use its resources in the best way possible to produce more.

## 3. The Formal Math & Models

Economic growth occurs when there is an increase in the total output level of an economy. This happens under two conditions: (1) an increase in the quantity or quality of economic resources available, or (2) an improvement in the production technology that firms use to turn these resources into output. The production possibilities frontier (PPF) is a graph that shows the various combinations of output that the economy can produce given the available factors of production and the available production technology.

## 5. Where It Breaks (Edge Cases & Flaws)

- **Assumes Constant Returns to Scale**: The model assumes that increasing all inputs by a certain factor will increase output by the same factor, which might not hold in reality.
- **Ignores Diminishing Returns**: It does not account for diminishing returns to scale or the law of diminishing marginal returns.
- **Omission of Institutional Factors**: Factors like governance, regulations, and property rights are crucial for economic growth but are not included.
- **Technological Stagnation**: Assumes technological progress can continuously contribute to growth without bound.
- **Environmental Degradation**: Ignores the potential negative impact of increased production on the environment.
- **Distributive Effects**: Does not consider how growth affects income distribution within the economy.
- **External Shocks**: Fails to account for external shocks such as wars, natural disasters, or global pandemics.
- **Human Capital**: Overlooks the role of education and skills (human capital) in economic growth.
