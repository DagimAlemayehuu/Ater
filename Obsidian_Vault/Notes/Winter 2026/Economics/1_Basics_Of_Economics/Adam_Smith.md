## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small village where resources are limited and people have different needs and wants. The villagers must decide how to allocate their resources, such as land, labor, and materials, to meet their needs. This scenario illustrates the basic questions of resource allocation: what resources are available, how should they be allocated, and for whom should they be allocated. In this scenario, the villagers are like [[Adam_Smith]]'s 'invisible hand', making decisions based on their own self-interest, which leads to an efficient allocation of resources.

## 2. Causal Mechanism

The study of economics, which is about two hundred years old, seeks to answer the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] of what resources are, how they are allocated, and for whom they are allocated. [[Adam_Smith]], a key figure in the study of economics, laid the foundation for understanding how resources are allocated in a market economy. The science of economics is concerned with the allocation of [[Limited_Resources]] to meet [[Unlimited_Wants]]. In a market economy, resources are allocated based on the interactions of buyers and sellers, leading to [[Efficiency]] in the allocation of resources. The concept of Scarcity is central to economics, as it highlights the need for choices about how to allocate resources. Market Failure can occur when the allocation of resources is not efficient, leading to a need for Positive Economics and Normative Economics to understand and address these issues.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of resource allocation assumes that resources are scarce and wants are unlimited. A limitation of this concept is that it assumes perfect information and rational decision-making, which may not always be the case in real-world scenarios. For example, in a real-world market, buyers and sellers may not have perfect information about each other's preferences or resources, leading to market failures. Additionally, the concept of resource allocation also assumes that resources are fungible, meaning they can be easily substituted for one another, which may not always be the case.
