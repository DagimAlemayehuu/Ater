## Mental Model

Imagine you're a farmer with a limited amount of land, water, and seeds to grow crops. You want to produce many different types of fruits and vegetables, but you can't have as much of everything because you don't have enough resources. This is an example of scarcity, where the resources you have are limited, but your desires for what you can produce are unlimited. Scarcity is a fundamental problem in economics because it forces you to make choices about how to use your limited resources.

Scarcity is defined as a situation where the resources available are limited, but people's wants are unlimited. 
- WHAT: The concept of scarcity arises because there are only limited amounts of resources available to produce the unlimited amount of goods and services we desire. 
- WHY: Scarcity exists because human wants are unlimited, but the resources available to satisfy those wants are limited. This mismatch between what we want and what we have creates the fundamental economic problem. 
- HOW: The mechanism of scarcity works as follows: 
  1. Resources such as land, labor, and capital are limited.
  2. Human wants for goods and services are unlimited.
  3. As a result, we cannot produce enough goods and services to satisfy all our wants.
  4. This forces individuals, firms, and governments to make choices about how to allocate their limited resources.
  5. These choices lead to the need to prioritize some wants over others, which is where [[Decision_Making_Units]] come into play.
  6. The scarcity of resources also leads to the concept of Opportunity Cost, which is the value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
  7. The basic questions of resource allocation, such as what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce, are also a result of scarcity.
  8. Different [[Economic_Systems]] have developed ways to address scarcity, such as markets, command economies, and mixed economies.
  9. Understanding scarcity is crucial for making informed decisions about how to allocate resources efficiently, which is a key aspect of [[Economic_Analysis]].
  10. Scarcity also highlights the importance of [[Limited_Resources]] and Unlimited Wants in shaping economic outcomes.

Economics is a social science which studies about efficient allocation of scarce resources so as to attain the maximum fulfillment of unlimited human wants. The formal definition of economics highlights the importance of scarcity in understanding economic behavior. 

* Some students assume that scarcity only applies to poor countries or individuals with low incomes. However, scarcity is a universal problem that affects everyone, regardless of their income level. 
* Others think that scarcity is only a problem of physical resources, but it also applies to time, skills, and other types of resources. 
* Another common misconception is that scarcity means we can't have anything we want, but it actually means that we can't have everything we want, and we have to make choices about how to use our limited resources.
