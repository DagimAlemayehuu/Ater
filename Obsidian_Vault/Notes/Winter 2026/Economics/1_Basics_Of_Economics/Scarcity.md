## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small island with a limited amount of land, water, and food. The islanders have unlimited desires for goods and services, such as more food, shelter, and entertainment. However, the island's resources are limited, and the islanders must make choices about how to allocate them. They must decide who gets the available food, who gets to use the land for farming, and who gets to build shelter. This scenario illustrates the fundamental economic problem of scarcity and choice. The islanders must prioritize their wants and make decisions about how to use their limited resources to satisfy their unlimited desires.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The concept of [[Scarcity]] refers to the fundamental economic problem that arises because resources are limited, while people's [[Unlimited_Wants]] are insatiable. This means that the needs and desires of individuals, firms, and governments cannot be fully satisfied due to the [[Limited_Resources]] available. As a result, choices must be made about how to allocate resources to produce the goods and services that people desire. This is known as the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] problem. The concept of scarcity is central to Economics and is a key aspect of Microeconomics and [[Macroeconomics]]. It is also related to the concept of Efficiency and Market Failure.

### Key Takeaways:

- Resources are scarce while people's wants are unlimited.
- The fundamental economic problem is about scarcity and choice.
- Individuals, firms, and governments must make choices due to scarcity of resources.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of scarcity assumes that resources are limited, but it does not account for potential technological advancements that could increase resource availability. Additionally, the concept of unlimited wants assumes that people's desires are insatiable, but it does not consider the possibility that people's wants may be satiated or that they may prioritize needs over wants. Furthermore, the concept of scarcity does not account for the distribution of resources, which can lead to unequal access to resources and opportunities. The concept also assumes that individuals, firms, and governments have perfect information about the resources available and the wants of people, which is not always the case. Lastly, the concept of scarcity is often studied in a static context, but in reality, economies are dynamic, and resources and wants can change over time.
