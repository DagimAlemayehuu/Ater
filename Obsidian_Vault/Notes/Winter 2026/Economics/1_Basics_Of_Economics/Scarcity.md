## 1. Mental Model
Imagine a small island with a limited amount of land, water, and food. The islanders have unlimited desires for goods and services, such as more food, shelter, and entertainment. However, the island's resources are scarce, and the islanders must make choices about how to allocate them. They must decide how much land to use for farming, how much water to use for drinking and irrigation, and how much food to produce. This scenario illustrates the fundamental economic problem of scarcity and choice. The islanders must prioritize their wants and make decisions about how to use their limited resources to satisfy their unlimited desires.

## 2. Foundational Concept
The concept of [[Scarcity]] refers to the fundamental economic problem that arises because resources are scarce while people's [[Human_Wants]] are unlimited. This means that the needs and desires of individuals, firms, and governments cannot be fully satisfied with the available resources. As a result, individuals, firms, and governments must make choices about how to allocate their resources. The basic economic problem is about Scarcity and choice, as there are only limited amounts of resources available to produce the unlimited amount of goods and services we desire. [[Microeconomics]] studies how individuals, firms, and governments make decisions about how to allocate resources in the face of Scarcity. The concept of Scarcity is central to Definition Of Economics, as it highlights the need for individuals, firms, and governments to make choices about how to use their resources. Economic Systems are designed to allocate resources in a way that addresses the problem of Scarcity.

### Key Takeaways:
- Resources are scarce while people's wants are unlimited.
- The fundamental economic problem is about scarcity and choice.
- Individuals, firms, and governments must make choices due to scarcity of resources.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases
The concept of scarcity assumes that resources are limited, but it does not account for potential technological advancements that could increase resource availability. Additionally, it assumes that people's wants are unlimited, but it does not consider the possibility that people's wants may be satiated at some point. Furthermore, the concept of scarcity is based on the idea that resources have alternative uses, but it does not account for the possibility that some resources may have no alternative uses. The concept also assumes that individuals, firms, and governments have perfect knowledge of the resources available and the wants of people, but in reality, this knowledge may be imperfect. Finally, the concept of scarcity does not account for the distribution of resources and wants across different individuals, firms, and governments.
