## 1. Mental Model
Imagine a small village where people live and work together. The villagers have various desires, such as food, shelter, clothing, and entertainment. However, the village has limited resources, such as land, labor, and capital. The villagers must decide how to allocate these resources to satisfy their unlimited wants. This scenario illustrates the fundamental problem of [[Scarcity]], which requires individuals to make choices about how to use resources efficiently.

## 2. Foundational Concept
The concept of human wants is a fundamental idea in economics. [[Human_Wants]] refer to the desires of individuals for goods and services. The core fact is that human wants are unlimited, while resources are limited. This leads to the basic questions of resource allocation: [[What_To_Produce]], [[How_To_Produce]], and [[For_Whom_To_Produce]]. These questions are at the heart of [[Microeconomics]] and Economic Systems. The study of human wants helps us understand how individuals make choices about how to use resources to satisfy their desires.

### Key Takeaways:
- Human wants are unlimited
- Resources are limited
- Basic questions of resource allocation include what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases
The concept of human wants assumes that individuals have unlimited desires for goods and services. However, this assumption may not hold in real-world scenarios where individuals may have limited access to information or face cognitive biases. For instance, the concept of bounded rationality may limit the ability of individuals to make optimal choices. Additionally, the assumption of unlimited wants may not account for social norms and cultural values that influence individual preferences. Furthermore, the concept of human wants does not consider the impact of externalities on individual decisions.
