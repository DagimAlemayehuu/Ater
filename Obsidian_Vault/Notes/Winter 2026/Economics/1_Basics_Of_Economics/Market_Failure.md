## Mental Model

Market failure occurs when the government intervenes in the economy because the free market cannot efficiently allocate resources on its own. A good example of market failure is when the production of goods and services leads to negative effects on third parties not directly involved in the transaction, such as pollution. For instance, if a computer industry produces computers but also releases a lot of pollution as a byproduct, the cost of this pollution is not accounted for in the market price of the computers.

- WHAT: Market failure refers to situations where the government steps in because the free market fails to allocate resources efficiently. 
- WHY: Market failure exists because the scarcity of resources implies that choices have to be made, and these choices involve costs, known as opportunity costs. The market may not always account for all the costs or benefits associated with the production and consumption of goods and services.
- HOW: 
  1. The economy has limited resources and unlimited wants.
  2. Producers make decisions based on their own costs and benefits.
  3. If production leads to external costs (like pollution) not paid by the producers, these costs are not reflected in market prices.
  4. The government may intervene to correct this by regulating the industry or imposing taxes to internalize these external costs.
- CONNECTIONS: This concept is closely related to [[Scarcity]], [[Limited_Resources]], and Role Of Government. It also ties into [[Economic_Analysis]] as it involves assessing the efficiency of market outcomes.

The source text does not provide a direct definition of market failure but discusses related concepts such as scarcity, opportunity cost, and the production possibilities frontier (PPF). The PPF shows the combinations of output that the economy can produce given its resources and technology. Market failure can be understood as a deviation from the efficient allocation of resources depicted by the PPF, often requiring government intervention.

- Students often assume market failure only occurs due to external factors like government intervention or natural disasters. However, market failure can arise from within the market process itself, such as when producers do not account for all costs.
- Another misconception is that market failure always leads to a complete market shutdown. In reality, government interventions aim to correct inefficiencies, not necessarily halt market activities.
- Some believe market failures are rare; however, they can be quite common due to the prevalence of externalities and information asymmetries in real-world markets.
