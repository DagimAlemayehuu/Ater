## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Market failure occurs when the government intervenes in the economy because the free market cannot efficiently allocate resources on its own. A good example of market failure is when the production of goods and services leads to negative effects on third parties not directly involved in the transaction, such as pollution from a factory. This concept is closely related to the idea of [[Scarcity]], where the limited availability of resources leads to choices and trade-offs.

## 2. How the Economics Actually Work

Market failure happens when the market does not allocate resources efficiently, leading to a loss of economic welfare. 
- WHAT: Market failure refers to situations where the market mechanism fails to achieve an optimal allocation of resources, often requiring government intervention. 
- WHY: The underlying reason for market failure is that the free market does not account for external factors, such as the impact of production on the environment or society as a whole. 
- HOW: 
  1. The market operates based on the principles of [[Human_Wants]] and [[Resource_Allocation]], where consumers and producers make decisions based on their own self-interest.
  2. However, when the production or consumption of goods and services affects third parties, either positively or negatively, the market does not account for these externalities.
  3. As a result, the market may produce too much or too little of a good or service, leading to inefficiencies.
  4. The government may then intervene to correct these inefficiencies, through policies such as taxes, subsidies, or regulations.
- CONNECTIONS: Market failure is closely related to [[Scarcity]], [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]], and the Role Of Government in addressing these issues.

## 5. Where It Breaks (Edge Cases & Flaws)

* **Externalities Not Internalized**: When production costs are not borne by producers, or benefits are not captured by consumers, market failure occurs.
* **Public Goods Provision**: Markets fail to provide public goods, as individuals may not pay for something they can use for free.
* **Information Asymmetry**: When one party has more information than others, market failure can occur due to unequal decision-making.

## 6. Common Misconceptions

- Students often assume that market failure only occurs due to externalities, but it can also result from other factors, such as information asymmetry or public goods.
- Some students believe that government intervention always corrects market failures, but in reality, government policies can sometimes exacerbate the problem.
- Others think that market failure is a rare occurrence, but in fact, it can happen frequently in various markets, such as healthcare or education.
