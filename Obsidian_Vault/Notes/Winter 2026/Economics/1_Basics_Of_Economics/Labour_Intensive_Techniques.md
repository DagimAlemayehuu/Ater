## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Labour intensive techniques refer to methods of production that rely heavily on human labor rather than machines or capital. For example, in the computer industry, a labor-intensive approach might involve manually assembling computers using a large workforce, whereas a capital-intensive approach would involve automating the process with machines. This concept is crucial in understanding how businesses and economies make decisions about how to produce goods and services.

## 2. How the Economics Actually Work

Labour intensive techniques are a type of production method that emphasizes the use of human labor over capital or machines. 
- WHAT: Labour intensive techniques are defined as production methods that rely heavily on human labor rather than machines or capital. 
- WHY: This concept exists because of the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, which implies that choices must be made about how to allocate limited resources. 
- HOW: When a firm or economy chooses to use labor-intensive techniques, it is essentially deciding to use more human labor and fewer machines or capital goods. This decision is often driven by the availability and cost of labor and capital. For instance, in an economy with abundant labor and limited capital, labor-intensive techniques might be more practical and cost-effective.

## 5. Where It Breaks (Edge Cases & Flaws)

* **Limited Labor Supply**: If there is a shortage of skilled or unskilled labor, labor-intensive techniques may not be feasible.
* **High Labor Costs**: If labor costs are high, firms may find it more cost-effective to use capital-intensive techniques instead.
* **Technological Constraints**: In some industries, technology may not be available to facilitate labor-intensive production methods, limiting their adoption.
