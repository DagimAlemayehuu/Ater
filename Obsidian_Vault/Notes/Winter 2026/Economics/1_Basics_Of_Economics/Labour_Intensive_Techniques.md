## Mental Model

Labour intensive techniques refer to methods of production that rely heavily on human labor rather than machines or capital. For example, imagine a small farm that uses many workers to plant, harvest, and pick fruits by hand, rather than using automated machinery. This approach is often used when labor is relatively cheap and abundant, and it allows for more flexibility and adaptability in the production process.

## How the Economics Actually Work

- WHAT: Labour intensive techniques are a type of production method that emphasizes the use of human labor over capital or machines. 
- WHY: These techniques exist because of the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, which implies that choices must be made about how to allocate limited resources. In some cases, using labor-intensive techniques can be more cost-effective or efficient, especially when labor is abundant and cheap. 
- HOW: The mechanism of labor-intensive techniques works as follows: 
  1. A firm or producer decides to use more labor and fewer machines in the production process.
  2. This often involves hiring more workers or assigning more tasks to existing employees.
  3. As a result, the production process becomes more labor-intensive, with workers performing tasks that could potentially be automated.
  4. This approach can lead to increased output, but it also means that the firm must consider the opportunity cost of using more labor, which could include the cost of training, wages, and benefits.
  The concept of labour intensive techniques is closely related to [[Scarcity]], Opportunity Cost, and [[Basic_Economic_Questions]], as it involves making choices about how to allocate resources in the face of scarcity. 
  Additionally, labour intensive techniques can be contrasted with [[Capital_Intensive_Techniques]], which rely more heavily on machines and capital.
