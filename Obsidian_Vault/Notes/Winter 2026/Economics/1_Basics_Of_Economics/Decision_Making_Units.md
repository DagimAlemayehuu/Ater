## Mental Model

In economics, decision-making units refer to the individuals, firms, and governments that make choices about how to allocate resources. Think of it like a school, where the principal, teachers, and students are all decision-making units. The principal decides how to allocate the school's budget, teachers decide how to allocate their time to teach different subjects, and students decide how to allocate their time to study different subjects. These decisions are all about making choices due to scarcity of resources, like time or money.

## How the Economics Actually Work

Decision-making units are a fundamental concept in economics because they are the entities that make choices about how to allocate scarce resources. 
- WHAT: The source text defines decision-making units as the different entities that make economic choices, specifically mentioning "individuals, firms and governments" as examples.
- WHY: These units exist because of the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, which means that people's wants are unlimited, but resources are limited. As a result, decision-making units must make choices about how to use resources efficiently.
- HOW: The mechanism works as follows: 
  1. Individuals, firms, and governments face scarcity of resources.
  2. They have unlimited wants and needs that cannot be fully satisfied with the available resources.
  3. They must make choices about how to allocate resources to meet their needs and wants.
  4. These choices involve trade-offs, which are reflected in the concept of Opportunity Cost.
  5. Decision-making units interact with each other through markets, influencing the allocation of resources and the overall economy.
- CONNECTIONS: Decision-making units are closely related to [[Economic_Systems]], which determine how resources are allocated and how decision-making units interact. They also relate to [[Basic_Economic_Questions]], which are the fundamental questions that decision-making units must answer when allocating resources. Additionally, decision-making units are influenced by [[Scarcity]], which limits their choices and forces them to prioritize.
