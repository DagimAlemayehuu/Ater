## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small island nation. One group of people (Households) provides labor and buys coconuts. Another group (Firms) owns the coconut trees and hires people to pick them. A central committee (Government) sets the rules for picking and ensures everyone gets a fair share of clean water. These three groups are the decision-making units that drive the entire economy through their interactions.

## 2. Causal Mechanism

The economy is composed of three primary agents, each with specific roles and decision-making logic:

1.  **Households**: These are individuals or groups living under one roof who make joint financial decisions. They act in two capacities:
    - **Resource Owners**: They sell their factors of production (land, labor, capital, and entrepreneurship) in the [[Factor_Market]].
    - **Consumers**: They buy goods and services in the [[Product_Market]] to satisfy their wants.
2.  **Firms**: These are production units that use economic resources to produce goods and services.
    - **Buyers**: They purchase factors of production from households.
    - **Sellers**: They sell finished products to households and the government.
3.  **Government**: An organization with legal and political power to control or influence other units.
    - **Providers**: They provide public goods and services (e.g., defense, infrastructure).
    - **Regulators**: They create an enabling business environment through law and order.

The interaction between these units is typically visualized through the **Circular Flow Model**, which shows the movement of resources and money between these agents.

### Key Takeaways:
- **Households** are the ultimate owners of resources.
- **Firms** are the engines of production.
- **Government** acts as a stabilizer and regulator to address market failures.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

- **Mixed Roles**: In many economies, an individual can be a household member, a firm owner (entrepreneur), and a government official simultaneously, blurring the lines between units.
- **Non-Profit Sector**: The model often overlooks non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and charities that act as production units but without a profit motive.
- **Foreign Sector**: A closed-economy model ignores the "External Sector" (International Trade), which is a critical fourth decision unit in open economies.

| Agent | Definition | Primary Decisions / Roles |

## 5. Walkthrough

**Step 1:** Households provide labor to firms.
**Step 2:** Firms use that labor to produce goods.
**Step 3:** Firms pay households wages (income).
**Step 4:** Households use that income to buy the goods produced by firms.
**Step 5:** The Government taxes both groups to provide infrastructure that helps both function efficiently.
