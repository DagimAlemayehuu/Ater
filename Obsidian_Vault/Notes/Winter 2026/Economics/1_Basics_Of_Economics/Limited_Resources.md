## 1. Mental Model

In a small town, the local government has only $100,000 to allocate for community projects, but there are numerous proposals from residents for new parks, community centers, and road improvements, all of which would enhance the quality of life. However, given the limited funds available, the government must choose which projects to prioritize and which to delay or forego. This situation illustrates the challenge of limited resources. The town's residents have unlimited wants, but the government can only allocate the available $100,000. This forces the government to make tough decisions.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The fundamental economic problem of [[Scarcity]] arises because resources are limited while people's wants are unlimited. Resources, in this context, refer to the factors of production used to create goods and services, such as labor, capital, and raw materials. Due to the scarcity of resources, individuals, firms, and governments must make choices about how to allocate these resources efficiently. This necessity for choice is a direct consequence of [[Limited_Resources]], which dictates that not all desires can be satisfied due to the finite nature of resources. The study of economics, defined by the [[Definition_Of_Economics]], involves understanding how societies make decisions about the allocation of Limited Resources.

### Key Takeaways:

- The economic problem of scarcity forces governments, like the one in our example, to make choices about how to allocate limited resources.
- Understanding Limited Resources helps in grasping why Basic Economic Questions about what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce are crucial in any economy.
- The concept of Limited Resources underpins the field of Microeconomics, which studies the decisions made by individuals and firms about how to allocate resources.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of limited resources assumes that resources are scarce relative to wants, but in some cases, resources can be abundant, temporarily alleviating scarcity. Additionally, technological advancements can sometimes mitigate scarcity by making resources more productive or creating substitutes. However, the fundamental issue of scarcity does not disappear; it merely changes form. Furthermore, the allocation of resources is not always efficient due to [[Market_Failure]] and other factors, complicating the challenge posed by limited resources. Ultimately, understanding these limitations is crucial for addressing the economic problem.

## 4. Prioritization Framework for Limited Resources

### Prioritization Framework

Given the limited resources of $100,000, the town government must prioritize projects. Here’s a framework:

1. **Identify Projects**: List all proposed projects (e.g., parks, community centers, road improvements).
2. **Evaluate Benefits**: Assess the benefits of each project (e.g., impact on quality of life, number of residents affected).
3. **Estimate Costs**: Estimate the cost of each project.
4. **Prioritize Projects**: Rank projects based on benefits and costs.
5. **Allocate Resources**: Allocate the $100,000 based on the prioritized list.

### Example

| Project        | Benefits   | Costs  | Priority |
|----------------|------------|--------|----------|
| Park           | High       | $30,000| 1        |
| Community Center| Medium     | $40,000| 2        |
| Road Improvements| High       | $20,000| 3        |

### Decision Making

- The government starts with the highest priority project (Park, $30,000).
- Next, it funds the Road Improvements ($20,000).
- The Community Center, though important, exceeds remaining budget ($50,000 - $30,000 - $20,000 = $0).

### Trade-Offs

- Choosing to fund the Park and Road Improvements means the Community Center must wait.
- This decision reflects the challenge of **Limited Resources**, where wants exceed available funds.
