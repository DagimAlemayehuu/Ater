## Mental Model

Imagine you are the manager of a school's music program, and you have to organize a concert with a limited number of instruments and practice rooms. You have to make choices about how to use these resources to get the best performance possible. This is similar to the economic concept of "Limited Resources", where the resources available to produce goods and services are not enough to meet everyone's wants. In economics, resources are the inputs used to produce goods and services, and they are limited, which means that people have to make choices about how to use them.

## How the Economics Actually Work

- WHAT: The concept of "Limited Resources" refers to the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, which arises because people's wants are unlimited, but the resources available to satisfy those wants are limited. 
- WHY: This concept exists because human wants are unlimited, but the resources available to produce goods and services are not enough to meet those wants. This scarcity of resources forces individuals, firms, and governments to make choices about how to allocate resources efficiently. 
- HOW: The mechanism of limited resources works as follows: 
  1. People's wants are unlimited, and they have to make choices about how to satisfy those wants.
  2. Resources, such as labor, capital, and raw materials, are limited.
  3. Because resources are limited, people have to prioritize their wants and allocate resources accordingly.
  4. This leads to the need for efficient allocation of resources, which is the core of economics.
  The concept of limited resources is closely related to [[Scarcity]], [[Unlimited_Wants]], and [[Economic_Analysis]]. It also leads to the need for [[Decision_Making_Units]] to make choices about resource allocation, which is a key aspect of [[Macroeconomics]].

## The Formal Math & Models

The formal definition of economics provided in the source text is: "Economics is a social science which studies about efficient allocation of scarce resources so as to attain the maximum fulfillment of unlimited human wants." This definition highlights the concept of limited resources and the need for efficient allocation to meet unlimited human wants. The source text also mentions that the science of economics is about two hundred years old and was formally introduced by Adam Smith in his book "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of Wealth of Nations" in 1776.
