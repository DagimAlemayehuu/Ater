## Mental Model

Imagine you're at a huge music festival with thousands of people, and you have a chance to buy merchandise like t-shirts, hats, and posters. You see so many cool things you want to buy, but you can't afford them all. This feeling of wanting more than you can have is similar to what economists mean by "unlimited wants." In economics, unlimited wants refer to the idea that people always want more things, like goods and services, than they can have, and these wants have no end.

## How the Economics Actually Work

- **WHAT**: Unlimited wants refer to the concept that human (society's) material wants are unlimited. This means that people always desire more goods and services than are available.
- **WHY**: This concept exists because, fundamentally, human desires and aspirations are boundless. As one want is satisfied, another arises. This is significant because it leads to the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, where resources are limited, but wants are not. This disparity necessitates choices about how to allocate resources efficiently.
- **HOW**: The mechanism works as follows:
  1. **Recognition of Wants**: Individuals recognize their desires for various goods and services.
  2. **Satisfaction of Wants**: Some wants are satisfied, but this satisfaction is temporary.
  3. **Emergence of New Wants**: Once a want is satisfied, new wants emerge.
  4. **Continuous Cycle**: This creates a continuous cycle where wants are never fully met, leading to an ongoing need for resource allocation decisions.
- **CONNECTIONS**: Unlimited wants are closely related to [[Scarcity]], as the unlimited nature of wants directly causes the problem of scarcity. It also connects to [[Economic_Systems]], as different systems attempt to address how to manage these unlimited wants with limited resources. Furthermore, it ties into [[Decision_Making_Units]], as individuals, firms, and governments must make choices due to the scarcity of resources and the unlimited nature of wants. Lastly, it relates to [[Basic_Economic_Questions]], which are fundamentally about how to allocate resources to meet unlimited wants.

## The Formal Math & Models

The formal definition provided in the source text is: "Economics is a social science which studies about efficient allocation of scarce resources so as to attain the maximum fulfillment of unlimited human wants." This definition directly ties the concept of unlimited wants to the core of what economics studies.
