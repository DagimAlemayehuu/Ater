## Mental Model

The basic economic questions are about how to allocate resources in a world where what we want is unlimited, but what we have is limited. Imagine you're the manager of a car factory that also produces computers, and you have to decide how many cars and computers to produce each month. The basic economic questions are essentially about making choices on how to use your limited resources - like workers, machines, and materials - to make the best possible combinations of cars and computers.

## How the Economics Actually Work

WHAT: The basic economic questions arise from the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, which leads to the need for choices about how to allocate limited resources. 
WHY: These questions exist because our wants are unlimited, but the resources available to satisfy those wants are limited. This mismatch between wants and resources necessitates making choices.
HOW: 
1. The process begins with recognizing that resources are scarce, which limits the output of goods and services.
2. Because of scarcity, not all wants can be satisfied, leading to the need for choice.
3. When a choice is made, it involves costs, specifically the opportunity cost, which is the value of the next best alternative that must be sacrificed.
4. Decisions about how to allocate resources involve trade-offs, meaning that having more of one thing means having less of another.
5. The production possibilities frontier (PPF) is a tool used to illustrate the various combinations of output that the economy can produce given its resources and technology.
6. The PPF shows the trade-offs between producing different goods and services, like cars and computers, and helps in understanding the opportunity costs involved in making production choices.
CONNECTIONS: This concept is closely related to [[Scarcity]], [[Limited_Resources]], and [[Unlimited_Wants]]. It also involves understanding [[Economic_Analysis]] and [[Decision_Making_Units]].

## The Formal Math & Models

The basic economic questions are fundamentally about the allocation of resources given the constraints of scarcity. The production possibilities frontier (PPF) or curve (PPC) is a graphical representation that shows the combinations of output that the economy can possibly produce given the available factors of production and the available production technology. It is defined by assumptions including fixed quantity and quality of economic resources, two broad classes of output, full employment and production, unchanged technology, and specialization of inputs. The PPF describes important concepts such as scarcity and opportunity cost.
