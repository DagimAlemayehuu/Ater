## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small village where resources are limited. The villagers must decide how to allocate their resources, such as land, labor, and capital, to produce the goods and services they need. They must answer three basic questions: what to produce, how to produce it, and for whom to produce it. These questions are fundamental to understanding how economies work and how resources are allocated.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] are a set of fundamental questions that every society must answer. These questions arise due to the problem of [[Scarcity]], which refers to the fact that the quantity and quality of economic resources available are limited. The three basic questions are: what to produce, how to produce it, and for whom to produce it. The first question, what to produce, refers to the types of goods and services that should be produced given the limited resources available. The second question, how to produce it, refers to the techniques and methods that should be used to produce the goods and services. The third question, for whom to produce it, refers to the distribution of goods and services among the members of society. These questions are related to the concept of [[Limited_Resources]] and [[Unlimited_Wants]], which highlights the fundamental economic problem of allocating resources to meet the unlimited wants and needs of society. The [[Definition_Of_Economics]] as a social science that studies how societies allocate their Limited Resources to meet their Unlimited Wants is closely related to these basic economic questions.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The theory assumes that the quantity and quality of economic resources available are fixed, which might not always be the case in reality. For instance, technological advancements can increase the quality of resources, and international trade can increase the quantity of resources available. Additionally, the theory does not account for the potential misallocation of resources due to imperfect information or irrational decision-making. The model also assumes that resources are scarce, which is a fundamental concept in economics, but does not consider the possibility of abundance or the impact of external factors such as environmental degradation. Furthermore, the theory is based on the idea that resources have alternative uses, but does not consider the potential for resources to be used in multiple ways simultaneously.
