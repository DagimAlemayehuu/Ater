## 1. Mental Model
Imagine a small town with a limited number of workers and machines. The town has two main industries: a car factory and a computer factory. The car factory uses all the available workers and machines to produce cars, leaving no resources for the computer factory. This scenario illustrates the fundamental economic problem of allocating limited resources among different industries. The town must decide how to allocate its resources to meet the demands of its citizens.

## 2. Foundational Concept
The fundamental economic problem leads to three important [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] related to the allocation of resources. The quantity as well as quality of economic resources available for use during the year is fixed. Together, the car industry and the computer industry use all of the economy's Factors of Production. This scenario illustrates the concept of [[Scarcity]], where the economy faces a shortage of resources. The economy must make choices about how to allocate its resources efficiently, taking into account the [[Limited_Resources]] and [[Unlimited_Wants]] of its citizens. The allocation of resources is a critical aspect of [[Macroeconomics]], which studies the economy as a whole.

### Key Takeaways:
- The fundamental economic problem leads to three basic economic questions.
- The economy's factors of production are used by various industries.
- Resources are limited in both quantity and quality.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases
This model assumes that the economy's factors of production are fixed and can be allocated among different industries. However, in reality, the availability of resources can change over time due to technological advancements or changes in government policies. Additionally, the model does not account for the possibility of resource misallocation, where resources are not used efficiently. For example, if the car industry uses all the available resources, it may lead to a shortage of resources in other industries, such as the computer industry. Furthermore, the model assumes that the quality of resources remains constant, but in reality, the quality of resources can deteriorate or improve over time.
