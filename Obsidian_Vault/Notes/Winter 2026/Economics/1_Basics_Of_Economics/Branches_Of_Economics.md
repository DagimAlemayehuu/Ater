## 1. Mental Model
Imagine a coffee shop where customers make individual purchasing decisions. This illustrates microeconomics, which studies how individual economic units make choices. On the other hand, consider a city's overall economy, including all its businesses and residents. This represents macroeconomics, which looks at the economy as a whole. Just as the coffee shop owner needs to understand both individual customer preferences and the overall market trends, economists use both microeconomics and macroeconomics to understand the economy.

## 2. Foundational Concept
The core of modern economics consists of its two primary branches: [[Microeconomics]] and Macroeconomics. The science of economics, in its current form, is approximately two hundred years old, with Adam Smith's book considered the foundation of economics as a distinct subject. [[Microeconomics]] focuses on individual economic units, such as households and firms, and their interactions in specific markets. In contrast, Macroeconomics examines the economy as a whole, studying aggregate variables like inflation, unemployment, and economic growth. Understanding both branches is essential for grasping the complexities of [[Economic_Systems]] and the ways in which they address fundamental [[Basic_Economic_Questions]].
