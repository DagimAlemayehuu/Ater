## 1. Mental Model
Imagine a small coffee shop. The owner must decide how to allocate resources like coffee beans, milk, and labor to meet the demands of customers. This simple scenario illustrates the fundamental problem of economics: how to allocate [[Limited_Resources]] to satisfy [[Unlimited_Wants]]. The owner must balance the supply of coffee and other resources with the demand from customers, all while making decisions about pricing, production, and staffing. This everyday situation demonstrates the core principles of economics, which studies how societies make choices about resource allocation.

## 2. Foundational Concept
The study of economics can be broadly divided into two main [[Branches_Of_Economics]]: [[Macroeconomics]] and Microeconomics, with Macroeconomics focusing on the economy as a whole and Microeconomics examining individual economic units. The science of economics in its current form is about two hundred years old, dating back to the work of [[Adam_Smith]]. Economics explores how societies allocate [[Limited_Resources]] to meet Unlimited Wants, and it encompasses various subfields, including Positive Economics and Normative Economics. Understanding these branches and their interrelations provides a solid foundation for analyzing economic phenomena and making informed decisions.

### Key Takeaways:
- Economics as a distinct subject began with Adam Smith's book.
- The science of economics is about two hundred years old.
- Economics studies how societies allocate Limited Resources to meet Unlimited Wants.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases
The study of economics relies heavily on the assumption that individuals act rationally. However, real-world edge cases such as [[Deductive_Reasoning]] and [[Inductive_Reasoning]] can lead to deviations from this assumption. For instance, during economic crises, people's behavior may become more unpredictable and less rational. Moreover, the field of economics often struggles to account for external factors like politics and social norms, which can significantly impact economic decisions. Another limitation arises from the challenge of modeling complex systems, where simplifications and assumptions may not fully capture the intricacies of real-world economies.

## 4. Case Study Analysis Table
There are several branches of economics, including microeconomics, macroeconomics, international economics, and development economics.

| Branch of Economics | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Microeconomics | Studies the behavior and decision-making of individual economic units, such as households and firms. |
| Macroeconomics | Examines the economy as a whole, focusing on issues like inflation, unemployment, and economic growth. |
| International Economics | Analyzes trade and investment between countries, including the impact of globalization. |
| Development Economics | Investigates the economic growth and development of low-income countries, exploring ways to reduce poverty and improve living standards. |
