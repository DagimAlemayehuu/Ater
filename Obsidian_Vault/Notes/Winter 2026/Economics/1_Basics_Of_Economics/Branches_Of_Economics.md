## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Imagine you're managing a big hospital. You have to make decisions about how to use your limited resources, like doctors, nurses, and equipment, to help as many patients as possible. Economics is like being the hospital manager, but instead of just one hospital, it's about how societies make choices about using their resources. The field of economics has grown and now includes many areas of study. Just like how a hospital has different departments like surgery, pediatrics, and pharmacy, economics has its own branches.

## 2. How the Economics Actually Work

- **WHAT**: The branches of economics are the different areas of study within the field of economics. 
- **WHY**: The branches of economics exist because the field of economics is vast and deals with a wide range of topics and issues. As societies grow and change, new areas of study emerge to understand and solve economic problems. 
- **HOW**: 
  1. The study of economics began with Adam Smith's book "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of Wealth of Nations" in 1776.
  2. Over time, as the nature and scope of economics grew, different economists defined economics from various perspectives, leading to different branches.
  3. The main branches include [[Macroeconomics]], which studies the economy as a whole, and Microeconomics, which studies individual economic units like households and firms.
  4. Other branches include [[Positive_Economics]], which describes "what is," and [[Normative_Economics]], which prescribes "what ought to be."
  5. The branches help in understanding how resources are efficiently allocated to meet unlimited [[Human_Wants]] given the problem of [[Scarcity]].
  6. They also help in analyzing how different Resource Allocation methods work and how economies can grow.
- **CONNECTIONS**: The branches of economics are connected to Basic Economic Questions, which every economy must answer: what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce. They also relate to Market Failure, which occurs when markets do not allocate resources efficiently.

## 3. The Formal Math & Models

The formal definition of economics provided is: "Economics is a social science which studies about efficient allocation of scarce resources so as to attain the maximum fulfillment of unlimited human wants." The field of economics includes various branches such as Macroeconomics and Microeconomics. Macroeconomics deals with the study of the economy as a whole, focusing on issues like inflation, unemployment, and economic growth. Microeconomics, on the other hand, focuses on individual economic units and their interactions. There are also Positive Economics and Normative Economics. Positive economics involves the study of facts and data to understand economic phenomena, while normative economics involves value judgments about what economic policies should be.

## 6. Common Misconceptions

- A common misconception is that economics is only about money and business. However, economics is about making choices about how to use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants.
- Some students think that economics is just about [[Scarcity]] and [[Human_Wants]], but it also involves understanding how economies grow and how to solve economic problems.
- Another misconception is that economics is not relevant to everyday life. However, the principles of economics apply to everyone, from individuals making decisions about how to spend their money to governments making policies about how to allocate resources.
