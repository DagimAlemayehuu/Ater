## 1. Mental Model

The car industry and the computer industry are two sectors that heavily rely on capital-intensive techniques. For instance, car manufacturers like Tesla invest heavily in automated machinery and robotics to streamline their production lines. This allows them to produce electric vehicles efficiently and at scale. Similarly, computer manufacturers like Apple invest in sophisticated equipment to design and assemble their products.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Capital-intensive techniques refer to methods of production that rely heavily on [[Limited_Resources]], particularly capital goods, such as machinery and equipment. These techniques are often used in industries where the use of advanced technology and automation can significantly increase efficiency and productivity. The car industry and the computer industry are examples of sectors that employ capital-intensive techniques, as they require significant investments in machinery and equipment to produce their goods. By using these techniques, firms can reduce their reliance on labor and increase their output, which can help address the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] of what, how, and for whom to produce.

### Key Takeaways:

- The car industry and the computer industry use all of the economy’s factors of production.
- Capital-intensive techniques often involve a trade-off between using more capital and less labor, which is in contrast to [[Labour_Intensive_Techniques]].
- Understanding capital-intensive techniques is crucial for analyzing [[Economic_Growth]] and How To Produce.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

However, the use of capital-intensive techniques has its limitations. For instance, these techniques may not be suitable for small-scale production or for industries with rapidly changing product lines. Additionally, the high upfront costs associated with capital-intensive techniques can be a barrier to entry for new firms, potentially leading to [[Market_Failure]]. Furthermore, the emphasis on capital-intensive techniques can lead to concerns about job displacement and the impact on employment, highlighting the need to consider the social and philosophical implications of these techniques in the context of [[Definition_Of_Economics]].
