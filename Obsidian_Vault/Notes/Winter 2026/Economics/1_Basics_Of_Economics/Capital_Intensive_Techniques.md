## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Capital intensive techniques refer to methods of production that rely heavily on machinery, equipment, and technology, rather than on human labor. For example, imagine a car manufacturing plant that uses robots and automated assembly lines to produce vehicles. This approach requires significant investments in capital, such as machines and equipment, to produce goods and services.

## 2. How the Economics Actually Work

- WHAT: Capital intensive techniques are a type of production method that emphasizes the use of capital, such as machinery and equipment, over labor. 
- WHY: These techniques exist because of the fundamental economic problem of [[Scarcity]], which necessitates choices about how to allocate limited resources. By choosing to use more capital, firms can increase efficiency and productivity. 
- HOW:

  2. They invest in machinery and equipment to automate production processes.
  3. This approach allows for increased production efficiency and potentially higher output.

  5. Firms must consider the Opportunity Cost of choosing capital intensive techniques over labor intensive ones, as this decision involves tradeoffs.

## 5. Where It Breaks (Edge Cases & Flaws)

* **Resource Constraint**: Firms may not have sufficient funds to invest in machinery and equipment for capital intensive techniques.
* **Technological Limitations**: The current state of technology may not support the adoption of capital intensive techniques.
* **Labor Skills**: Firms may not have access to skilled labor to effectively utilize capital intensive techniques.
