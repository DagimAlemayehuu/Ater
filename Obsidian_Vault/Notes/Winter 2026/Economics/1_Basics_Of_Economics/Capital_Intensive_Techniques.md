## Mental Model

Capital intensive techniques refer to methods of production that rely heavily on machinery, equipment, and technology, rather than on human labor. For example, imagine a car manufacturing plant that uses robots and automated assembly lines to produce vehicles. This approach requires significant investments in capital, such as machines and equipment, to produce goods and services.

## How the Economics Actually Work

Capital intensive techniques are a type of production method that contrasts with labor-intensive techniques. 
- WHAT: Capital intensive techniques are characterized by a high ratio of capital to labor, meaning that a large amount of money is spent on machinery, equipment, and technology to produce goods and services.
- WHY: These techniques exist because of the fundamental economic problem of scarcity, which implies that choices must be made about how to allocate limited resources. 
- HOW: The mechanism of capital intensive techniques works as follows: 
  1. A firm decides to adopt a capital intensive technique to produce a good or service.
  2. The firm invests a significant amount of money in machinery, equipment, and technology.
  3. The machinery and equipment are used to produce the good or service, reducing the need for human labor.
  4. The firm benefits from increased efficiency and productivity, but also faces higher costs due to the investment in capital.
  Capital intensive techniques are closely related to [[Scarcity]], [[Limited_Resources]], and [[Decision_Making_Units]]. 
  They also involve Opportunity Cost, as the decision to invest in capital intensive techniques means giving up the opportunity to use those resources for other purposes.
