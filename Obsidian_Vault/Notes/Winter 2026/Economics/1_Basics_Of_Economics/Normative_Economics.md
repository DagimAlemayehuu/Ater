## Mental Model

Normative economics is about making value judgments or opinions on economic issues. It's like when you're discussing whether a government should prioritize spending on healthcare or education - there's no right or wrong answer, it's about what you think is fair or best. This type of economics can't be proven true or false, it's just a matter of personal opinion.

## How the Economics Actually Work

Normative economics is a branch of economics that deals with opinions and value judgments. 
- WHAT: It involves making statements about what ought to be, which can't be tested or proven true or false.
- WHY: This concept exists because economic issues often involve making choices that affect people's lives, and these choices are influenced by personal values and opinions. 
- HOW: In normative economics, economists and policymakers express their opinions on what economic policies or outcomes are desirable or undesirable. For example, a person might argue that a country should have a progressive tax system because it promotes fairness and equality. 
- CONNECTIONS: This concept is closely related to [[Positive_Economics]], which deals with factual statements about economic issues. Normative economics also interacts with [[Economic_Systems]], as different systems can be evaluated based on their alignment with certain values or goals. Additionally, [[Decision_Making_Units]], such as governments and individuals, play a crucial role in making normative economic decisions.
