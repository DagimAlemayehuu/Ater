## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Normative economics is about making value judgments or opinions on economic issues. It's like when you're discussing whether a government should prioritize spending on healthcare or education - there's no right or wrong answer, it's just about what people think is right. This type of economics can't be proven true or false with facts; it's more about personal opinions and values.

- WHAT: Normative economics is a matter of opinion/value judgments. 
- WHY: It exists because economics deals with human wants and scarce resources, leading to subjective decisions on how resources should be allocated to maximize welfare. 
- HOW: 
  1. It involves evaluating economic outcomes based on desired goals or values.
  2. Economists and policymakers use it to argue for certain policies over others, based on what they think is right or wrong, rather than just on facts.
  3. This approach is essential for making decisions that affect society as a whole, like [[Resource_Allocation]] and addressing [[Market_Failure]].
- CONNECTIONS: 
  - [[Positive_Economics]], which focuses on factual, testable statements about economic phenomena.
  - [[Branches_Of_Economics]], as normative economics is a key aspect that distinguishes economics from other social sciences.
  - [[Definition_Of_Economics]], which underpins why normative economics is necessary due to the scarcity of resources and unlimited human Human Wants.

Normative economics is described in the source text as "a matter of opinion/value judgments. It can be true or false, but cannot be tested." This definition highlights its subjective nature and distinguishes it from positive economics, which deals with objective, testable facts.

## 5. Where It Breaks (Edge Cases & Flaws)

* **Subjectivity**: Normative economics is limited by its subjective nature, making it difficult to reach consensus on policy decisions.
* **Lack of Empirical Evidence**: It cannot be tested or proven empirically, which can lead to disagreements among economists and policymakers.
* **Value-Based Judgments**: It relies on value judgments, which can vary across individuals and cultures, making it challenging to develop universally accepted policies.
