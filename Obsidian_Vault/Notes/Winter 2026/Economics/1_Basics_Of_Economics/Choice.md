## Mental Model

Imagine you're the manager of a car factory that also produces computers. You have limited resources, like workers and machines. "Choice" refers to the decisions you make about how to use these resources to produce cars and computers. For example, if you decide to produce more cars, you might have to produce fewer computers, because you can't use the same resources to do both at the same time.

## How the Economics Actually Work

WHAT: The concept of "Choice" in economics refers to the decisions made about how to allocate limited resources to satisfy wants. According to the source text, "scarcity implies choice. Choice, in turn, implies cost." 
WHY: Choice exists because of scarcity, which means that the resources available are limited, and not all wants can be satisfied. This leads to the need to make decisions about how to use resources. The significance of choice lies in its role in allocating resources efficiently.
HOW: The mechanism of choice works as follows:
1. Scarcity leads to limited output, meaning that not all wants can be satisfied.
2. This leads to the need to make choices about how to allocate resources.
3. When a choice is made, it involves giving up an opportunity to do something else, which is known as the opportunity cost.
4. The opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that must be sacrificed.
[[Scarcity]], [[Limited_Resources]], and [[Unlimited_Wants]] are related concepts that underlie the concept of choice.

## The Formal Math & Models

The formal definition of choice is closely tied to the concept of scarcity and opportunity cost. The source text states that "scarcity → limited resource → limited output → we might not satisfy all our wants → make choice → choice involves costs." This highlights the relationship between scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost.
