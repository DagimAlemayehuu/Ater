## 1. The Plain English Explanation

Imagine you're the manager of a small town's only factory, which can produce either cars or computers. The town's resources, like workers and machines, are limited, so you have to decide how many cars and computers to produce. "What To Produce" is the economic question that deals with making choices about how to use these limited resources to create the right mix of goods and services, like cars and computers, to satisfy people's needs and wants.

## 2. How the Economics Actually Work

- WHAT: The concept of "What To Produce" refers to the economic decisions made about the allocation of resources to produce different goods and services. According to the source text, economies cannot produce everything, so they must decide what to produce and in what quantities.
- WHY: This concept exists because of [[Scarcity]], which implies that the needs and wants of individuals are unlimited, but the resources available to satisfy those needs and wants are limited. This leads to the need for choices about how to use resources efficiently. The significance of "What To Produce" lies in its role in addressing the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] that every economy must answer.
- HOW: The mechanism works as follows:
  1. An economy has limited resources and technology.
  2. There are various goods and services that can be produced, but not all of them can be produced at the same time due to scarcity.
  3. The economy decides to produce a certain mix of goods and services, like cars and computers, based on the available resources and technology.
  4. This decision involves trade-offs, as producing more of one good means producing less of another.
  5. The [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]] (PPF) is a tool used to illustrate the possible combinations of output that the economy can produce given its resources and technology.
  6. The PPF shows the opportunity costs of producing more of one good versus another, which is related to the concept of Opportunity Cost.
- CONNECTIONS: This concept is closely related to [[Resource_Allocation]], as it deals with how resources are allocated to produce different goods and services. It also connects to Human Wants, as the economy aims to satisfy people's needs and wants through its production decisions.

## 3. The Formal Math & Models

The formal definition of the concept "What To Produce" is tied to the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]] (PPF) or curve (PPC), which shows the combinations of output that the economy can possibly produce given the available factors of production and the available production technology. The PPF is drawn under certain assumptions:
1. The quantity and quality of economic resources available for use during the year are fixed.
2. There are two broad classes of output to be produced over the year.
3. The economy is operating at full employment and is achieving full production (efficiency).
4. Technology does not change during the year.
5. Some inputs are better adapted to the production of one good than to the production of the other (specialization).

## 5. Where It Breaks (Edge Cases & Flaws)

* **Insufficient Resources**: If an economy lacks sufficient resources, it may not be able to produce the desired goods and services, affecting the decision of what to produce.
* **Unlimited Wants**: When the needs and wants of individuals are unlimited, it becomes challenging for an economy to decide what to produce, as it cannot satisfy all wants.
* **Inefficient Allocation**: If resources are not allocated efficiently, the economy may produce the wrong mix of goods and services, leading to a suboptimal answer to the question of what to produce.

## 6. Common Misconceptions

- A common misconception is that an economy can produce anything it wants without any limitations. However, the source text clearly states that economies cannot produce everything due to [[Scarcity]].
- Another misconception is that the decision of what to produce is not related to opportunity costs. However, the source text explains that decisions involve trade-offs and that the value of the next best alternative that must be sacrificed is the Opportunity Cost.
- Some might assume that technology and resources can change easily, which is not assumed when drawing the PPF, as it assumes that technology does not change during the year and that resources are fixed.
