## Mental Model

Imagine you're the manager of a small town's only factory, which can produce either cars or computers. The town's resources, like workers and machines, are limited. "What To Produce" is the question of how to decide whether to make more cars or more computers with those limited resources. This decision is crucial because it affects what goods are available for the town's residents to buy and use.

- WHAT: The concept of "What To Produce" refers to the decisions economies make about how to allocate their limited resources to produce goods and services. 
- WHY: This concept exists because economies face scarcity, meaning they have limited resources and cannot produce everything they want. As a result, they must make choices about what to produce and in what quantities. 
- HOW: The mechanism works as follows:
  1. An economy has limited resources, such as labor, machines, and raw materials.
  2. There are various goods and services that can be produced with these resources.
  3. The economy must choose which goods and services to produce, given that it cannot produce everything.
  4. This choice involves trade-offs, as producing more of one good means producing less of another.
  5. The economy uses a Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF) or Curve to visualize these choices and understand the trade-offs.
  The concept of "What To Produce" is closely related to [[Scarcity]], [[Limited_Resources]], and [[Basic_Economic_Questions]]. It also involves understanding Opportunity Cost, which is the value of the next best alternative that must be sacrificed when making a choice.

The formal definition of the Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF) or Curve is: "a graph that shows the combinations of output that the economy can possibly produce given the available factors of production and the available production technology." The PPF is drawn under several assumptions:
1. The quantity and quality of economic resources available for use during the year are fixed.
2. There are two broad classes of output to be produced over the year.
3. The economy is operating at full employment and achieving full production (efficiency).
4. Technology does not change during the year.
5. Some inputs are better adapted to the production of one good than to the production of the other (specialization).

- A common misconception is that an economy can produce anything it wants without limitations. However, the source text clearly states that economies face scarcity and limited resources, which restrict their production capabilities.
- Another misconception is that the decision of what to produce is arbitrary. In reality, this decision is based on the economy's resources, technology, and the trade-offs involved in choosing one product over another.
- Some may assume that producing more of one good does not affect the production of other goods. However, the concept of opportunity cost highlights that producing more of one good means producing less of another.
