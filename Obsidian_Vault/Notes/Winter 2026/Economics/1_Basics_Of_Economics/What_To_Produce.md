## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small island economy that can produce either fish or coconuts. The islanders have a limited amount of land, labor, and technology, which they can use to produce either fish or coconuts. The production possibilities frontier shows the various combinations of fish and coconuts that the islanders can produce given their available resources and technology. For example, they may be able to produce 100 fish and 50 coconuts, or 50 fish and 100 coconuts, but they cannot produce 200 fish and 200 coconuts because they do not have enough resources.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The concept of what to produce is a fundamental question in economics, as economies cannot produce everything and must therefore decide what goods and services to produce and in what quantities. The production possibilities frontier Production Possibilities Frontier is a graph that shows the various combinations of output that an economy can produce given its available factors of production Factors Of Production and the available production technology Production Technology. There are two broad classes of output to be produced over the year: Goods and Services. The production possibilities frontier is a useful tool for illustrating the trade-offs that economies face when deciding what to produce. For example, if an economy decides to produce more cars, it may have to produce fewer computers, and vice versa. This is because the resources used to produce cars, such as labor and raw materials, cannot be used to produce computers at the same time. The concept of scarcity [[Scarcity]] is closely related to the production possibilities frontier, as it highlights the limited nature of resources and the need for economies to make choices about what to produce.
