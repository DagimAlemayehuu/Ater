## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a company distributing pizza to its employees. **Efficiency** is making sure no pizza is wasted and everyone is working hard to earn it. **Equity** is making sure the distribution is fair (e.g., the hungriest or hardest working get enough). **Stability** is making sure the pizza delivery arrives every day without fail and the price doesn't suddenly triple. Economists use these three lenses to judge if an economic system is "working well."

## 2. Causal Mechanism

When economists study how choices are made, they evaluate the outcomes using three primary criteria:

1.  **Efficiency**: Achieving the maximum possible output from available resources.
    - **Productive Efficiency**: Producing goods at the lowest possible cost.
    - **Allocative Efficiency**: Producing the combination of goods most desired by society.
2.  **Equity**: The fairness of the distribution of resources or wealth.
    - Unlike efficiency (which is often a [[Positive_Economics]] fact), equity is deeply rooted in [[Normative_Economics]] and value judgments.
    - It asks: Is the outcome "just"?
3.  **Stability**: The consistency and predictability of the economy over time.
    - It involves maintaining steady economic growth, full employment, and stable prices (low inflation).
    - An unstable economy creates uncertainty, which hinders long-term investment.

### Key Takeaways:
- **Efficiency** is about "the size of the pie."
- **Equity** is about "how the pie is sliced."
- **Stability** is about "making sure there is a pie tomorrow."

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

- **Efficiency-Equity Trade-off**: High efficiency (e.g., extreme competition) can sometimes lead to high inequality (low equity). Conversely, aggressive redistribution (high equity) can reduce incentives for hard work (low efficiency).
- **Measurement Difficulties**: While efficiency can be measured in units of output/cost, "equity" is subjective and varies by political and cultural context.

## 4. Evaluation Matrix

| Criterion | Question it Asks | Economic Branch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Efficiency** | Are resources wasted? | Microeconomics |
| **Equity** | Is the distribution fair? | Welfare/Normative Economics |
| **Stability** | Are growth and prices steady? | Macroeconomics |

## 5. Walkthrough

**Step 1:** Observe an economic outcome (e.g., a new tax policy).
**Step 2:** Analyze if the tax leads to better resource use (Efficiency).
**Step 3:** Analyze if the tax burden falls fairly on different groups (Equity).
**Step 4:** Analyze if the tax causes market shocks or helps smooth the business cycle (Stability).
