## Overview
The Basics of Economics Hub revolves around the fundamental concepts of demand and supply, which interlock to form the core of market dynamics. The demand side is anchored on the inverse relationship between the price of a product and the quantity demanded, as described by the law of demand. This relationship is influenced by factors such as changes in income, prices of related goods, and consumer preferences, which are reflected in the individual demand schedule and market demand curve. The degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to changes in price, income, or related goods' prices is measured by elasticity, providing insights into consumer behavior.

On the supply side, the law of supply posits that as the price of a good increases, the quantity supplied also increases. The supply curve can shift due to changes in production costs, technology, or other factors, affecting the market equilibrium. The elasticity of supply measures the responsiveness of supply to price changes, influenced by factors such as production complexity, factor mobility, and time to respond. The market equilibrium occurs when market demand equals market supply, and changes in demand and supply lead to adjustments in the equilibrium price and quantity.

The intersection of demand and supply curves determines the market equilibrium price and quantity, with changes in either side causing shifts in the equilibrium. Understanding these concepts, including elasticity, enables analysis of how markets respond to various shocks and policy interventions. The mathematical representation of demand and supply, such as the example provided, allows for quantitative analysis and predictions of market behavior, forming the foundation for more advanced economic analysis and decision-making.
