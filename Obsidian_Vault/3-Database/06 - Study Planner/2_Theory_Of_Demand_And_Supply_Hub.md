# 2 Theory of Demand and Supply Hub

## Overview
The theory of demand and supply provides a framework for understanding the dynamics of markets. The law of demand states that as the price of a commodity increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa, assuming all other factors remain constant, or ceteris paribus. The demand schedule, demand curve, and demand function all describe the relationship between the price of a commodity and the quantity demanded. Market demand is the aggregate demand of all buyers in the market, represented by a market demand curve. The price elasticity of demand and income elasticity of demand measure the responsiveness of quantity demanded to changes in price and income, respectively.

The theory of supply complements the theory of demand, describing the relationship between the price of a commodity and the quantity supplied. The supply curve represents this relationship, and a change in supply occurs when there is a shift in the supply curve due to changes in determinants such as production costs, technology, and government policies. The price elasticity of supply measures the responsiveness of quantity supplied to changes in price. The market equilibrium occurs when market demand equals market supply, resulting in an equilibrium price and quantity. Changes in demand and supply can disrupt this equilibrium, leading to changes in the equilibrium price and quantity.

The concepts of demand and supply are interconnected, and changes in one can affect the other. For instance, an increase in demand can lead to a shortage, causing prices to rise, which in turn can lead to an increase in supply. Understanding the determinants of demand and supply, as well as the elasticities of demand and supply, is crucial for analyzing market behavior and making informed decisions. The use of mathematical representations, such as demand and supply functions, and graphical representations, such as demand and supply curves, facilitates the analysis of market dynamics and the identification of market equilibrium. By considering the interactions between demand and supply, policymakers and businesses can better navigate the complexities of markets and make more informed decisions.
