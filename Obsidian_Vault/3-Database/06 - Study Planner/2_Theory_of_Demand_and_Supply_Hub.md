## Overview
The theory of demand and supply provides a framework for understanding the dynamics of markets. The law of demand states that as the price of a commodity increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa. This relationship is represented graphically by a demand curve, which shows the various quantities of a commodity that consumers are willing to buy at different price levels. The demand curve is influenced by several factors, including the price of the good, income, prices of related goods, consumer expectations, and the number of buyers. 

The theory of supply, on the other hand, describes the relationship between the price of a commodity and the quantity that producers are willing to supply. The supply curve shows the various quantities of a commodity that producers are willing to sell at different price levels. The position of the supply curve is influenced by factors such as production costs, technology, and the prices of inputs. The concept of elasticity is also crucial in understanding the responsiveness of demand and supply to changes in price and other factors. 

The intersection of the demand and supply curves determines the market equilibrium, where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. Changes in demand and supply can disrupt this equilibrium, leading to changes in the equilibrium price and quantity. Understanding the theory of demand and supply is essential for analyzing the behavior of markets and making informed decisions about production, pricing, and investment. By examining the various factors that influence demand and supply, businesses and policymakers can better anticipate and respond to changes in the market, ultimately leading to more efficient allocation of resources.
