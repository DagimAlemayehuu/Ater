## 1. Mental Model

Imagine you're at a lemonade stand, and the owner, let's call her Emma, is selling lemonade for 50 cents a cup. If the price of lemonade suddenly goes up to 75 cents, Emma gets super excited and quickly makes more lemonade to sell, like she's a superhero with a magic lemonade-making machine! The "Price Elasticity of Supply" formula helps us figure out how much more lemonade Emma will make when the price changes. It's calculated as: (Change in Quantity Supplied / Original Quantity Supplied) ÷ (Change in Price / Original Price). For example, if Emma originally sold 10 cups and now sells 15 cups when the price increases, and the price went from 50 cents to 75 cents, the formula would help us calculate how "elastic" her lemonade supply is - in other words, how easily she can change the amount of lemonade she makes when the price changes.

## 2. Micro Theory

The **Price Elasticity of Supply Formula** is a measure of the responsiveness of the quantity supplied of a good to a change in its price, while holding all other influencing factors constant, as per the [[Ceteris_Paribus]] assumption. It is calculated as the percentage change in quantity supplied in response to a percentage change in price. The formula for price elasticity of supply can be expressed as: 

Es = (dQs / dP) * (P / Qs)

where Es is the price elasticity of supply, dQs is the change in quantity supplied, dP is the change in price, P is the original price, and Qs is the original quantity supplied.

There are two primary methods used to calculate the price elasticity of supply: the **point method** and the **arc method**. The point method involves calculating the elasticity at a specific point on the supply curve, whereas the arc method measures elasticity over a range of prices and quantities.

In the **point method**, the price elasticity of supply is calculated using Es = (dQs / dP) * (P / Qs)

This method requires the use of calculus to determine the slope of the supply curve (dQs / dP) at a specific point.

On the other hand, the **arc method**, also known as the midpoint method, calculates the elasticity over a range of prices and quantities. The formula for the arc method is:

Es = [(Q2 - Q1) / (Q2 + Q1) / 2] / [(P2 - P1) / (P2 + P1) / 2]

where Q1 and Q2 are the initial and final quantities supplied, and P1 and P2 are the initial and final prices.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The price elasticity of supply formula has specific limitations and edge cases, particularly when calculated using the point and arc methods. A key limitation is that the point elasticity method assumes a linear supply curve and provides elasticity at a specific point, which may not accurately represent the entire supply curve. Additionally, when using the arc method, which calculates elasticity over a range of prices and quantities, the result may not be precise if the change in price or quantity is too large or too small. Furthermore, the formula assumes that other factors affecting supply, such as technology and input prices, remain constant, which may not hold true in reality, leading to inaccurate estimates of elasticity. Moreover, the formula may not be applicable in cases where supply is highly non-linear or when there are external shocks to the market, resulting in unreliable estimates of price elasticity of supply.

### Price Elasticity of Supply Formula

The price elasticity of supply formula can be expressed in LaTeX as:

$$
E_s = \frac{dQ_s}{dP} \cdot \frac{P}{Q_s}
$$

This formula measures the responsiveness of the quantity supplied of a good to a change in its price. The price elasticity of supply is calculated as the percentage change in quantity supplied in response to a percentage change in price, holding all other influencing factors constant.

## 5. Walkthrough

**Step 1: Define the Variables and Formula**

Identify the variables:
- Es = price elasticity of supply
- dQs = change in quantity supplied
- dP = change in price
- P = original price
- Qs = original quantity supplied

The price elasticity of supply formula is: 
Es = (dQs / dP) * (P / Qs)

**Step 2: Calculate Change in Quantity Supplied (dQs) and Change in Price (dP)**

Determine the change in quantity supplied (dQs) and the change in price (dP) from the given data or supply curve. 
- dQs = Qs2 - Qs1 (change in quantity supplied)
- dP = P2 - P1 (change in price)

**Step 3: Apply the Point Method or Arc Method**

**Point Method:**
If using the point method, find the derivative of the supply function (dQs/dP) at a specific point (P, Qs). 
- The supply function can be in the form Qs = f(P)
- Find dQs/dP at the point of interest.

**Arc Method:**
If using the arc method, calculate the elasticity over a range of prices and quantities.
- Use the midpoint formula for a more accurate calculation: 
  Es = ((Qs2 - Qs1) / ((Qs2 + Qs1) / 2)) / ((P2 - P1) / ((P2 + P1) / 2))

**Step 4: Substitute Values into the Formula**

Substitute the calculated values into the price elasticity of supply formula: 
- Using the point method: Es = (dQs / dP) * (P / Qs)
- Using the arc method: Es = ((Qs2 - Qs1) / ((Qs2 + Qs1) / 2)) / ((P2 - P1) / ((P2 + P1) / 2))

**Step 5: Interpret the Elasticity Value**

Interpret the calculated Es value:
- If Es > 1, the supply is elastic (responsive to price changes).
- If Es < 1, the supply is inelastic (not very responsive to price changes).
- If Es = 1, the supply is unit elastic.
