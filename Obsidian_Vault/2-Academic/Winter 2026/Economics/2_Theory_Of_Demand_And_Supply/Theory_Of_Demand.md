## 1. Mental Model

Imagine you're at a school bake sale, and they have delicious cookies for sale. If the price of the cookies is really high, you might decide to buy only one or not buy any at all. But if the price is lower, you might buy two or three cookies. This shows that when the price is lower, people tend to buy more, and when the price is higher, people tend to buy less.

## 2. Economic Theory

The [[Theory_Of_Demand]] states that there is an inverse relationship between the price of a good and the quantity demanded, assuming [[Ceteris_Paribus]] (all other factors are constant). This relationship is often represented by a [[Demand_Curve]], which shows how the quantity demanded changes in response to changes in price. The [[Law_Of_Demand]] summarizes this concept, stating that as prices rise, quantity demanded falls, and vice versa.

## 4. Walkthrough

* The theory of demand states that as the price of a good increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa.
* For example, if the price of cookies at the bake sale is $2, you might buy 2 cookies. If the price increases to $3, you might buy only 1 cookie.
* Conversely, if the price decreases to $1, you might buy 3 cookies.
* This relationship is represented by the demand curve, which slopes downward from left to right.

## 5. Market Failures

The theory of demand assumes ceteris paribus, or that all other factors are constant. However, in reality, other factors such as changes in consumer income, tastes, or prices of related goods can affect demand. Additionally, the theory of demand may not hold in cases where goods are perfectly inelastic, such as life-saving medications, or where consumers have irrational preferences.
