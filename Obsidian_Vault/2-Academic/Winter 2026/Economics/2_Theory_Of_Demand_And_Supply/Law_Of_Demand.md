# 1. Mental Model

Imagine you're at your favorite ice cream shop, and they raise the price of your favorite flavor from $3 to $5 a scoop. If you loved it at $3, but now it's too expensive at $5, you might decide to buy less or look for a cheaper alternative. This everyday situation shows that when prices go up, people tend to buy less.

# 2. Economic Theory

The [[Law_Of_Demand]] states that, [[Ceteris_Paribus]] (all else being equal), as the price of a product increases, the quantity demanded of it decreases, and vice versa. This is often illustrated by a [[Demand_Curve]] that slopes downward from left to right, showing the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded. The [[Theory_Of_Demand]] provides a framework for understanding this relationship, which is also represented by a [[Demand_Schedule]] or [[Demand_Function]].

## 4. Walkthrough

* The model starts with a change in Price (P) of a product.
* When Price increases, the Quantity Demanded (Q) decreases, and vice versa.
* This change in Quantity Demanded affects the overall Demand (D) for the product.
* For example, if the price of ice cream increases from $3 to $5, the quantity demanded might decrease from 2 scoops to 1 scoop.

## 5. Market Failures

The Law of Demand assumes that all else is equal (Ceteris Paribus), but in reality, other factors like income, tastes, and substitutes can influence demand. A common pitfall is ignoring these external factors, which can lead to inaccurate predictions of demand. Additionally, the Law of Demand may not hold for essential goods or services with no close substitutes.
