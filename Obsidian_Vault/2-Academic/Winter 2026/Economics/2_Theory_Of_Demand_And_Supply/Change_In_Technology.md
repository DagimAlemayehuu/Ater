# 1. Mental Model

Imagine you have a lemonade stand and you figure out a way to make lemonade faster and cheaper. This means you can now make more lemonade than before with the same amount of effort. As a result, you're willing to sell lemonade at a lower price or supply more lemonade at the same price.

# 2. Economic Theory

A [[Change_In_Technology]] refers to an improvement in the production process that allows firms to produce more output with the same amount of inputs. This leads to an increase in supply, causing the [[Supply_Schedule]] to shift outward or downward, and subsequently shifting the [[Supply_Curve]] to the right. As a result, the [[Market_Equilibrium]] changes, often leading to a decrease in price and an increase in quantity supplied, assuming [[Ceteris_Paribus]].

## 4. Walkthrough

* The initial change in technology improves the production process, allowing for increased efficiency.
* This increased efficiency leads to an increase in supply, as more output can be produced with the same amount of inputs.
* The increased supply causes the supply curve to shift to the right, indicating that more is supplied at each price level.
* As a result, the equilibrium price decreases or the quantity supplied increases, benefiting consumers.

## 5. Market Failures

This concept may fail when the change in technology leads to significant externalities, such as environmental degradation, that are not accounted for in the market. Additionally, if the technology change primarily benefits a single firm or group, it may lead to market concentration and decreased competition. Edge cases include situations where the technology change is not easily adoptable by all firms, leading to unequal market distribution.
