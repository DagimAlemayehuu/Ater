## 2. Micro Theory

The Point and Arc Method is a technical approach used in microeconomics to measure the price elasticity of supply. This method is based on the formula for the price elasticity of supply (ESP), which is defined as the percentage change in quantity supplied (∆Q/Q) divided by the percentage change in price (∆P/P). 

The point elasticity method calculates the elasticity at a specific point on the supply curve, using ESP = (dQ/Q) / (dP/P) 

where dQ and dP represent infinitesimally small changes in quantity and price, respectively. 

On the other hand, the arc elasticity method, also known as the midpoint method, estimates the elasticity over a range of prices and quantities, rather than at a single point. The arc elasticity formula is: 

ESP = (∆Q/Q) / (∆P/P) 

where ∆Q and ∆P represent finite changes in quantity and price between two points on the supply curve. 

To compute the arc elasticity, the midpoint formula is often used: 

ESP = [(Q2 - Q1) / ((Q2 + Q1) / 2)] / [(P2 - P1) / ((P2 + P1) / 2)]

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The Point and Arc method of estimating elasticity, given by the formula ESP = ∆Q / Q ÷ ∆P / P, has specific limitations and edge cases. A major limitation is that it assumes a linear demand curve and provides a single elasticity estimate over a specific range or arc of the demand curve, which may not accurately represent elasticity at different points. Additionally, the method can yield varying elasticity estimates depending on the direction of the price change (i.e., from point A to B or B to A), and it does not account for non-linear demand curves. Furthermore, the midpoint formula, often used in conjunction with the arc elasticity method, assumes that the elasticity is constant over the arc, which may not hold true for large changes in price or quantity. These limitations can lead to inaccurate estimates of elasticity, particularly in cases where demand curves are non-linear or where there are significant changes in price or quantity.
