# 1. Mental Model

Imagine you're the manager of a school cafeteria, and you have a limited amount of food to serve to all the students. You have to decide how to distribute the food so that everyone gets a fair share. This is similar to the economic problem of "For Whom To Produce", where a society has to decide how to distribute its limited resources and goods among its people.

# 2. Economic Theory

The concept of "For Whom To Produce" refers to the problem of distributing a society's goods and services among its population. It is one of the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] and is also known as the problem of distribution of national product. This concept is closely related to [[Scarcity]] and [[Opportunity_Cost]], as the distribution of resources and goods involves making choices about how to allocate them efficiently.

## 4. Walkthrough

* The society's resources (A) are used to produce goods and services (B).
* The produced goods and services are then distributed (C) among the population.
* The distribution of goods and services raises the question of "For Whom to Produce" (D), i.e., who should receive the goods and services.
* The goal is to achieve an equitable allocation (E) of resources, which ultimately leads to maximizing utility (F) for the society.

## 5. Market Failures

The concept of "For Whom to Produce" can fail when there are unequal distributions of income and wealth, leading to market failures such as poverty and inequality. Additionally, imperfect information and externalities can also lead to inefficient allocation of resources. Edge cases to watch out for include situations where certain groups are excluded from accessing essential goods and services.
