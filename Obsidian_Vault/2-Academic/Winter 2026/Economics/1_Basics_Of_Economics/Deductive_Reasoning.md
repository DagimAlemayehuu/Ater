# 1. Mental Model

Imagine you're at a lemonade stand, and you know that if it's a sunny day and the stand has a lot of lemons, they can make a lot of lemonade. If it's a sunny day and they have a lot of lemons, you can logically conclude that they will make a lot of lemonade. This is similar to deductive reasoning, where you use facts to make a logical conclusion.

# 2. Economic Theory

[[Deductive_Reasoning]] is a method of reasoning where conclusions are drawn from certain fundamental assumptions or truths through a process of logical arguments. In economics, deductive reasoning involves using axioms or assumptions to derive conclusions about economic phenomena. This approach allows economists to build theories and models that explain and predict economic behavior, often in conjunction with [[Positive_Economics]] and [[Macroeconomics]].

## 4. Walkthrough

* The model starts with a condition: it's a **Sunday** (A), which is assumed to be a day with high foot traffic.
* The availability of **Lemons** (B) is a necessary condition for making lemonade.
* If both conditions are met, there's **High Demand** (C) for lemonade, and the stand can **Make Lemonade** (D).
* As a result, the stand can **Sell More Lemonade** (E) due to the high demand.

## 5. Market Failures

This deductive reasoning model can fail if there are external factors not considered, such as a rival lemonade stand opening nearby or a sudden rainstorm. Additionally, the model assumes that the lemonade stand has the capacity to make and sell more lemonade, which might not always be the case. Edge cases, like a shortage of cups or sugar, can also disrupt the logical conclusion.
