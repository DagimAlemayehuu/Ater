## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small bakery that produces handmade bread. The bakery uses labor-intensive techniques, with bakers manually mixing, kneading, and baking the dough. Now, imagine a large industrial bakery that uses capital-intensive techniques, with machines performing most of the tasks. The industrial bakery can produce a much larger quantity of bread with the same amount of labor, but it requires a significant investment in machinery and equipment.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Capital-intensive techniques refer to the use of Machinery and Technology to produce goods and services. This approach involves investing in Capital such as factories, equipment, and research and development to increase productivity and efficiency. In contrast to [[Labour_Intensive_Techniques]], capital-intensive techniques rely more heavily on machines and automation to perform tasks. The decision to use capital-intensive techniques is often driven by the desire to reduce Labour Costs and increase Productivity. However, this approach can also lead to job displacement and requires significant upfront investments in Capital. Firms must carefully consider the trade-offs between labor and capital when deciding how to produce goods and services, taking into account factors such as the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]] and the [[Law_Of_Increasing_Opportunity_Cost]].

### Key Takeaways:

- Capital-intensive techniques involve using more machinery and technology to produce goods and services.
- These techniques often require significant investments in capital, such as factories, equipment, and research and development.
- The use of capital-intensive techniques can lead to increased productivity and efficiency in production.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

One limitation of capital-intensive techniques is that they can lead to job displacement, as machines and automation replace human labor. Another limitation is that the high upfront costs of investing in capital can be a barrier to entry for some firms, particularly small or low-income businesses. Additionally, capital-intensive techniques may not be suitable for all types of production, such as customized or artisanal goods, which may require more labor-intensive approaches. The technique also assumes that the technology and machinery used are efficient and effective, which may not always be the case. For instance, if the machinery used is outdated or not well-maintained, it can lead to decreased productivity and inefficiencies.
