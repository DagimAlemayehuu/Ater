## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of 'Choice' in economics is limited by the assumptions of rationality and perfect information, which often do not hold in reality. For instance, consumers may not have complete knowledge of available options, and their decisions may be influenced by cognitive biases, emotions, and social pressures, leading to suboptimal choices. Additionally, the range of choices available to individuals may be constrained by factors such as income, social status, and institutional barriers, effectively limiting their ability to make choices. Moreover, the notion of choice also assumes that individuals have the capacity to make decisions, which may not be the case for vulnerable populations such as children, elderly, or those with disabilities. Furthermore, 'Choice' also presumes that individuals have the freedom to make decisions, which can be curtailed by factors such as government policies, cultural norms, and social expectations, ultimately rendering the concept of choice more nuanced and context-dependent than often assumed.
