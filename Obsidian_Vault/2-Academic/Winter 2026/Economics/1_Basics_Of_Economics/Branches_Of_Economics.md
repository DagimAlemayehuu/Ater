## 2. Micro Theory

The study of economics is bifurcated into two primary branches, namely [[Macroeconomics]] and Microeconomics, which collectively encompass a broad spectrum of topics and issues. At its core, [[Definition_Of_Economics]] revolves around the allocation of [[Economic_Resources]] to satisfy [[Human_Wants]], which are unlimited, thereby necessitating the study of economics in the context of [[Scarcity_And_Choice]].

The branch of Microeconomics focuses on the study of individual [[Decision_Making_Units]], such as households, firms, and markets, to understand how they make decisions regarding the allocation of resources. This branch is concerned with the efficient allocation of resources, which is achieved through the mechanism of [[Market_Failure]] and the [[Role_Of_Government]] in correcting such failures. [[Adam_Smith]], a renowned economist, laid the foundation for microeconomics through his work on the [[Economic_Theory]] of [[Capitalist_Economy]], which emphasizes the importance of free markets in achieving an [[Efficient_Allocation]] of resources.

On the other hand, [[Macroeconomics]] deals with the study of the economy as a whole, focusing on issues such as [[Economic_Growth]], Inflation, and Unemployment. This branch of economics seeks to understand the factors that influence the overall performance of an economy and the impact of policy interventions on [[Economic_Systems]]. The study of macroeconomics relies heavily on [[Positive_Economics]], which involves the use of [[Inductive_Reasoning]] and [[Deductive_Reasoning]] to analyze data and make predictions about future economic trends.

Both branches of economics employ various tools and concepts, including the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]], which illustrates the tradeoffs involved in [[Choice]] and [[Scarcity]], and the [[Law_Of_Increasing_Opportunity_Cost]], which highlights the costs associated with making choices. Additionally, economists use the concept of [[Opportunity_Cost]] to evaluate the costs and benefits of different economic decisions.

The field of economics also encompasses [[Normative_Economics]], which involves making value judgments about what ought to be, and is often influenced by [[Inductive_Reasoning]]. The study of economics aims to provide answers to the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] of [[What_To_Produce]], [[How_To_Produce]], and [[For_Whom_To_Produce]], which are fundamental to understanding how economies function.

Ultimately, the study of [[Branches_Of_Economics]] provides a comprehensive framework for analyzing and understanding the complexities of economic systems, and for developing effective policies to promote [[Economic_Growth]] and improve the well-being of society. By examining the inter relationships between [[Economic_Resources]], [[Human_Wants]], and [[Scarcity]], economists can gain insights into the mechanisms that govern [[Resource_Allocation]] and inform decision-making processes.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The branches of economics, including microeconomics, macroeconomics, international economics, and others, have specific limitations and edge cases. For instance, microeconomics, which studies individual economic units, often relies on assumptions of rational behavior and perfect information, which may not hold in real-world scenarios, such as during times of extreme uncertainty or irrational market behavior. Similarly, macroeconomic models, which analyze aggregate economic activity, can be limited by their reliance on historical data and failure to account for unforeseen events or structural changes in the economy. Moreover, international economics often struggles to capture the complexities of global economic interactions, such as the impact of politics and culture on trade agreements. Furthermore, the increasing importance of non-market activities, such as household work and environmental degradation, poses significant challenges to the traditional branches of economics, which often neglect these issues or treat them as externalities. Overall, these limitations highlight the need for ongoing development and refinement of economic theories and models to better capture the complexities of the real world.
