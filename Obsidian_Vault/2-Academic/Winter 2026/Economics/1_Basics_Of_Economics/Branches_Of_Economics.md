## 2. Micro Theory
The study of economics is bifurcated into two primary branches, namely [[Macroeconomics]] and [[Microeconomics]], which collectively encompass a broad spectrum of topics and issues. At its core, [[Definition_Of_Economics]] revolves around the allocation of [[Scarce_Resources]] to satisfy [[Unlimited_Wants]], thereby necessitating [[Scarcity_And_Choice]]. This fundamental concept gives rise to the [[Opportunity_Cost]] of selecting one option over another, which is a crucial consideration for [[Decision_Making_Units]], including consumers and firms.

The branch of [[Microeconomics]] focuses on the interactions among these [[Decision_Making_Units]] and the resulting [[Resource_Allocation]] in specific markets. It examines how the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]] is determined and how it relates to the [[Law_Of_Increasing_Opportunity_Cost]]. In contrast, [[Macroeconomics]] explores aggregate economic phenomena, such as [[Economic_Growth]], and the factors that influence it.

Within the realm of economics, researchers employ both [[Inductive_Reasoning]] and [[Deductive_Reasoning]] to develop and test the [[Theory_Of_Economics]]. This theoretical framework provides a foundation for [[Economic_Analysis]], which aims to achieve an [[Efficient_Allocation]] of resources. Economists utilize various tools, including the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]], to address the [[Basic_Economic_Questions]] of [[What_To_Produce]], [[How_To_Produce]], and [[For_Whom_To_Produce]].

The study of economics also encompasses various [[Economic_Systems]], including the [[Capitalist_Economy]], and the [[Role_Of_Government_In_Market_Economy]]. In a market economy, the government plays a crucial role in correcting [[Market_Failure]] and ensuring that the economy operates efficiently.

Furthermore, economists engage in both [[Positive_Economics]], which involves the objective analysis of economic phenomena, and [[Normative_Economics]], which incorporates subjective value judgments. The former seeks to describe the economy as it is, while the latter proposes how the economy ought to be.

The expanding scope of economics has led to the development of specialized fields, which are encompassed within the [[Branches_Of_Economics]]. These subfields apply economic principles to specific areas, such as international trade, environmental economics, and health economics. By understanding the technical aspects of economics, researchers and policymakers can develop more effective solutions to address the complex economic challenges facing societies today.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases
The branches of economics, including microeconomics, macroeconomics, international economics, and others, have specific limitations and edge cases. For instance, microeconomics, which studies individual economic units, often relies on assumptions of rational behavior and perfect information, which may not hold in real-world scenarios, such as during times of extreme uncertainty or irrational market behavior. Similarly, macroeconomic models, which analyze aggregate economic activity, can be limited by their reliance on historical data and failure to account for unforeseen events or structural changes in the economy. Moreover, international economics often struggles to capture the complexities of global economic interactions, such as the impact of politics and culture on trade agreements. Furthermore, the increasing importance of non-market activities, such as household work and environmental degradation, poses significant challenges to the traditional branches of economics, which often neglect these issues or treat them as externalities. Overall, these limitations highlight the need for ongoing development and refinement of economic theories and models to better capture the complexities of the real world.
