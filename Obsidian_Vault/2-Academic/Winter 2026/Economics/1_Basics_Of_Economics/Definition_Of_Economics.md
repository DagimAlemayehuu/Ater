## 1. Mental Model

Imagine you have a lemonade stand and you want to make the best lemonade ever, but you only have a certain amount of sugar, lemons, and water. Economics is like figuring out how to make the best lemonade with what you have, and deciding what to do with your lemonade stand to make it successful. It's about making choices with the resources you have.

## 2. Economic Theory

The [[Definition_Of_Economics]] is the social science that studies how individuals, businesses, governments, and societies make choices about how to use [[Economic_Resources]] to satisfy [[Unlimited_Human_Wants]] in a world of [[Scarcity]]. This involves understanding how to allocate resources efficiently to meet the needs and wants of individuals and societies. It also examines the interactions between [[Decision_Making_Units]], such as consumers and producers, and the [[Economic_Systems]] they operate within.

## 4. Walkthrough

* The model starts with **Resources**, which are the inputs used to make decisions, such as sugar, lemons, and water for the lemonade stand.
* **Choices** are made based on these resources, such as deciding how much lemonade to produce or what price to charge.
* The **Opportunity Cost** of a choice is the value of the next best alternative that is given up, such as choosing to spend time making lemonade instead of doing something else.
* The goal of **Decision Making** is to reach an **Optimal Outcome**, where the lemonade stand is successful and resources are used efficiently.

## 5. Market Failures

This concept fails when there are externalities, such as environmental costs or social impacts, that are not accounted for in the decision-making process. Additionally, imperfect information or unequal distribution of resources can lead to suboptimal outcomes. Edge cases to watch out for include non-rival or non-excludable goods, such as public goods, that can lead to market failures.
