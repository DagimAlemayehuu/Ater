## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small household where the family has to decide how to allocate their income among different expenses, such as rent, food, and entertainment. The family has to make choices about what they value most and how to prioritize their spending. This is similar to how economics works at a larger scale, where individuals, businesses, and governments have to make choices about how to allocate resources to meet their unlimited wants. Just as the family has to balance their budget and make trade-offs between different expenses, economics helps us understand how societies make trade-offs between different goals and priorities.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The [[Definition_Of_Economics]] is a fundamental concept in the field of economics, which is one of the most exciting disciplines in social sciences. The word economy comes from the Greek phrase 'one who manages a household', highlighting the importance of resource management in economic decision-making. Economics involves studying how individuals, businesses, and governments make choices about how to allocate resources to satisfy their unlimited wants. This involves analyzing the [[Scarcity]] of resources and the trade-offs that must be made between different goals and priorities. The study of economics also involves understanding the [[Economic_Systems]] that are used to organize economic activity, such as [[Capitalist_Economy]] and [[Microeconomics]]. By understanding these concepts, economists can provide insights into how societies can make informed decisions about how to allocate resources and promote economic growth and development.

### Key Takeaways:

- Economics is a social science that studies how individuals, businesses, and governments manage resources to satisfy their unlimited wants.
- The word 'economy' comes from the Greek phrase 'one who manages a household'.
- Economics involves making choices about how to allocate resources in a world where wants are unlimited.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

One limitation of the definition of economics is that it assumes that individuals and businesses make rational decisions about how to allocate resources. However, in reality, people may not always make rational choices, and factors such as emotions, social pressures, and imperfect information can influence their decisions. Another limitation is that the definition focuses on the management of resources, but it does not explicitly consider the distribution of resources among different members of society. For example, a society may have a high level of economic growth, but if the benefits of that growth are concentrated among a small elite, then the majority of the population may not experience significant improvements in their standard of living. A further limitation is that the definition assumes that wants are unlimited, but it does not consider the possibility that people's wants may be influenced by their social and cultural context. For example, people's desires for certain goods and services may be shaped by advertising and social norms, rather than by any objective measure of need.
