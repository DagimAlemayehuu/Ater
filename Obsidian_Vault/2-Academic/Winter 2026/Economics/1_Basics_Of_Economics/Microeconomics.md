## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small coffee shop. The owner must decide how much coffee to buy and at what price to sell it. This decision affects not just the shop but also the suppliers and customers. Microeconomics helps the owner understand how to make this decision by analyzing the interactions between the shop, its suppliers, and its customers.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Microeconomics is a branch of economics [[Branches_Of_Economics]] that focuses on the economic behavior of individual decision-making units such as households, firms, markets, and industries [[Microeconomics]]. It examines how these units interact within specific markets to determine the prices and quantities of goods and services Market Equilibrium. The core idea is to understand the [[Scarcity]] of resources and how it influences the allocation of these resources among competing uses. By analyzing the interactions between buyers and sellers, microeconomics helps explain the Law Of Demand and Law Of Supply, which are fundamental to understanding market economies [[Capitalist_Economy]].

### Key Takeaways:

- Microeconomics studies individual economic units like households, firms, and markets.
- It analyzes how these units make decisions about resource allocation.
- Microeconomics helps understand the interactions and outcomes in specific markets.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

One limitation of microeconomics is that it assumes individuals and firms have perfect information about the market. However, in reality, information can be asymmetric, leading to market failures. For example, in the used car market, sellers often have more information about the vehicle's condition than buyers, which can result in adverse selection. Another limitation is that microeconomics typically assumes that individuals and firms act rationally. However, real-world behavior can be influenced by psychological biases and irrationality, leading to deviations from predicted outcomes. Additionally, microeconomics often ignores externalities, such as environmental impacts, which can be crucial in evaluating the overall efficiency of market outcomes.
