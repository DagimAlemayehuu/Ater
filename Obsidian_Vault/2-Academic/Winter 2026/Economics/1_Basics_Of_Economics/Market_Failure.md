## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a coffee shop where the price of coffee is determined by the market forces of supply and demand. However, if the coffee shop produces a lot of pollution, which affects the health of nearby residents, the market may not account for this Negative Externality. As a result, the market fails to allocate resources efficiently, and government intervention may be necessary to regulate the coffee shop's pollution levels. This scenario illustrates how market failure can occur when the price mechanism does not work efficiently.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Market failure occurs when the [[Price_Mechanism]] does not work efficiently, resulting in an Inefficient Allocation of resources. This can happen when there are Externalities, Information Asymmetry, or Market Power. In a Capitalist Economy, the market is supposed to allocate resources efficiently, but market failure can occur when the market does not account for the costs or benefits imposed on third parties. For example, a factory may produce a good at a low cost, but if it pollutes the environment, the market may not account for the Social Cost of pollution. As a result, the market may produce too much of the good, leading to market failure. The [[Role_Of_Government_In_Market_Economy]] is to intervene and correct market failures, ensuring an [[Efficient_Allocation]] of resources.

### Key Takeaways:

- Market failure occurs when the price mechanism does not work efficiently.
- This happens when the market does not allocate resources optimally.
- As a result, government intervention may be necessary to correct the market failure.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of market failure assumes that markets are perfectly competitive and that buyers and sellers have perfect information. However, in reality, markets may be imperfectly competitive, and buyers and sellers may not have perfect information. For example, in a market with significant Information Asymmetry, buyers may not be able to make informed decisions, leading to market failure. Additionally, the presence of Externalities can also lead to market failure, as the market may not account for the costs or benefits imposed on third parties. Furthermore, the assumption of Rationality may not always hold, as buyers and sellers may make irrational decisions. These limitations highlight the need for government intervention to correct market failures and ensure an [[Efficient_Allocation]] of resources.
