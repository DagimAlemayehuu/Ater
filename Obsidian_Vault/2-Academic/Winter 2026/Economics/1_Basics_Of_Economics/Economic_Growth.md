## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small farming community where the farmers use traditional methods to cultivate their land. One day, they get more land, which allows them to produce more crops. This is similar to an increase in the quantity of economic resources. Now, imagine that the farmers learn a new technique to cultivate the land more efficiently, which allows them to produce even more crops without needing more land. This is similar to an advance in technology. In both cases, the total output of the community increases, which is a sign of economic growth.

## 2. Foundational Concept

Economic growth refers to an increase in the total output level of an economy. This phenomenon occurs when one or both of two conditions are met: an increase in the quantity or quality of [[Economic_Growth]]'s underlying resources, or advances in Technology. The quantity of resources can increase through population growth, immigration, or the discovery of new natural resources. On the other hand, the quality of resources can improve through investments in Human Capital, such as education and training. Meanwhile, advances in technology enable the production of more goods and services with the same amount of resources, effectively increasing productivity. A [[Capitalist_Economy]] often spurs such growth by incentivizing innovation and efficient Resource Allocation.

### Key Takeaways:

- Economic growth occurs when there is an increase in the total output level of an economy.
- This can happen when there is an increase in the quantity or quality of economic resources.
- Advances in technology also drive economic growth.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of economic growth, as described, assumes that the increase in output is a result of either more resources or better technology. However, in the real world, other factors such as institutional changes, political stability, and social norms can also influence economic growth. For instance, a country with poor governance or unstable political environment may not be able to utilize its resources efficiently, even with advances in technology. Additionally, the quality of resources, such as the skill level of the workforce, can also impact the effectiveness of technological advancements. Furthermore, the concept does not account for environmental degradation or resource depletion, which can occur as a result of increased economic activity.
