## 2. Foundational Concept

In economics, [[Human_Wants]] refer to the desires or preferences that individuals have for goods and services. These wants are considered Unlimited and diverse, meaning that people always want more or better things. The study of economics is driven by the fundamental problem of [[Scarcity]], which arises because the resources available to fulfill these wants are limited. As a social science, economics seeks to understand how individuals, firms, and societies allocate these scarce resources to attain the maximum fulfillment of their Human Wants. This involves making choices about how to use resources efficiently, which is a central theme in [[Microeconomics]]. The concept of Human Wants is closely related to the Theory Of Economics, which provides a framework for understanding how economies work and how resources are allocated.

### Key Takeaways:

- Human wants are unlimited and diverse
- Economics seeks to allocate scarce resources to fulfill these wants
- The study of economics is driven by the fundamental problem of scarcity

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of human wants assumes that individuals have the capacity to make rational choices, but in reality, people may make irrational decisions. Additionally, the theory does not account for external factors such as cultural and social influences that can shape human wants. Furthermore, the assumption of unlimited wants may not hold true in cases where individuals have limited cognitive abilities or are bounded by their environment. The theory also overlooks the possibility of changing wants over time, which can be influenced by various factors such as technological advancements or changes in societal values.
