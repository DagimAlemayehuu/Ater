# 1. Mental Model

Imagine you have a big box of LEGOs, and you really want to build a cool spaceship. But then, you see a picture of a LEGO castle and think, "Wow, I want to build that too!" And then, you see a LEGO car and think, "I want that one too!" This shows how our wants can keep going and going, and we can never have enough.

# 2. Economic Theory

[[Unlimited_Human_Wants]] refer to the limitless desires of individuals for goods and services. This concept, a fundamental aspect of [[Definition_Of_Economics]], implies that people's desires for products and services are never fully satisfied. As a result, [[Scarcity]] arises because [[Economic_Resources]] are limited, making it impossible to fulfill all of these [[Unlimited_Human_Wants]].

## 4. Walkthrough

* The model starts with **Unlimited Wants**, representing the infinite desires of individuals for goods and services.
* These unlimited wants are confronted with the **Scarcity of Resources**, meaning that there are not enough resources to satisfy all desires.
* As a result, individuals must make **Choices and Trade-Offs**, selecting which wants to satisfy and which to forego.
* This leads to the concept of **Opportunity Cost**, which represents the value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.

## 5. Market Failures

This concept fails when individuals' unlimited wants lead to overconsumption or overexploitation of resources, resulting in market failures such as overfishing or pollution. Additionally, unequal distribution of resources can exacerbate the issue of unlimited wants, leading to issues of fairness and equity. Edge cases to watch out for include externalities and information asymmetry.
