## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small island economy where there are only two industries: fishing and tourism. The islanders have to decide how to allocate their resources between these two industries. If they choose to focus on fishing, they will have to give up some of their tourism potential. This illustrates the concept of Opportunity Cost, which is a fundamental principle in economics. The islanders must weigh the benefits of producing more fish against the benefits of producing more tourism-related services.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The decision of what to produce is one of the Basic Economic Questions that every economy must answer. In a market economy, the [[Price_Mechanism]] plays a crucial role in determining what goods and services are produced. When consumers demand more of a particular good or service, producers are incentivized to produce more of it. This leads to an [[Efficient_Allocation]] of resources, as resources are allocated to the production of goods and services that are in high demand. The concept of what to produce is closely related to the [[Production_Possibilities_Frontier]], which shows the various combinations of goods and services that an economy can produce given its resources and technology. In reality, economies face constraints such as [[Labour_Intensive_Techniques]] and [[Capital_Intensive_Techniques]], which can affect the decision of what to produce.

### Key Takeaways:

- Economies have limited resources and must decide what goods and services to produce.
- The decision of what to produce is influenced by the availability of resources and technology.
- The Price Mechanism plays a crucial role in determining what to produce in a market economy.

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

One limitation of the concept of what to produce is that it assumes that resources are scarce and that there is a need to make choices about how to allocate them. However, in reality, some economies may have more abundant resources than others, which can affect the decision of what to produce. Another limitation is that the concept assumes that consumers have perfect information about the goods and services available, which may not always be the case. Additionally, the concept does not take into account external factors such as [[Market_Failure]] and [[Role_Of_Government_In_Market_Economy]], which can influence the decision of what to produce.
