## 1. Mental Model

Imagine a small island with a limited amount of land, water, and food. The islanders have unlimited desires for goods and services, such as more housing, better healthcare, and luxury items. Due to the limited resources, the islanders must make choices about how to allocate their resources to satisfy their wants. This scenario illustrates the concept of scarcity, where the limited resources cannot meet the unlimited wants.

## 2. Foundational Concept

The basic economic problem is about [[Scarcity]] and choice since there are only limited amounts of resources available to produce the unlimited amount of goods and services we desire. This fundamental concept in economics is rooted in the idea that [[Human_Wants]] are unlimited, but [[Economic_Resources]] are limited. As a result, individuals, businesses, and governments must make choices about how to allocate their resources to satisfy their wants. This is a key aspect of [[Microeconomics]], which studies the behavior of individual economic units and the interactions among them. The concept of scarcity is also closely related to [[Economic_Systems]], which are the structures that societies use to organize their economic activities.

### Key Takeaways:

- Scarcity is the fundamental economic problem
- Resources are limited, but wants are unlimited
- Choices must be made due to scarcity

## 3. Limitations & Edge Cases

The concept of scarcity assumes that resources are limited and cannot satisfy all human wants. A limitation of this concept is that it does not account for potential technological advancements that could increase resource availability. Additionally, it assumes that human wants are unlimited, but in reality, human wants may be influenced by cultural and social factors. Furthermore, the concept of scarcity does not consider the possibility of abundance in certain resources, which could affect the allocation of resources. Lastly, scarcity does not directly address issues of inequality and distribution of resources.
