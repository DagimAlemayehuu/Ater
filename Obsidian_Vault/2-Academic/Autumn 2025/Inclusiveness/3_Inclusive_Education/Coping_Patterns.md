# 1. Mental Model

The concept of coping patterns can be likened to a sailor navigating through turbulent waters. Just as a sailor uses various techniques, such as adjusting the sails and rudder, to maneuver through changing sea conditions, individuals employ coping patterns, including problem-focused coping and emotional regulation, to manage stressful demands. The sailor's ability to adapt to shifting winds and waves parallels the individual's capacity to adjust their coping strategies to effectively handle internal or external demands.

# 2. Behavioral Triggers & Societal Shift

The [[Impact_Of_Disability_On_Daily_Life]] of an individual can significantly influence their coping patterns, as they may need to adapt to new challenges and stressors. The [[Meaning_Of_Impairment_To_Individual]] and their [[Current_Life_Circumstances]] can also shape their coping mechanisms, with some individuals drawing on their [[Support_System]] and [[Self_Efficacy_Beliefs]] to manage adversity. Furthermore, [[Psychological_Factors]], including [[Personality_Disposition]] and [[Coping_Patterns]], interact to determine an individual's ability to navigate stressful situations. Societal factors, such as [[Economic_Factors]] and [[Political_Factors]], can also impact an individual's coping patterns by influencing their access to resources and support. The [[Rehabilitation_Team_Members]] can play a crucial role in helping individuals develop effective coping patterns.

# 3. Cognitive Bias & Systemic Erosion

When individuals rely on maladaptive coping patterns, such as avoidance or denial, they may experience increased stress and decreased well-being. If left unchecked, these patterns can lead to [[Coping_Patterns]] erosion, where the individual's ability to effectively manage stress is compromised. 

| Maladaptive Pattern | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Avoidance | Increased anxiety |
| Denial | Decreased self-awareness |
| Substance use | Decreased problem-solving ability | 

This erosion can have severe consequences, including decreased [[Self_Efficacy_Beliefs]] and increased vulnerability to [[Impact_Of_Disability_On_Daily_Life]].

| **Confirmation Bias** | Favoring info that confirms pre-existing beliefs | A researcher only citing studies that support their hypothesis | Leads to flawed conclusions, neglect of contradictory evidence |
| **Anchoring Bias** | Relying too heavily on the first piece of information encountered | A scientist assuming a gene's function based on its sequence similarity to a well-studied gene | Results in inaccurate assumptions, potential misinterpretation of data |
