# 1. Mental Model

The concept of "Family And Disability" can be analogized to a dynamic ecosystem, where the family unit is like a coral reef. Just as a coral reef provides a supportive structure for diverse marine life to thrive, a family can offer a nurturing environment for a person with a disability to develop and adapt. Conversely, just as a coral reef can be damaged by external factors, a family's dynamics can be disrupted by the presence of a disability, impacting the individual's well-being.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Confirmation Bias** | Favoring information that confirms existing beliefs | Leads to selective data interpretation | Ignoring contradictory genetic data that challenges a hypothesis |
| **Anchoring Bias** | Relying too heavily on the first piece of information encountered | Influences estimation and prediction | Using an initial genome assembly as a reference, even if it's outdated |

## 5. Walkthrough

### Applying Cognitive Bias Awareness in Bioinformatics & Genomic Sequencing

1. **Initial Hypothesis Formation**: A researcher, influenced by **Confirmation Bias**, starts with a hypothesis about the genetic basis of a disease, based on preliminary data. They then begin to design experiments to confirm this hypothesis.

2. **Data Interpretation**: Upon collecting sequencing data, the researcher, susceptible to **Anchoring Bias**, uses an initial, possibly flawed, genome assembly as a reference point. This influences their interpretation of the data, potentially leading to incorrect conclusions.

3. **Rare Mutation Analysis**: While analyzing genomic data, the researcher encounters a rare mutation. Due to the **Availability Heuristic**, they overestimate the significance of this mutation in the disease's etiology, possibly overlooking more common contributing factors.

4. **Post-Analysis Reflection**: After obtaining results, the researcher, affected by **Hindsight Bias**, reflects on their decision-making process and claims that the outcome was "predictable." This distorts their recollection of the actual process and may lead to overconfidence in future predictions.

5. **Communication of Results**: When presenting findings, the researcher, influenced by the **Framing Effect**, chooses to emphasize a "90% chance of successful treatment" rather than a "10% chance of failure." This framing can affect how clinicians and patients perceive the treatment options.

6. **Bias Mitigation**: Recognizing these cognitive biases, the researcher implements strategies to mitigate them, such as seeking diverse perspectives, using objective data analysis tools, and critically evaluating the framing of their findings. This leads to more robust and reliable conclusions in their bioinformatics analysis.
