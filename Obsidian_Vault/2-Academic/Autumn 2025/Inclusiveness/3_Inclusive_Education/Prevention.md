# 1. Mental Model

The concept of prevention can be likened to a multi-layered defense system in a computer network. Just as a computer network has firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and incident response protocols to prevent and mitigate cyber threats, prevention in healthcare involves primary, secondary, and tertiary measures to prevent or mitigate the consequences of disease or injury. The firewalls represent primary prevention, which blocks the causes of impairment, while the intrusion detection systems and early intervention protocols represent secondary prevention, and the incident response protocols represent tertiary prevention, which aims to rehabilitate and restore function.

# 2. Systemic Function & Homeostasis

Prevention is a critical component of maintaining health and function, and it is influenced by various factors, including [[Current_Life_Circumstances]], [[Support_System]], and [[Economic_Factors]]. Effective prevention strategies take into account an individual's [[Personality_Disposition]] and [[Coping_Patterns]], as well as the [[Impact_Of_Disability_On_Daily_Life]] and the [[Meaning_Of_Impairment_To_Individual]]. A comprehensive approach to prevention also involves [[Community_Based_Rehabilitation]] and [[Disability_Inclusive_Intervention]], which aim to promote health and function in individuals with disabilities. Furthermore, prevention is often a collaborative effort involving [[Rehabilitation_Team_Members]] and [[Assistive_Technology]] to facilitate optimal outcomes. By addressing these factors, prevention can help mitigate the effects of impairment and promote overall well-being.

# 3. Pathological Failure & Necrosis

When prevention fails, pathological changes can occur, leading to impairment and disability. For instance, if primary prevention measures fail to prevent a disease or injury, secondary prevention measures may be implemented to detect and treat the condition early, but if these measures are also unsuccessful, tertiary prevention measures may be necessary to rehabilitate and restore function. However, if these measures are inadequate or ineffective, the consequences can be severe, leading to significant impairment and disability, and potentially even [[Special_Needs_Of_Pwds]]. In such cases, a re-evaluation of the prevention strategy and the implementation of new measures, such as [[Inclusive_Job_Opportunities]] and [[Strategies_For_Employment]], may be necessary to mitigate the effects of impairment and promote optimal outcomes.

2. **Primary Prevention (Firewall Configuration)**: Configure firewalls on each node to block malicious traffic. 3. **Identify Vulnerabilities**: Perform a vulnerability assessment on the network and identify potential weaknesses in the configuration. This step is similar to secondary prevention, where the goal is to detect and intervene early to prevent complications.
4. **Implement Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)**: Install IDS on each node to detect and alert on potential security threats. This enhances the network's ability to detect and respond to threats, similar to secondary prevention in healthcare.
5. **Incident Response Protocol**: Establish an incident response protocol to quickly respond to and contain security breaches. This is analogous to tertiary prevention, where the goal is to mitigate the consequences of a disease or injury.
6. **Continuous Monitoring and Update**: Continuously monitor the network for new vulnerabilities and update the prevention measures (firewalls, IDS, incident response protocol) as needed to ensure the network remains secure. This step ensures that the prevention measures remain effective over time.
