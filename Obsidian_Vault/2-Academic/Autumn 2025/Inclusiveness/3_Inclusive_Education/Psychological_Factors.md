# 3. Cognitive Bias & Systemic Erosion

The influence of Psychological Factors on an individual's behavior and decision-making processes can be compromised by cognitive biases, such as confirmation bias or anchoring bias. If left unchecked, these biases can lead to flawed decision-making and a decline in overall well-being. 

| Psychological Factor | Potential Bias | Erosion Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-Efficacy Beliefs | Confirmation Bias | Decreased Motivation |
| Stress | Anchoring Bias | Poor Decision-Making |
| Anxiety | Availability Heuristic | Increased Fear | 

When these biases are not addressed, they can contribute to the erosion of an individual's psychological resilience, making it more challenging to cope with disabling conditions.

## 5. Walkthrough

### Applying Awareness of Cognitive Biases in Telecommunications & Core Network Routing

1. **Initial Assessment**: A network engineer is tasked with evaluating the performance of a new routing protocol. They come across a report indicating the protocol has performed well in similar networks. This information serves as an **anchor**, influencing their initial positive assessment.

2. **Data Collection**: As the engineer gathers more data, they start to notice some inconsistencies in the protocol's performance. However, they tend to **favor information that confirms their pre-existing positive beliefs** (confirmation bias), initially downplaying data that suggests flaws.

3. **Incident Occurs**: A significant network outage occurs due to an unexpected flaw in the protocol. The engineer is surprised but then rationalizes that they **should have seen it coming** (hindsight bias), even though there was no way to predict the specific failure.

4. **Re-evaluation**: After the incident, the engineer reviews the data again and realizes the importance of considering all information, not just what confirms their initial beliefs. They make a conscious effort to **seek out diverse perspectives** and question their assumptions.

5. **Reporting**: When reporting on the incident, the engineer decides to present the information in a way that highlights both the positive and negative aspects clearly, aware of the **framing effect** and its potential to influence perceptions.

6. **Implementation of Bias Awareness**: The engineer implements a checklist for future evaluations that includes questions designed to mitigate cognitive biases, such as "What are the potential downsides of this approach?" and "Am I giving too much weight to recent events?" This helps in making more balanced decisions.
