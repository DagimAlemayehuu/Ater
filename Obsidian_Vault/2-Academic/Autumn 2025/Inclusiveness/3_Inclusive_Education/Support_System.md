# 1. Mental Model

A support system can be likened to a network of interconnected lifelines, where each lifeline represents a source of assistance, such as family, friends, or social groups. Just as a network of lifelines provides a safety net that can catch a person if they fall, a support system provides a network of resources that can help an individual cope with the impact of impairment on their daily life. The structural components of a support system, such as the sources of support and the relationships between them, map to the components of the lifeline network, including the number and strength of the lifelines.

# 2. Requirement Elicitation & Specification

The individual's [[Support_System]] plays a crucial role in minimizing the [[Impact_Of_Disability_On_Daily_Life]] by providing emotional, physical, and financial assistance. A strong support system can help individuals with impairments cope with their condition by enhancing their [[Self_Efficacy_Beliefs]] and promoting positive [[Coping_Patterns]]. The [[Family_And_Disability]] relationship is a critical aspect of the support system, as family members can provide essential care and support. Additionally, [[Inclusive_Job_Opportunities]] and [[Assistive_Technology]] can also be part of the support system, enabling individuals with impairments to participate fully in society. Effective support systems can mitigate the effects of [[Psychological_Factors]] and [[Economic_Factors]] that may exacerbate the impact of impairment.

# 3. Ambiguity Failures & Scope Creep

A support system can fail or break if there are gaps in the network of resources, such as a lack of family support or limited access to social services. Boundary conditions, such as a change in the individual's circumstances, can also impact the effectiveness of the support system. For example, a change in [[Current_Life_Circumstances]] may require adjustments to the support system to ensure it remains effective. If not addressed, these failures can lead to scope creep, where the individual's needs are not fully met, resulting in increased [[Impact_Of_Disability_On_Daily_Life]].

| Failure Mode | Description | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lack of Family Support | Limited or no emotional or physical support from family members | Increased stress and decreased well-being |
| Limited Access to Social Services | Insufficient access to essential services, such as healthcare or financial assistance | Decreased quality of life and increased disability |
| Change in Life Circumstances | Changes in the individual's circumstances, such as a move or job loss | Disruption to the support system and decreased effectiveness |
