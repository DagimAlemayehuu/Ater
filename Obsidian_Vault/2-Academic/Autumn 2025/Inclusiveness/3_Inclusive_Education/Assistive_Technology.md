# 1. Mental Model

The concept of Assistive Technology (AT) can be likened to a customized prosthetic limb, where the limb's structural components, such as sensors and actuators, map to the AT's components, like software and hardware. Just as a prosthetic limb is designed to compensate for the loss of a biological limb, AT is designed to prevent, compensate, or relieve impairments, disabilities, or handicaps. The AT's interface and user input systems can be thought of as the "nervous system" of the device, allowing users to control and interact with it.

# 2. Systemic Function & Homeostasis

Assistive Technology plays a vital role in maintaining the homeostasis of individuals with disabilities by providing them with the necessary tools to interact with their environment. The [[Impact_Of_Disability_On_Daily_Life]] of an individual can be significantly improved with the use of AT, which can help to mitigate the effects of [[Nature_Of_Impairment_To_Individual]]. For instance, AT can enable individuals with mobility impairments to navigate their surroundings through the use of wheelchairs or walkers, thereby promoting [[Self_Efficacy_Beliefs]] and [[Coping_Patterns]]. Additionally, AT can also facilitate [[Inclusive_Job_Opportunities]] and reduce [[Barriers_To_Employment]] by providing individuals with disabilities with the necessary accommodations to perform their job tasks effectively. Furthermore, the use of AT can be influenced by various factors, including [[Economic_Factors]], [[Psychological_Factors]], and [[Support_System]].

# 3. Pathological Failure & Necrosis

The failure of Assistive Technology can occur when the device is not properly designed or matched to the individual's needs, leading to a breakdown in the [[Twin_Track_Approach]] to rehabilitation. For example, if an individual with a visual impairment is provided with a screen reader that is not compatible with their computer system, it can lead to frustration and decreased [[Self_Efficacy_Beliefs]]. In such cases, the AT device can become a source of disability rather than a means of compensation, highlighting the importance of proper [[Community_Based_Rehabilitation]] and [[Disability_Inclusive_Intervention]]. The boundary conditions for AT failure include inadequate [[Rehabilitation_Team_Members]] training, insufficient [[Assistive_Technology]] maintenance, and lack of [[Preventive_Maintenance]]. 

| AT Component | Failure Mode | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardware | Incompatibility | Device malfunction |
| Software | Inadequate training | User frustration |
| Interface | Insufficient maintenance | Decreased usability | 
| User Input | Lack of customization | Abandonment of device |
