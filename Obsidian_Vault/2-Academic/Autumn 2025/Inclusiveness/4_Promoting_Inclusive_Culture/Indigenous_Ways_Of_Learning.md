# 1. Mental Model
Imagine a tree with deep roots that connect the present to the past, and its branches stretching towards the future. Indigenous Ways of Learning can be thought of as a holistic approach that weaves together the threads of community, culture, and land, much like the interconnected roots and branches of a tree. This approach honors the wisdom of ancestors and Elders, and recognizes that learning is a lifelong journey that occurs in relationship with others and the natural world.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Indigenous Ways of Learning are grounded in [[Community-Based Learning]] and [[Place-Based Education]], which emphasize the importance of contextualizing learning within the local community and environment. This approach often involves [[Storytelling]] as a pedagogical tool, where Elders and knowledge keepers share stories that convey cultural values, histories, and traditional practices. Instructional design that incorporates Indigenous Ways of Learning might involve co-creation of curriculum with Indigenous community members, and the use of [[Land-Based Learning]] experiences that connect students with the natural world.

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
When incorporating Indigenous Ways of Learning into educational practices, it's essential to consider the potential cognitive load associated with decolonizing and reconciling historical narratives. Students may experience [[Cognitive Dissonance]] as they confront dominant narratives that have been challenged or reframed. Moreover, knowledge gaps may arise when instructional materials and pedagogies fail to account for the diversity of Indigenous experiences and perspectives. Effective implementation of Indigenous Ways of Learning requires careful consideration of [[Cultural Sensitivity]] and [[Contextual Relevance]] to mitigate these risks and create a supportive learning environment.
