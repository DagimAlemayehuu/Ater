# 1. Mental Model
Imagine you're the captain of a school club, and you want to make sure everyone feels welcome to join. Inclusive values are like a set of guidelines that help you make sure your club is open and friendly to all students, no matter their background, ability, or interests. It's like making sure the club's door is open, and everyone knows they can come in and participate.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Inclusive values operate within the framework of [[Social_Constructivist_Theory]], which posits that knowledge and understanding are constructed through social interactions. In the context of instructional design, inclusive values inform the creation of learning environments that promote [[Cultural_Responsiveness]] and [[Universal_Design_For_Learning]] (UDL). By incorporating the seven pillars of inclusion - Access, Attitude, Choice, Partnership, Communication, Policy, and Opportunity - educators can develop curricula that acknowledge and value diverse perspectives, ultimately fostering a sense of belonging among learners. This approach enables learners to engage with the material in a way that is meaningful to them, promoting deeper understanding and increased motivation.

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
When learners perceive that their values and experiences are not reflected in the learning environment, it can lead to increased [[Cognitive_Dissonance]] and decreased motivation. Conversely, when inclusive values are embedded in the learning design, learners are more likely to experience a sense of [[Cognitive_Fluency]], as they can easily relate to the material and see its relevance to their lives. However, if the learning environment neglects to address issues of inclusivity, learners may experience [[Knowledge_Gaps]] that hinder their ability to fully engage with the subject matter. Furthermore, educators must be aware of the potential for [[Implicit_Bias]] to influence their instructional design, and take steps to mitigate its impact. By acknowledging and addressing these factors, educators can create learning environments that promote inclusive values and support the diverse needs of all learners.
