# 1. Mental Model
Imagine you're in a school where everyone gets an equal chance to participate in the school play, regardless of their background or who their parents are. Fairness is like making sure everyone has an equal opportunity to try out for the lead role, get selected for the play, and have access to the same resources, like rehearsal space and costumes. This way, everyone has a fair shot at succeeding.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Fairness in educational settings operates through the implementation of [[Equitable_Assessment]] practices, ensuring that all students have access to the same [[Learning_Opportunities]] and [[Resource_Allocation]]. This involves designing instructional materials and activities that are free from bias, providing accommodations for students with disabilities, and making sure that students have equal opportunities to participate in decision-making processes. By doing so, educators can promote a sense of fairness and inclusivity, which can lead to increased student engagement and motivation. The mechanical process of achieving fairness involves analyzing and addressing potential biases in [[Curriculum_Design]] and [[Instructional_Strategies]].

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
When fairness is not achieved, students from marginalized groups may experience increased [[Cognitive_Load]] due to the added stress and anxiety of facing unfair barriers to learning. This can lead to knowledge gaps, as students who do not have equal access to resources and opportunities may fall behind their peers. Furthermore, a lack of fairness can result in [[Implicit_Bias]] and [[Stereotype_Threat]], which can further exacerbate knowledge gaps and decreased academic performance. In order to mitigate these effects, educators must be aware of the potential for unfairness and take steps to address it, such as providing [[Scaffolding]] and [[Support]] to students who may be at a disadvantage. By acknowledging and addressing these boundary conditions, educators can work to create a more fair and inclusive learning environment.

## 5. Walkthrough
### Scenario: Evaluating Fairness in School Play Participation

Maria is the drama teacher at a local school. She wants to ensure fairness in the selection process for the school play. Here are the steps she takes:

1. **Define Clear Criteria**: Maria establishes clear, transparent criteria for selecting students for lead roles and supporting roles in the play. These criteria include acting ability, availability, and participation in previous school plays.

2. **Advertise Opportunities**: She advertises tryouts for the play to all students, ensuring that everyone has an equal chance to participate. This includes posting announcements in the school hallways, during morning assemblies, and in each classroom.

3. **Provide Resources**: Maria makes sure that all students have access to the same resources, such as rehearsal space, costumes, and acting coaches, if needed.

4. **Accommodate Needs**: She works with the school administration to provide accommodations for students with disabilities, ensuring they can participate fully in the tryouts and rehearsals.

5. **Monitor and Adjust**: Throughout the process, Maria monitors for biases and takes steps to address any issues that arise, ensuring that the selection process remains fair.

### Intermediate Calculations or State Changes
- **Initial State**: All students have an equal opportunity to try out for the play.
- **Change**: Clear criteria and resource allocation are implemented to ensure fairness.
- **Outcome**: Students feel they have a fair chance at being selected for the play, leading to increased motivation and engagement.
