# 1. Mental Model
Imagine you're the captain of a soccer team, and you want to build a strong team. Recruitment and Advancement is like finding the best players, giving them the right training, and helping them improve their skills so they can play better positions or even become team leaders. Just as a soccer team needs a strong lineup and player development to win, organizations need effective recruitment and advancement strategies to build a talented and diverse workforce.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Recruitment and Advancement works mechanically through a combination of [[Talent_Pipeline_Management]], [[Competency_Based_Selection]], and [[Professional_Development_Plans]]. The process begins with identifying job requirements and desired competencies, then sourcing candidates through targeted recruitment strategies. Once hired, employees undergo training and development programs tailored to their needs, which are often designed using [[Addie_Model]] principles. As employees progress, their skills and knowledge are assessed through [[Performance_Appraisal]] systems, informing future development and advancement opportunities.

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
Boundary conditions for effective Recruitment and Advancement include managing [[Cognitive_Load]] during the recruitment process, ensuring that job requirements and expectations are clear, and minimizing [[Information_Overload]] during training. Failure states can occur when there are [[Skill_Mismatches]] between job requirements and employee competencies, or when [[Bias_In_Hiring]] leads to unfair recruitment practices. Constraints on advancement may arise from [[Limited_Career_Pathways]] or inadequate [[Professional_Development_Resources]], highlighting the need for organizations to invest in employee growth and provide clear pathways for advancement.

## 5. Walkthrough
### Scenario:
An organization is looking to improve its Recruitment and Advancement processes. The goal is to enhance employee skills, reduce turnover, and increase diversity.

### Steps:

1. **Identify Job Requirements and Desired Competencies:**
   - Conduct a job analysis to determine key skills and competencies needed for each role.
   - Example: For a software developer position, key competencies might include programming languages (Python, Java), software development methodologies (Agile), and tools (GitHub).

2. **Source Candidates through Targeted Recruitment Strategies:**
   - Utilize social media, job boards, and professional networks to reach a diverse pool of candidates.
   - Example: Post job openings on LinkedIn, Glassdoor, and industry-specific forums.

3. **Implement Competency-Based Selection:**
   - Develop assessment tools (e.g., coding tests for developers) to evaluate candidates' competencies.
   - Example: Use HackerRank for coding assessments.

4. **Develop and Implement Professional Development Plans:**
   - Create tailored training programs using the ADDIE Model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation).
   - Example: Design a Python programming course for junior developers.

5. **Evaluate and Advance Employees:**
   - Use performance appraisal systems to assess employee skills and knowledge.
   - Example: Conduct quarterly reviews and provide feedback for growth.

### Intermediate Calculations:
- **Turnover Rate:** If the current turnover rate is 20%, and the goal is to reduce it by 5%, the target turnover rate would be 15%.
- **Diversity Metrics:** Track the percentage of underrepresented groups in the hiring pipeline and adjust strategies accordingly.
