# 1. Mental Model
Imagine you're looking at a photograph of a city. The photo represents the city, but it's not the actual city. It shows a snapshot of what the city looks like, with its buildings, streets, and people. In a similar way, representation in a community or institution is like a snapshot that shows whether people with disabilities are present and included in various roles and positions.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Representation works mechanically by influencing learners' perceptions of [[Social_Identity]] and [[Group_Membership]], which in turn affect their sense of belonging and engagement. When instructional materials and learning environments demonstrate [[Diverse_Representation]], learners are more likely to develop empathy and understanding of different perspectives. Effective instructional design should incorporate diverse representations to promote inclusivity and challenge learners' [[Implicit_Biases]]. By doing so, educators can create a learning environment that values and supports the presence of people with disabilities.

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
Inadequate representation can lead to [[Cognitive_Dissonance]] and increased cognitive load, as learners struggle to reconcile the lack of diversity with their existing knowledge and values. Conversely, authentic representation can mitigate knowledge gaps by providing learners with accurate and relatable information about people with disabilities. However, if representation is tokenistic or superficial, it can create [[False_Familiarity]] and reinforce existing biases, ultimately undermining the learning process. Furthermore, a lack of representation can result in [[Situational_Implicit_Bias]], where learners' biases are reinforced by the absence of diverse perspectives.

## 5. Walkthrough
### Scenario: Evaluating Representation in an Educational Setting

A school is reviewing its representation of people with disabilities in its curriculum and learning materials. The goal is to ensure that students see themselves and others reflected in a positive and realistic way.

#### Steps:
1. **Current State Assessment**: The school begins by assessing its current curriculum and learning materials to identify the level of representation of people with disabilities. They find that people with disabilities are largely absent or tokenistically represented.

2. **Gap Analysis**: The school conducts a gap analysis to understand the disparity between the current state and the desired state of representation. They identify a need for more diverse and authentic representations.

3. **Goal Setting**: The school sets specific goals for improving representation, including increasing the number of images and stories featuring people with disabilities and ensuring that these representations are positive and realistic.

4. **Strategy Development**: The school develops a strategy to achieve its goals, which includes working with diverse content creators, incorporating more inclusive language, and providing training for teachers on how to discuss representation and disability.

5. **Implementation and Monitoring**: The school implements its strategy and establishes a system to monitor progress. This includes regular reviews of curriculum materials and feedback from students and teachers.
