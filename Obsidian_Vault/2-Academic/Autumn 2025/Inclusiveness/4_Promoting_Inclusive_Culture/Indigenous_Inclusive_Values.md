# 1. Mental Model
Imagine you're part of a community that has lived in harmony with nature for generations. The way you learn and interact with each other is deeply rooted in respecting the land, your ancestors, and the community. This is similar to how Indigenous Inclusive Values work, where the focus is on integrating the customs, norms, and ways of learning of Indigenous societies into educational practices.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Indigenous Inclusive Values operate at the intersection of [[Cultural_Historical_Activity_Theory]] and [[Social_Constructivist]] learning theories. Mechanically, this concept works by recognizing that Indigenous ways of learning, such as experiential learning through storytelling and hands-on activities, can be integrated into educational practices to create a more inclusive and culturally responsive learning environment. This approach acknowledges the importance of [[Intergenerational_Knowledge_Transmission]] in preserving Indigenous cultures and promoting cross-cultural understanding. By incorporating Indigenous values and practices into instructional design, educators can create learning experiences that are more relevant and engaging for both Indigenous and non-Indigenous learners.

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
When implementing Indigenous Inclusive Values in educational settings, it's essential to consider the potential cognitive load associated with navigating different cultural norms and values. Learners may experience [[Cognitive_Dissonance]] when confronted with new and unfamiliar ways of learning, which can lead to knowledge gaps if not addressed properly. Moreover, the risk of [[Cultural_Tokenism]] and [[Cultural_Appropriation]] can create boundary conditions that hinder the effective integration of Indigenous values into educational practices. To mitigate these risks, educators must be aware of their own biases and cultural limitations, and engage in ongoing [[Critical_Reflection]] to ensure that Indigenous Inclusive Values are being implemented in a respectful and meaningful way.

## 5. Walkthrough
Here's a step-by-step scenario applying Indigenous Inclusive Values:

Maria is an educator designing a new environmental science course for Indigenous and non-Indigenous students. She wants to incorporate Indigenous Inclusive Values to promote cross-cultural understanding.

1. **Identify the learning objectives**: Maria determines that students will learn about the importance of land conservation and the interconnectedness of human and natural systems.
2. **Integrate Indigenous ways of learning**: Maria incorporates experiential learning activities, such as a field trip to a nearby Indigenous community, where students learn about traditional land management practices through storytelling and hands-on activities.
3. **Incorporate intergenerational knowledge transmission**: Maria invites an Elder from the Indigenous community to share their knowledge and experiences with the students, highlighting the importance of preserving traditional knowledge.
4. **Address potential cognitive load**: Maria provides opportunities for students to reflect on their own cultural biases and assumptions, and offers support for students who may experience cultural dissonance.
5. **Evaluate the effectiveness**: Maria assesses student learning outcomes and gathers feedback from students and the Indigenous community to ensure that the course is meeting its goals and respecting Indigenous cultures.
