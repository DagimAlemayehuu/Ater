# 1. Mental Model
Imagine you're at a park with a big, beautiful lake, but there's a huge fence around it that you can't climb over. This fence represents an environmental barrier, like a physical obstacle that prevents people with certain needs or abilities from enjoying the lake. Similarly, technical barriers are like super complicated computer systems that are hard to use for people who aren't tech-savvy.

# 2. Learning Theory & Instructional Design
Environmental and technical barriers can significantly hinder the learning process by creating obstacles that prevent individuals from accessing information or participating fully. In instructional design, it's crucial to consider [[Universal_Design_For_Learning]] (UDL) principles to minimize these barriers. This involves providing multiple means of representation, expression, and engagement. For instance, ensuring that digital materials are compatible with [[Screen_Readers]] and [[Closed_Captions]] can help reduce technical barriers. Moreover, designing learning environments that are physically accessible and inclusive can mitigate environmental barriers. Effective instructional design should also take into account the [[User_Experience]] (UX) to ensure that learning platforms and materials are intuitive and easy to use.

# 3. Cognitive Load & Knowledge Gaps
The presence of environmental and technical barriers can significantly increase [[Cognitive_Load]], as individuals may need to expend additional mental effort to navigate around these obstacles. This can lead to frustration and decreased motivation, ultimately widening [[Knowledge_Gaps]]. For example, if a student with a disability encounters a technical barrier while trying to access online course materials, it may hinder their ability to learn and keep up with their peers. Furthermore, environmental barriers such as poor lighting or uncomfortable seating can also impact cognitive load and learning outcomes. It's essential to identify and address these barriers to create an inclusive learning environment that supports the diverse needs of all learners. By doing so, educators can help reduce [[Split_Attention]] and promote more effective learning.

## 5. Walkthrough
### Scenario: Implementing Accessible Online Course Materials

A university is planning to implement a new online course management system. The goal is to ensure that all course materials are accessible to students with disabilities.

#### Steps:
1. **Needs Assessment**: Identify the types of disabilities that might affect students' ability to access course materials (e.g., visual impairments, hearing impairments, mobility issues).

2. **Technical Requirements**: Determine the technical specifications for accessible materials, such as compatibility with screen readers, inclusion of closed captions for videos, and use of alt text for images.

3. **Material Conversion**: Convert all digital course materials to meet these technical specifications. This includes providing transcripts for audio and video content, using readable fonts, and ensuring that all interactive elements can be navigated using a keyboard.

4. **Testing and Feedback**: Conduct thorough testing of the course materials with a group of students with disabilities to identify any barriers. Gather feedback and make necessary adjustments.

5. **Training for Instructors**: Provide training for instructors on how to create accessible course materials and use the new course management system effectively.

#### Intermediate Calculations:
- **Accessibility Checklist**: Develop a checklist to ensure all materials meet accessibility standards. For example, for each video, confirm that:
  - A closed caption is provided.
  - A transcript is available.
  - The video player is compatible with screen readers.
