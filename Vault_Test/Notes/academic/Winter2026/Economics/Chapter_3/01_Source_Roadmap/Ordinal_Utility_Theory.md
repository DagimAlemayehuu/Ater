## Mental Model
The concept of ordinal utility is a fundamental idea in microeconomics that helps us understand how consumers make choices. It's based on the idea that consumers can rank their preferences, but can't assign a specific numerical value to each good or service.

## The Economic Intuition
Ordinal utility theory assumes that consumers can compare and rank their preferences for different goods and services. This ranking is subjective and based on individual tastes and preferences. For example, imagine you prefer burgers to pizza. You can't say exactly how much more you prefer burgers, but you know you prefer them. This theory is useful because it helps economists understand how consumers make decisions without having to know the exact utility they get from each good.

## The Comparison Mechanism
The comparison mechanism in ordinal utility theory is based on indifference curves. An indifference curve is a graph that shows all the combinations of two goods that give a consumer the same level of satisfaction or utility. Consumers are indifferent between these combinations because they provide the same level of utility. For instance, if you like both burgers and fries, an indifference curve might show all the combinations of burgers and fries that give you the same level of satisfaction.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal math behind ordinal utility theory involves using indifference curves and budget constraints to model consumer behavior.
