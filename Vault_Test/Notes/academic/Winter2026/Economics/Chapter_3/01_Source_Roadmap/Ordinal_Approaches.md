## Mental Model
The concept of Ordinal Approaches is a way to measure or compare consumer utility. There are two major approaches: the Cardinal Approach and Ordinal approaches. The Ordinal approach focuses on ranking preferences rather than assigning specific numerical values to utility.

## The Economic Intuition
Ordinal Approaches are based on the idea that consumers can rank their preferences. This ranking is crucial for understanding how consumers make choices. For instance, a consumer might prefer one product over another but not be able to quantify exactly how much more they prefer it. This approach is practical because it acknowledges that while consumers can compare and rank their preferences, they might not always be able to assign a precise numerical value to their utility.

## The Comparison Mechanism
The comparison mechanism in Ordinal Approaches involves analyzing how consumers rank different bundles of goods or services. This is often visualized using indifference curves, which show combinations of goods that provide the same level of satisfaction. By comparing these curves, economists can understand consumer preferences and make predictions about consumer behavior. For example, if a consumer prefers one bundle of goods over another, their indifference curve will be steeper or shallower in certain areas, reflecting their relative preferences.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal math and models behind Ordinal Approaches involve using indifference curves and utility functions that represent consumer preferences. These models help economists understand how changes in prices or income affect consumer choices. A key concept is the idea of diminishing marginal rate of substitution, which describes how consumers are willing to give up some of one good to get more of another, but at a decreasing rate. This concept is crucial for understanding consumer behavior and is represented formally through equations and graphs.
