## Mental Model
The budget line is a fundamental concept in microeconomics that represents the various combinations of two goods that a consumer can purchase given their income and the prices of the goods. It is a graphical representation of the consumer's budget constraint.

## The Economic Intuition
The budget line shows the trade-off between two goods that a consumer can afford. Any bundle of goods on or within the budget line is affordable, while any bundle outside the budget line is unaffordable. The slope of the budget line is negative, indicating that in order to consume more of one good, the consumer must consume less of the other good.

## The Calculation Logic
The budget line can be calculated using the following formula:

Let $M$ be the consumer's income, $P_X$ be the price of good X, and $P_Y$ be the price of good Y. The budget constraint is given by:

$$P_X \cdot X + P_Y \cdot Y = M$$

The slope of the budget line is given by:

$$\text{slope} = -\frac{P_X}{P_Y}$$

## The Formal Math & Models
The budget line can be represented mathematically as:

$$Y = -\frac{P_X}{P_Y} \cdot X + \frac{M}{P_Y}$$

This equation represents a line with a negative slope, indicating that as the consumer consumes more of good X, they must consume less of good Y.
