## Mental Model

The concept of Total and Marginal Utility Curves is crucial in understanding consumer behavior in economics. Utility refers to the satisfaction or pleasure a consumer derives from consuming a good or service.

## The Economic Intuition

The economic intuition behind total and marginal utility curves can be understood by analyzing how consumers make decisions about consuming goods and services. When a consumer starts consuming a good, their total utility increases. However, as they continue to consume more units of the good, the marginal utility - the additional utility gained from consuming one more unit - starts to decrease. This happens because the consumer may start to feel satiated or even tired of the good.

At some point, the marginal utility becomes zero, which means that the consumer no longer gains any additional satisfaction from consuming another unit of the good. If the consumer continues to consume beyond this point, the marginal utility becomes negative, indicating that the consumer is now experiencing dissatisfaction or discomfort.

## The Choice Mechanism

The choice mechanism involves how consumers make decisions about how much of a good to consume based on their preferences and budget constraints. Consumers will continue to consume a good until the marginal utility they derive from it equals the marginal cost or price they have to pay.
