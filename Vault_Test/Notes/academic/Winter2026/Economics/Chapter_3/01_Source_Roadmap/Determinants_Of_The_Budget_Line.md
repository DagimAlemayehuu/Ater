## Mental Model
The budget line is a fundamental concept in microeconomics that represents the various combinations of two goods that a consumer can purchase given their income and the prices of the goods. Understanding the determinants of the budget line is crucial for analyzing consumer behavior and decision-making.

## The Economic Intuition
The budget line changes when any of the following factors change:
1. The consumer's income: An increase in income shifts the budget line outward, allowing the consumer to purchase more goods, while a decrease in income shifts it inward, reducing purchasing power.
2. The prices of goods: A decrease in the price of one or both goods shifts the budget line outward, making the goods more affordable, while an increase in price shifts it inward.
3. Taxes, subsidies, and rationing: These factors can also affect the budget line by altering the consumer's effective income or the prices of goods.

## The Calculation Logic
The budget line can be represented by the equation: $p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 = I$, where $p_1$ and $p_2$ are the prices of the two goods, $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the quantities of the goods, and $I$ is the consumer's income. Changes in income, prices, or other factors that affect the consumer's purchasing power will change the slope or intercepts of this line.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal representation of the budget line is given by the equation $p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 = I$. The slope of the budget line is $-\frac{p_1}{p_2}$, and the intercepts are $\frac{I}{p_1}$ and $\frac{I}{p_2}$. Changes in the determinants of the budget line will change these parameters, shifting or rotating the budget line.
