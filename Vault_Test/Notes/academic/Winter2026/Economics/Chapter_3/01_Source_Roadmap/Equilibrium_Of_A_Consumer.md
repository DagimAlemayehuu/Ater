## Mental Model
The concept of equilibrium of a consumer refers to the point at which a consumer maximizes their total utility given their limited income and the prices of goods and services. This concept is crucial in understanding how consumers make decisions about what goods and services to purchase.

## The Economic Intuition
The economic intuition behind the equilibrium of a consumer is that they will allocate their income in a way that maximizes their total utility. This means that the consumer will compare the marginal utility of each good or service to its price and allocate their income accordingly. If the marginal utility of a good or service is greater than its price, the consumer will purchase more of it. On the other hand, if the marginal utility is less than its price, the consumer will purchase less of it.

## The Calculation Logic
The calculation logic for determining the equilibrium of a consumer involves comparing the marginal utility of each good or service to its price. The consumer will continue to purchase a good or service until the marginal utility equals its price. This can be represented by the equation:

where MUx and MUy are the marginal utilities of goods x and y, and Px and Py are their prices.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal math and models for determining the equilibrium of a consumer involve the use of indifference curves and budget lines. The indifference curve represents the different combinations of goods and services that provide the same level of utility to the consumer. The budget line represents the different combinations of goods and services that the consumer can afford given their income and the prices of goods and services.

The equilibrium of the consumer is determined at the point where the indifference curve is tangent to the budget line. This can be represented by the equation:
