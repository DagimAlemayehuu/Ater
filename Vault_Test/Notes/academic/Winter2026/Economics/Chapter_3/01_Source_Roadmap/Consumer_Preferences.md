## Mental Model

Consumer preferences refer to the way individuals make choices among different bundles of goods and services. Understanding consumer preferences is crucial in economics as it helps businesses and policymakers predict how consumers will react to changes in prices, income, and other factors.

## The Economic Intuition

The economic intuition behind consumer preferences is that individuals have different tastes and preferences when it comes to consuming goods and services. These preferences can be influenced by various factors such as income, prices, and cultural background. To understand consumer preferences, economists use the concept of a consumption bundle, which is a complete list of goods and services that are available for choice by the consumer.

For example, consider three consumption bundles:

| Bundle | Good 1 | Good 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1 Pizza | 1 Coca Cola |
| B | 1 Pizza | 1 Pepsi |
| C | 1 Burger | 1 Beer |

Consumers can rank these bundles according to their preferences. For instance, a consumer may prefer bundle A over bundle B because they prefer Coca Cola over Pepsi.

## The Choice Mechanism

The choice mechanism refers to the way consumers make decisions among different consumption bundles. Economists assume that consumers have a preference ordering over different bundles, which means they can rank bundles according to their desirability. This preference ordering is represented using the following notation:

* X ≻ Y: Bundle X is strictly preferred to bundle Y
* X ~ Y: Bundle X is indifferent to bundle Y
* X ⪰ Y: Bundle X is weakly preferred to bundle Y

For example, if a consumer prefers bundle X over bundle Y, but is indifferent between bundle Y and bundle Z, we can write:

X ≻ Y and Y ~ Z

## The Formal Math & Models

The formal math and models used to represent consumer preferences include the concept of a utility function, which is a mathematical function that represents the consumer's preferences over different bundles. The utility function is typically denoted by u(x1, x2), where x1 and x2 are the quantities of two goods.
