## Mental Model
The Cardinalist approach in economics relies on the concept of cardinal utility, which assumes that utility can be measured numerically. This approach has several limitations that affect its applicability in real-world economic scenarios.

## The Economic Intuition
The economic intuition behind the limitation of the Cardinalist approach lies in its unrealistic assumptions. Firstly, the assumption of cardinal utility is extremely doubtful because it is challenging to measure utility numerically. People may not always be able to quantify their preferences accurately.
- The assumption that utility can be measured on a numerical scale is problematic.
- Another issue is the assumption of constant utility of money, which is also unrealistic.
- The utility derived from money can vary significantly from person to person and even for the same person over time.
- Lastly, the axiom of diminishing marginal utility, which suggests that the marginal utility of a good decreases as its consumption increases, is considered a psychological law that must be taken for granted.
