## Mental Model
The concept of utility in economics refers to the satisfaction or pleasure an individual derives from consuming a good or service. There are two main approaches to measuring utility: the Cardinalist school and the Ordinalist school.

## The Economic Intuition
The Cardinalist school, pioneered by Alfred Marshall, assumes that utility can be measured objectively and quantitatively using units called utils. This approach claims that it is possible to express the utility or satisfaction an individual derives from consuming a commodity in quantitative terms, such as 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and so on. On the other hand, the Ordinalist school, pioneered by John R. Hicks, argues that utility cannot be measured objectively and quantitatively. Instead, it suggests that utility can only be measured subjectively.

## The Calculation Logic
The Cardinalist approach is considered less realistic because it assumes that utility can be measured quantitatively, which is not possible. In contrast, the Ordinalist approach is more realistic as it relies on qualitative measurement. However, the Ordinalist approach has its limitations, as it does not provide a precise way to measure utility.
