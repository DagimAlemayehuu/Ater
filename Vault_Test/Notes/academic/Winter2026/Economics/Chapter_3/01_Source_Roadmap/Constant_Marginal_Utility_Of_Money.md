## Mental Model

The concept of Constant Marginal Utility of Money is a crucial assumption in cardinal utility theory. It states that the marginal utility derived from a unit of money is constant, regardless of the amount of money consumed. This assumption is critical because money is used as a standard unit of measurement of utility.

## The Economic Intuition

The economic intuition behind the Constant Marginal Utility of Money assumption is that money is a neutral good that does not affect the overall satisfaction or utility of a consumer. In other words, having more money does not necessarily lead to more happiness or satisfaction. This assumption allows economists to use money as a numéraire, or a unit of account, to measure the utility of other goods and services.

## The Calculation Logic

The calculation logic behind the Constant Marginal Utility of Money assumption is that it simplifies the analysis of consumer behavior. By assuming that the marginal utility of money is constant, economists can focus on the marginal utility of other goods and services, without having to consider the effects of changes in money income. This assumption is often used in microeconomic models, such as the theory of consumer choice.

## The Formal Math & Models

The formal math behind the Constant Marginal Utility of Money assumption can be represented as follows:

Let $M$ be the amount of money and $U(M)$ be the utility derived from money. The marginal utility of money is given by:

$$\frac{\partial U(M)}{\partial M} = k$$

where $k$ is a constant. This equation states that the marginal utility of money is constant, regardless of the amount of money consumed.
