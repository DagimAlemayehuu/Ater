## Mental Model
The concept of Assumptions of the Cardinal Utility Theory is crucial in understanding consumer behavior in microeconomics. This theory is based on several key assumptions that help economists predict how consumers will make decisions about which goods and services to purchase.

## The Economic Intuition
The Assumptions of the Cardinal Utility Theory are foundational to understanding consumer choice. The first assumption is that consumers are rational, meaning they will choose the option that brings them the most satisfaction. Second, utility is cardinally measurable, which implies that consumers can quantify their satisfaction. Third, there's the assumption of constant marginal utility of money, which is critical because money serves as a standard unit to measure utility. Fourth, the theory assumes diminishing marginal utility, meaning that as a consumer consumes more of a good, the additional satisfaction they get from each additional unit decreases. Lastly, the total utility of a basket of goods depends on the quantities of individual commodities, and it's assumed that consumers derive satisfaction from consuming goods one at a time.

## The Calculation Logic
To apply the Assumptions of the Cardinal Utility Theory, economists use various calculations. For instance, when analyzing consumer behavior, one might calculate the marginal utility of a good by determining the change in total utility from consuming one more unit of that good. The formula for marginal utility is \(MU = \frac{\Delta TU}{\Delta Q}\), where \(MU\) is marginal utility, \(\Delta TU\) is the change in total utility, and \(\Delta Q\) is the change in quantity consumed.

## The Formal Math & Models
The theory is also represented through formal mathematical models. For example, the budget constraint of a consumer can be represented as \(P_1Q_1 + P_2Q_2 \leq I\), where \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) are the prices of goods 1 and 2, \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\) are the quantities of goods 1 and 2 consumed, and \(I\) is the consumer's income. The goal of the consumer is to maximize utility \(U(Q_1, Q_2)\) subject to this budget constraint.
