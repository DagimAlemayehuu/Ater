## Mental Model
The budget equation is a fundamental concept in microeconomics that helps consumers make decisions about how to allocate their income among different goods and services. It represents the various combinations of two goods that a consumer can purchase given their income and the prices of the goods.

## The Economic Intuition
The budget equation is based on the idea that a consumer has a limited amount of money to spend on goods and services. The consumer must make choices about how to allocate their income among different goods, taking into account the prices of each good. The budget equation helps to visualize the trade-offs that a consumer faces when making these decisions.

## The Calculation Logic
To derive the budget equation, we need to know the consumer's income (M) and the prices of the two goods (P1 and P2). The budget equation is given by:

P1X + P2Y = M

where X and Y are the quantities of the two goods that the consumer purchases.

For example, suppose a consumer has an income of 100 birr to spend on two goods, X and Y, with prices of 3 birr and 5 birr, respectively. The budget equation would be:

3X + 5Y = 100

## The Formal Math & Models
The budget equation can be represented graphically as a budget line, which shows the various combinations of the two goods that the consumer can purchase. The slope of the budget line is given by:

- P1/P2

Using the previous example, the slope of the budget line would be:

- 3/5

The intercepts of the budget line can be found by setting one of the goods to zero. For example, when Y = 0, X = M/P1 = 100/3 = 33.33. When X = 0, Y = M/P2 = 100/5 = 20.
