## Mental Model
The concept of the Assumption of Constant Utility of Money is crucial in understanding consumer behavior in microeconomics. This assumption is one of the limitations of the Cardinalist approach, which is a method used to analyze consumer preferences and utility.

## The Economic Intuition
The Assumption of Constant Utility of Money implies that the utility derived from money remains constant, regardless of the amount of money a person has. In simpler terms, it assumes that the marginal utility of money does not change with an increase or decrease in income. This assumption is unrealistic because, in reality, the marginal utility of money typically decreases as income increases. For example, the first $100 earned might provide significant utility, but the 100th $100 earned might provide much less utility due to diminishing marginal utility.

## The Comparison Mechanism
To compare the utility derived from different goods and services, economists often use the concept of marginal utility. The Assumption of Constant Utility of Money simplifies comparisons by assuming that the utility of money remains constant. However, this assumption can lead to inaccurate predictions of consumer behavior, especially when analyzing high-income individuals or large changes in income.

## The Formal Math & Models
Mathematically, the assumption can be represented as a constant marginal utility of money. However, formal models that incorporate diminishing marginal utility of money provide more realistic insights into consumer behavior. These models adjust the utility function to reflect changes in the marginal utility of money as income levels change.
