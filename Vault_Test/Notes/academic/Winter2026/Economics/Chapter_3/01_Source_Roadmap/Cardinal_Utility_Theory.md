## Mental Model
The concept of Cardinal Utility Theory is essential in understanding consumer behavior in microeconomics. It is based on the idea that utility, which is a measure of satisfaction or pleasure, can be quantified and ranked. This theory assumes that consumers can assign a numerical value to the utility they derive from consuming a particular good or service.

## The Economic Intuition
The cardinal utility theory is based on marginal utility analysis. This means that the utility of a good or service is determined by the additional satisfaction a consumer derives from consuming one more unit of it. For example, consider Donald Trump who gets 60 utils of satisfaction from a burger and 40 utils from a pizza. Here, utils are the units of utility. This example illustrates how cardinal utility theory quantifies satisfaction.

## The Comparison Mechanism
In cardinal utility theory, consumers compare the utilities of different goods and services to make consumption decisions. For instance, if a consumer derives 60 utils from a burger and 40 utils from a pizza, they might choose to consume the burger because it provides more utility. This comparison mechanism helps consumers allocate their resources efficiently.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal model of cardinal utility theory can be represented using the following equation:

Let $U$ be the total utility, and $x$ and $y$ be the quantities of two goods consumed. The utility function can be written as:

$U = f(x, y)$

The marginal utility of a good is the partial derivative of the utility function with respect to the quantity of that good. For example, the marginal utility of good $x$ is:

$MU_x = \frac{\partial U}{\partial x}$
