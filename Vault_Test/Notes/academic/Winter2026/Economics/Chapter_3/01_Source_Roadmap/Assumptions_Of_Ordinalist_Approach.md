## Mental Model
The ordinalist approach in economics is a way to understand consumer behavior and preferences. It assumes that consumers can rank their preferences for different goods and services in order of satisfaction or utility.

## The Economic Intuition
The ordinalist approach is based on several key assumptions. First, it assumes that consumers can compare and rank their preferences for different combinations of goods and services. This means that consumers can say they prefer one bundle of goods to another, but they cannot necessarily quantify how much more they prefer it. The approach also assumes that consumers have not reached a saturation point for any commodity, meaning they always prefer more of a good to less. Additionally, it assumes that the total utility of a consumer is measured by the quantities of all items they consume.

## The Calculation Logic
The ordinalist approach does not require the calculation of total utility in specific numbers. Instead, it focuses on the ranking of preferences. For example, if a consumer has two bundles of goods, A and B, they can rank them as follows: they either prefer A to B, B to A, or are indifferent between the two. This ranking is based on the consumer's preferences and does not require a specific numerical value for utility.

## The Formal Math & Models
The ordinalist approach can be represented formally using a utility function that shows how a consumer's utility changes with the consumption of different goods and services. This function is often expressed as $U = f(x_1, x_2, x_3, ..., x_n)$, where $U$ is the total utility and $x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$ are the quantities of each good consumed. The approach assumes that this function is increasing, meaning that more of any good is preferred to less.
