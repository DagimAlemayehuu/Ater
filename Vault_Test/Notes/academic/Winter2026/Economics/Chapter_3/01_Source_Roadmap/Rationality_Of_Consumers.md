## Mental Model
The concept of rationality of consumers is central to understanding how individuals make decisions about the goods and services they consume. It assumes that consumers are utility maximizers, meaning they aim to get the most satisfaction or utility from the resources they have.

## The Economic Intuition
The economic intuition behind the rationality of consumers is that individuals will make choices that lead to the greatest satisfaction. This satisfaction is often referred to as utility. The assumption is that consumers can rank their preferences and make decisions based on those rankings. For example, if a consumer has to choose between two goods, they will select the one that provides more utility.

## The Calculation Logic
The calculation logic involves determining how consumers allocate their resources to maximize utility. This often involves comparing the marginal utility of different goods and services. Marginal utility refers to the additional utility gained from consuming one more unit of a good or service. Consumers will continue to consume a good or service until the marginal utility equals the marginal cost.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal math and models used to represent the rationality of consumers include the utility function and the budget constraint. The utility function represents the relationship between the quantities of goods and services consumed and the total utility obtained. The budget constraint represents the limit on the consumer's expenditure.

For a consumer choosing between two goods, $x$ and $y$, with prices $p_x$ and $p_y$, and a budget $B$, the budget constraint can be represented as:
\[ p_x x + p_y y = B \]

The utility function can be represented as:
\[ U = U(x, y) \]

The goal is to maximize $U(x, y)$ subject to the budget constraint.
