## Mental Model

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility (LDMU) is a fundamental concept in microeconomics that explains how the satisfaction or utility a consumer derives from consuming a good or service changes as they consume more of it.

## The Economic Intuition

The economic intuition behind LDMU is based on the idea that as a consumer consumes more units of a good or service, the additional satisfaction they derive from each successive unit decreases. This is because the consumer's needs are gradually being met, and the additional units provide less and less satisfaction. For example, if you are thirsty and drink a glass of water, the satisfaction you derive from that first glass is high. However, if you continue to drink more glasses of water, the satisfaction you derive from each additional glass decreases, as your thirst is gradually being quenched.

## The Calculation Logic

To calculate the marginal utility of a good or service, we need to calculate the change in total utility resulting from a one-unit increase in consumption. The formula for marginal utility is:

MU = ΔTU / ΔQ

Where MU is the marginal utility, ΔTU is the change in total utility, and ΔQ is the change in quantity consumed.

## The Formal Math & Models

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility can be represented mathematically as:

TU = f(Q)

Where TU is the total utility and Q is the quantity consumed.

The marginal utility can be represented as:

MU = dTU/dQ

The LDMU states that as Q increases, MU decreases.
