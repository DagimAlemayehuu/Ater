## Mental Model
The concept of Total and Marginal Utility is crucial in understanding consumer behavior in economics. It helps in analyzing how consumers make decisions about the goods and services they consume.

## The Economic Intuition
Total Utility (TU) refers to the overall satisfaction a consumer gets from consuming a certain quantity of a commodity at a given time. Marginal Utility (MU), on the other hand, is the additional satisfaction a consumer derives from consuming one more unit of the product. The idea is that as a consumer consumes more units of a product, the marginal utility derived from each additional unit may decrease.

## The Calculation Logic
The Marginal Utility (MU) can be calculated using the formula: $MU = \frac{\Delta TU}{\Delta Q}$, where $\Delta TU$ is the change in total utility and $\Delta Q$ is the change in the quantity of the product consumed. This calculation helps in understanding how the satisfaction changes with the consumption of additional units of a product.

## The Formal Math & Models
The relationship between Total Utility (TU) and Marginal Utility (MU) can be represented graphically. The TU curve typically increases at a decreasing rate, while the MU curve decreases. The MU curve is the slope of the TU curve at any point, illustrating how marginal utility changes with the consumption of additional units.
