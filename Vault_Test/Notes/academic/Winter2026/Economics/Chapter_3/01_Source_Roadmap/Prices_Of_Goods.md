## Mental Model
The budget line is a fundamental concept in microeconomics that represents the various combinations of two goods that a consumer can purchase given their income and the prices of the goods. Understanding how the budget line changes in response to different factors is crucial for analyzing consumer behavior.

## The Economic Intuition
The budget line changes when there is a shift in the consumer's income, the prices of goods, or due to government interventions such as taxes, subsidies, and rationing. An increase in the consumer's income will shift the budget line outward, allowing the consumer to purchase more goods. Conversely, a decrease in income will shift the budget line inward. Changes in the prices of goods also affect the budget line. For instance, if the price of one good decreases while the other remains constant, the budget line will rotate outward, making the good with the decreased price more affordable.

## The Calculation Logic
The equation of the budget line can be represented as \(P_1Q_1 + P_2Q_2 = I\), where \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) are the prices of the two goods, \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\) are the quantities of the two goods, and \(I\) is the consumer's income. When the consumer's income changes to \(I'\), or the prices change to \(P_1'\) and \(P_2'\), the budget line shifts accordingly. For example, an increase in income to \(I'\) results in a new budget line \(P_1Q_1 + P_2Q_2 = I'\).

## The Formal Math & Models
Mathematically, the slope of the budget line is given by \(-\frac{P_1}{P_2}\), which represents the rate at which one good can be substituted for the other given the consumer's income. When the price of one good changes, say \(P_1\) to \(P_1'\), the new slope becomes \(-\frac{P_1'}{P_2}\). This change in slope indicates a rotation of the budget line.
