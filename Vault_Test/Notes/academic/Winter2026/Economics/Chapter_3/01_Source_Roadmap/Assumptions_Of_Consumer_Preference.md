## Mental Model
The assumptions of consumer preference are a set of rules that help ensure consistency in how consumers make choices between different bundles of goods and services. These assumptions are crucial in understanding how consumers behave and make decisions.

## The Economic Intuition
The economic intuition behind these assumptions is to ensure that consumers' preferences are logical and consistent. This means that if a consumer prefers one bundle over another, they should be able to consistently choose between similar bundles. The assumptions help to prevent contradictory choices, such as preferring bundle A over bundle B, but then also preferring bundle B over bundle A.

## The Comparison Mechanism
The comparison mechanism involves evaluating different bundles of goods and services based on the assumptions of consumer preference. There are three key assumptions:
- **Complete**: This assumption means that a consumer can compare and rank any two bundles. They can decide which one they prefer or if they are indifferent between the two.
- **Reflexive**: This assumption states that any bundle is at least as good as itself. In other words, a consumer considers a bundle to be equal to or better than itself.
- **Transitive**: This assumption ensures that if a consumer prefers bundle A over bundle B, and bundle B over bundle C, then they also prefer bundle A over bundle C.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal math and models behind these assumptions involve representing consumer preferences using mathematical equations and functions. For example, the assumptions of completeness, reflexivity, and transitivity can be represented using the following equations:
- Completeness: For any two bundles $x$ and $y$, either $x \geq y$ or $y \geq x$.
- Reflexivity: For any bundle $x$, $x \geq x$.
- Transitivity: For any three bundles $x$, $y$, and $z$, if $x \geq y$ and $y \geq z$, then $x \geq z$.
