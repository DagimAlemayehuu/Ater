## Mental Model
Consumer preferences and utility are fundamental concepts in microeconomics that help us understand how individuals make choices about goods and services. Preferences refer to the likes and dislikes of consumers, while utility represents the satisfaction or pleasure derived from consuming goods and services.

## The Economic Intuition
The economic intuition behind consumer preferences and utility is that individuals aim to maximize their satisfaction or utility given their limited resources. Consumers have different preferences for various goods and services, and they make choices based on these preferences. The concept of utility helps economists understand how consumers make decisions and how they respond to changes in prices and income.

## The Calculation Logic
To calculate utility, economists use different approaches, including the cardinal and ordinal utility approaches. The cardinal utility approach assumes that utility can be measured numerically, while the ordinal utility approach assumes that utility can only be ranked. The ordinal utility approach is more commonly used in microeconomics.

## The Formal Math & Models
The formal math and models used to represent consumer preferences and utility include indifference curves and budget lines. An indifference curve represents a set of goods and services that provide the same level of utility to a consumer. The budget line represents the different combinations of goods and services that a consumer can afford given their income and prices.
