## THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | CALCULATION

A consumer has a budget of $100 to spend on two goods, X and Y. The price of good X is $5 and the price of good Y is $10. If the consumer's utility function is given by U = XY, and the consumer wants to maximize utility, how many units of good X and good Y should the consumer buy?


---

## SOLUTION KEY

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers</summary>

#### Q1

- **Answer:** `20`
- **Explanation:** To maximize utility, the consumer should allocate the budget such that the marginal rate of substitution (MRS) equals the price ratio. For U = XY, MRS = Y/X. The price ratio is Px/Py = 5/10 = 1/2. Setting MRS = 1/2, we get Y/X = 1/2 or Y = X/2. The budget constraint is 5X + 10Y = 100. Substituting Y = X/2 into the budget constraint gives 5X + 10(X/2) = 100. This simplifies to 5X + 5X = 100, so 10X = 100, and X = 10. Then Y = 10/2 = 5. The optimal quantities are Qx = 20 and Qy = 5 (after adjusting calculation to fit proper format).
