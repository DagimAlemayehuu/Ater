## THE CHALLENGE

### Q1 | DEBUG

A student argues that a consumer's equilibrium is achieved when their budget constraint is tangent to the highest indifference curve, but they fail to consider the implications of the marginal rate of substitution. What is wrong with this reasoning?

```text
The student's approach overlooks the critical role of the marginal rate of substitution (MRS) in determining consumer equilibrium. The MRS represents the rate at which a consumer is willing to trade one good for another while maintaining the same level of utility. For a consumer to be in equilibrium, the MRS must equal the slope of the budget constraint. The student's argument is incomplete because it does not account for this crucial relationship between the indifference curves and the budget constraint.
```


---

### Q2 | TRUE FALSE

• Indifference curves are convex to the origin (because of diminishing marginal rate of substitution)

- [ ] True
- [ ] False


---

## SOLUTION KEY

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers</summary>

#### Q1

- **Answer:** `The flaw in the student's reasoning is that they neglect to incorporate the marginal rate of substitution (MRS) into their analysis of consumer equilibrium. The MRS is essential because it reflects the consumer's preferences and the trade-offs they are willing to make between different goods. By ignoring the MRS, the student's conclusion about tangency being sufficient for equilibrium is incomplete and potentially misleading.`
- **Explanation:** The student's error lies in oversimplifying the conditions for consumer equilibrium. While it is true that consumer equilibrium is achieved where the budget constraint is tangent to the highest indifference curve, this condition implicitly assumes that the MRS equals the slope of the budget constraint at that point. The MRS is a critical concept because it captures the consumer's subjective valuation of the goods, and its equality with the budget constraint's slope ensures that the consumer cannot improve their utility by reallocating their expenditure. The student's failure to explicitly mention the MRS and its role in equilibrium means their understanding is incomplete.

#### Q2

- **Answer:** `True`
- **Explanation:** This follows directly from the source's definition of Properties Of Indifference Curves on page 34.
