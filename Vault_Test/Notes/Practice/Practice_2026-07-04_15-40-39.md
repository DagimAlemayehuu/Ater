### Q1 | CALCULATION

A consumer has a budget of $100 to spend on two goods, X and Y. The price of good X is $5 and the price of good Y is $10. If the consumer's utility function is given by U = 2X + 3Y, find the optimal quantities of X and Y that maximize the consumer's utility subject to the budget constraint.

## SOLUTION KEY

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers</summary>

#### Q1

- **Answer:** `20, 5`
- **Explanation:** To maximize utility, the consumer should allocate their budget such that the marginal utility per dollar spent on each good is equal. The budget constraint is 5X + 10Y = 100. We can express Y in terms of X: Y = (100 - 5X)/10 = 10 - 0.5X. Substitute Y into the utility function: U = 2X + 3(10 - 0.5X) = 2X + 30 - 1.5X = 0.5X + 30. To maximize U, we take the derivative of U with respect to X and set it equal to zero, but since U = 0.5X + 30 is linear in X, the maximum utility within the budget constraint occurs at the endpoint where X is maximized or minimized. Given X and Y are non-negative, we solve the budget constraint for X and Y. Setting Y = 0, we get 5X = 100, X = 20. Then, setting X = 0, we get 10Y = 100, Y = 10. The optimal quantities that maximize utility while satisfying the budget constraint are X = 20 and Y = 5.
