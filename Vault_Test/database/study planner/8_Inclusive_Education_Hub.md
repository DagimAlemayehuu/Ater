## Overview

Inclusive education is built on the foundation of collaboration and partnership among stakeholders, which enables the sharing of resources, improves service coordination, and fosters a culture of teamwork. This collaborative approach involves working together to produce or create something, leveraging individual capacities and abilities to achieve a common goal. Effective collaboration requires mutual relationships and goals, a jointly developed structure, shared responsibility, and mutual authority and accountability.

The partnership between stakeholders is a critical component of inclusive education, characterized by a range of essential elements, including an agreement, shared profits, equal and equitable relationships, non-discriminatory membership, trust, common values, defined expectations, mutual respect, synergy, and two-way communication. Stakeholders, defined as individuals, organizations, or social groups with a vital interest in the business or its activities, must work together to create an inclusive environment that values diversity and promotes equal opportunities.

By engaging with the community and promoting inclusive development, educators can ensure that all individuals, including those with diversities, are able to participate fully. Strategies for community involvement include committing to participation, establishing non-discriminatory communication, tapping into local networks, and using accessible communication channels. Through collaboration, partnership, and community involvement, educators can create a supportive and inclusive learning environment that values diversity and promotes social equity.
