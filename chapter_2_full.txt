Theory of Demand  
• Demand - quantity of a good or service that consumers 
are willing and able to buy at a given market price in a 
given time period, ceteris paribus. 
• Theory of demand - an economic principle describing the 
relationship between consumer demand for goods and 
services and their prices in the market. 
Law of  demand - price of a commodity and its quantity 
demanded are inversely related. 
– The higher the price of a product is, all else equal, the 
less of it will be demanded and vice versa
The ‘Ceteris paribus’ Assumption 
• Ceteris paribus is a Latin term widely used by 
economists to refer to a situation where ‘other things 
remain equal’ or are unchanged. 
• Ceteris paribus allows economists to simplify a 
situation by assuming that apart from a single 
change of circumstances, everything else is 
unchanged. In this way, economists can model the 
effects of one change at a time. 
• Remember to include the term in any written 
answers where relevant.

Note 
Do not confuse ‘quantity demanded’ and ‘demand’.  
Demand refers to the whole demand schedule and does not 
change when the price of the product changes. A price change 
causes a change in the quantity demanded. 
 
• A change in the price of a good or service causes a change in 
the quantity demanded (movement along the demand curve). 
• A change in a demand shifter causes a change in demand, 
which is shown as a shift of the demand curve.  
Demand shifters include preferences, the prices of related goods 
and services, income, demographic characteristics, and buyer 
expectations.

Change in Demand  
 Income of the consumer 
The effect of change in the income of a consumer on quantity 
demand of a good depends on the type of good. 
 
• Normal goods are goods whose demand increases as income 
increase. 
 
•  Inferior goods are goods whose demand is inversely related with 
income. Eg. Low quality goods (noodles), second hand clothes,…
Change in Demand
Change in Demand  
 Consumer expectation of income and price 
 
• Price Expectation: Higher price expectation will increase 
current while a lower future price expectation will decrease 
the demand for the good. 
 
• Income Expectation: Higher expectation of future income 
increase current demand while lower expectation of future 
income decrease demand.
Change in Demand  
 Number of buyer in the market 
• Since market demand is the horizontal sum of individual 
demand, an increase in the number of buyers will increase 
demand while a decrease in the number of buyers will 
decrease demand. 
 
 Taste or preference of the consumer  
• When the taste of a consumer changes in favor of a good, 
her/his demand will increase and vise versa.

Elasticity of demand 
• Elasticity of demand refers to the degree of responsiveness 
of quantity demanded of a good to a change in its price, or 
change in income, or change in prices of related goods.  
 
• There are three kinds of demand elasticity:  
o Price elasticity  
o Income elasticity 
o Cross-price elasticity

Determinants of Price Elasticity of Demand 
• The availability of substitutes: the more substitutes available for a 
product, the more elastic will be the price elasticity of demand. 
 
• Time: in the long- run, price elasticity of demand tends to be 
elastic.  
– More substitute goods could be produced. 
– People tend to adjust their consumption pattern. 
 
• The proportion of income consumers spend for a product: the 
smaller the proportion of income spent for a good, the less price 
elastic will be. 
 
• The importance of the commodity in the consumers’ budget : 
– Luxury goods: tend to be more elastic, example: gold. 
– Necessity goods: tend to be less elastic example: Salt.

Theory of Supply  
• Supply: various quantities of a product that sellers or 
producers are willing and able to provide at a given market 
price in a particular period of time. 
• Theory of Supply: an economic principle describing the 
relationship between producers supply of goods and services 
and their prices in the market. 
• The law of supply: Price and quantity supplied has a positive 
relationship.  
– It states that, ceteris paribus, as price of a product increase, 
quantity supplied of the product increases, and vice versa.

Determinants of supply 
Change in input price  
• An increase in the price of inputs such as labor, raw materials, capital, 
etc. causes a decrease in the supply of the product which is 
represented by a leftward shift of the supply curve. Likewise, a 
decrease in input price causes an increase in supply. 
Price of related goods 
• If the price of a substitute good in production rises, then the firm 
increases the supply of the goods that have a higher price. This leads 
to a decrease in the supply of the goods that have a lower price. 
• An increase in the price of a complementary good in production 
causes an increase in supply. With the higher price, sellers sell more 
of the complementary good, resulting in increased production and 
supply of this good as well.
Determinants of supply 
Suppliers' Expectations 
• Higher price expectation by suppliers in the future reduces 
the  current supply of the good and vice versa. 
 
Taxes and Subsidies 
• Taxes reduces profits, therefore increase in taxes reduce 
supply whereas decrease in taxes increase supply. Subsidies 
reduce the burden of production costs on suppliers, thus 
increasing the profits. Therefore increase in subsidies increase 
supply and decrease in subsidies decrease supply.
Determinants of supply 
Change in weather condition 
• A change in weather condition will have an impact on the 
supply of a number of products, esp. agricultural products. 
Eg., Good weather condition boosts the supply of agricultural 
products, other things remaining unchanged. This shifts the 
supply curve of a given agricultural product outward. Bad 
weather condition will have the opposite impact. 
 
Change in Technology 
• Technological advancement enables a firm to produce and 
supply more in the market. This shifts the supply curve 
outward.

• Price elasticity of supply can be elastic, inelastic, unitary elastic, 
perfectly elastic or perfectly inelastic.
Determinants of elasticity of supply 
 
• Length and complexity of production: the less complex and the shorter to 
produce a good, the more elastic will be its supply, and vice versa  
 
• Mobility/availability of factors: If the factors of production are easily 
available and if a producer producing one good can switch their resources 
and put it towards the creation of a product in demand, then it can be said 
that the PES is relatively elastic. The inverse applies to this, to make it 
relatively inelastic. 
 
• Time to respond: The more time a producer has to respond to price 
changes the more elastic the supply. Supply is normally more elastic in 
the  long run that in the short run  for produced goods.   
 
• Inventories: A producer who has a supply of goods or available storage 
capacity can quickly increase supply to market making supply more elastic.
Determinants of elasticity of supply 
 
• Changes in marginal cost of production. If with the expansion of output, 
marginal cost increases and marginal return declines, the price elasticity of 
supply will be less elastic to that extent. 
 
• Spare or excess production capacity: A producer who has unused capacity 
can (and will) quickly respond to price changes in his market assuming 
that variable factors are readily available, resulting in more elastic supply. 
 
• Availability of infrastructure facilities: If infrastructure facilities are 
available for expanding output of a particular good in response to the rise 
in prices, the elasticity of supply will be relatively more elastic. 
 
• Easiness of entering in the market : goods operating in markets of 
free/easy entrance have relatively more elastic supply.

c)  Effects of Combined Changes in Demand and Supply 
 
• When both demand and supply increase, the equilibrium 
quantity of the product will increase definitely. 
• But, it is not certain whether the price will rise or fall.  
– If an increase in demand is more than an increase in supply, 
then the price goes up.  
– If an increase in supply is more than an increase in demand, the 
price falls but the quantity increases.  
– If the increase in demand and supply is same, then the price 
remains the same. 
• The same principle applies for a decreases in both demand 
and supply.
