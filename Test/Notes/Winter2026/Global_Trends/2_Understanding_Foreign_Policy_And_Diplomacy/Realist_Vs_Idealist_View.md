## Mental Model

Imagine a shipbuilder constructing a vessel. The realist view is like a shipbuilder focused on building a sturdy, seaworthy ship with a strong hull and robust engine, prioritizing its ability to navigate treacherous waters and protect its crew. In contrast, the idealist view is like a shipbuilder who designs a ship with luxurious amenities and eco-friendly features, aiming to create a vessel that's not only functional but also a beacon of hope and sustainability, reflecting the builder's moral values.

## The Global Economic Engine

The **Realist Vs Idealist View** refers to two distinct perspectives on [[Foreign_Policy]], with realists focusing on power and survival, and idealists emphasizing morality and justice. Realists prioritize pragmatic criteria when defining [[National_Interest]] and employing **foreign policy**, whereas idealists stress the relevance of legal, ideological, and moral elements.

The underlying mechanism of the Realist View is that **national interest** is defined in terms of pursuits of power, which is about establishing control or influencing the behaviors of others, either diplomatically or through coercion. International politics is seen as a struggle among states, and thus the prime interest of a state is survival and security. In contrast, idealists do not separate legal and moral factors from reality and derive specific actions and objectives of [[Foreign_Policy]] from general moral and legal guidelines and principles. This fundamental difference in perspective leads to distinct approaches to **foreign policy**, with realists emphasizing prudence and careful balancing of needs and aspirations, and idealists focusing on addressing global problems through moral and legal frameworks.

The real-world significance of the **Realist Vs Idealist View** lies in its implications for addressing global problems. Realists are criticized for failing to recognize and prescribe solutions for global issues due to their exclusive emphasis on state and [[National_Interest]]. Idealists, on the other hand, believe in the prevalence of common human problems, such as environmental pollution and ecological imbalance, and advocate for the establishment of new institutions with a global orientation to address these challenges. Ultimately, the Realist Vs Idealist View highlights the complexities and trade-offs involved in shaping foreign policy and addressing global problems.

## The Macro Model & Jargon

The **Realist Vs Idealist View** dichotomy is a fundamental paradigm in [[Foreign_Policy]] analysis, characterized by distinct epistemological and ontological assumptions. Realists prioritize state-centric, power-based [[National_Interest]], whereas idealists emphasize moral, legal, and ideological considerations. This paradigmatic divide influences the determination of **national interest**, with realists focusing on survival and security, and idealists stressing global problem-solving. The realist view operationalizes [[National_Interest]] through pragmatic, power-based calculations, whereas the idealist view integrates moral and legal frameworks into **foreign policy** decision-making. A nuanced understanding of these competing perspectives is essential for effective [[Foreign_Policy]] analysis.
