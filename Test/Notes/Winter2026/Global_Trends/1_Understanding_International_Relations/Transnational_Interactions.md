## Mental Model

Imagine a vast, intricate network of rivers and canals, where water flows not just within individual lakes (states) but also across them, forming complex, interconnected waterways. Just as rivers can flow through and connect multiple lakes, **transnational interactions** represent the flows of people, goods, and ideas that move across state borders, transcending traditional boundaries. As the water flows, it carries with it the influences and exchanges that shape global affairs, illustrating how issues and challenges can no longer be confined within the borders of individual states.

## Global Strategy

**Transnational interactions** refer to the phenomenon where individuals and groups interact across borders, marking a shift away from traditional "inter-national" relations, which occur between states. This concept acknowledges that societal and political issues, challenges, and problems are no longer confined by the borders of individual states or groups of states. As a result, thinking about world affairs in "trans-national" terms has become an analytical necessity.

The underlying mechanism driving **transnational interactions** involves the changing nature of global affairs, where state borders no longer accurately delimitate global interactions. The majority of global interactions, including those related to finance, production, education, travel, labor migration, and terrorism, no longer occur solely through state channels. This shift has led to a greater emphasis on non-state actors and cross-border issues, effectively altering the general dynamics of international relations. Consequently, the [[International_System]] can be conceived of as comprising states, groups of states, organizations, societies, or individuals within and across those societies.

The real-world significance of transnational interactions lies in their ability to reveal the complex and wide-ranging dynamics that emerge from the global economic system, affecting its various components, including states, national economies, societies, and individuals. By considering transnational interactions, researchers can grasp the big picture and better understand developments that are outside the immediate control of any particular state or group of states, such as the global economy, transnational terrorism, and the internet. This perspective ultimately highlights the need to move beyond traditional state-centric approaches to understanding international relations.

This concept is fundamentally connected to [[Non_State_Actors]] and operates within the [[State_Sovereignty]] framework.
