Just as a sturdy foundation and load-bearing walls determine the overall layout and stability of a building, nationalism asserts that the nation is the basic unit that shapes and gives meaning to the lives of its people. ## Global Strategy

Nationalism is defined as the doctrine that asserts the nation as the basic political unit in organizing society. This definition highlights the central role that nationalism assigns to the nation, positioning it as the fundamental building block around which societal and political structures are organized.

The underlying mechanism of nationalism, as per this definition, involves the prioritization of the nation's interests and identity above other considerations. This leads to the formation of a community of people who share a common identity, culture, and often, a historical narrative. The nation, in this context, is not just a collection of individuals but a cohesive unit that demands loyalty and allegiance from its members. This doctrine influences how societies are organized, with the nation's needs and aspirations taking precedence over other potential organizing principles.

The real-world significance of nationalism lies in its profound impact on international affairs. It has been a driving force behind numerous revolutions and wars, the collapse of empires, and the emergence of new states. Nationalism continues to shape global politics, with many nation-states facing pressures that challenge their roles in world politics. Moreover, the resurgence of nationalism, often tied to assertions of religion, culture, and ethnicity, underscores its ongoing relevance and influence in contemporary international relations. Understanding nationalism, therefore, is crucial for grasping the complexities of global politics and the dynamics that shape international interactions.
