# 2. The Core Execution

### Global Strategy

Theories of international relations allow us to understand and try to make sense of the world around us through various lenses, each of which represents a different theoretical perspective. These theories provide a framework for analyzing the complex interactions that take place across state boundaries, involving not only the diplomatic core or representatives of individual states, but also the business community, the media, charitable organizations, and so on.

The underlying mechanism of **international relations theories** lies in their distinct assumptions about human nature, the role of the state, and the dynamics of global interactions. For instance, idealism or liberalism views human beings as innately good and believes that peace and harmony between nations is not only achievable but desirable. In contrast, other theories such as [[Realism]], structuralism, and critical theories offer alternative perspectives on the nature of international relations, often highlighting factors such as power, economics, and social structures. Constructivism, commonly viewed as a middle ground between mainstream theories and critical theories, emphasizes the importance of values and shared interests between individuals who interact on the global stage.

The real-world significance of **international relations theories** lies in their ability to help us understand and navigate the complexities of global politics. By providing different lenses through which to view international interactions, these theories enable policymakers, scholars, and practitioners to identify patterns, predict outcomes, and develop more effective strategies for addressing global challenges. Moreover, critical theories, by identifying positions that have typically been ignored or overlooked within international relations, provide a voice to marginalized groups and offer new insights into the workings of global power structures. Ultimately, international relations theories offer a way to chronicle the accumulation of accepted norms of the past and the emerging norms of the future, highlighting the dynamic and ever-changing nature of global politics.
