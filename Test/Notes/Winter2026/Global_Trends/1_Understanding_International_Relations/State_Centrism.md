# 2. The Core Execution

### Global Strategy

State-centrism is a foundational concept in international relations where the state is considered the primary actor and the main point of reference for understanding global politics. This concept is deeply ingrained in the discipline of international relations, serving as a cornerstone for analyzing and interpreting global interactions.

The underlying mechanism of state-centrism lies in the assumption that the state is the dominant unit of analysis, with the state acting as the arena in which state officials, politicians, and decision-makers operate. The state is seen as the framework that encapsulates society and serves as the main point of reference for the individual. Furthermore, international relations scholars conceive of the state as a point of reference for other types of actors, implying that the state is where power is primarily concentrated and located. This perspective posits that today's political life remains managed within the state framework, based on issues like national security, domestic cohesion, or internal stability.

The real-world significance of state-centrism lies in its implications for understanding global politics and international relations. With states forming the primary kind of actor in major international organizations, such as the United Nations, and still holding a monopoly on violence - the exclusive right to the legitimate use of physical force - state-centrism remains a relevant and influential perspective. This perspective has far-reaching consequences for how scholars and policymakers understand and engage with global issues, reinforcing the state as a central player in international relations.
