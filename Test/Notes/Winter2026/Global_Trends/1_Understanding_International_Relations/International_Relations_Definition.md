Just as the stability and functionality of the bridge rely on the interplay between the pillars and the connecting cables, international relations depend on the dynamic interactions and transactions between nation-states and other actors in the global arena. As pillars face stress and strain, and new roadways are added or old ones modified, the bridge adapts, reflecting the evolving challenges and pressures on nation-states and the [[International_System]].

## Global Strategy

International relations refers to the field of study that examines the interactions and transactions between nations, states, and other actors in the global arena. This field of study encompasses the analysis of the complex relationships between various entities that operate in the [[International_System]], shaping the structure of the system and determining the relative influence of actors.

The underlying mechanism of international relations is rooted in the concept of [[Power_Relations]] in the supra-national domain. According to Hans Morgenthau, a prominent thinker of [[Realism]] theory, international politics is a struggle for power, where power is the lifeblood of international relations. Power can be defined in terms of both relational and material aspects. Robert Dahl's relational definition of power understands power as one actor's ability to get another actor to do something it would not otherwise do. This definition implies that power is not solely determined by capabilities, but also by the relationships between actors. A small rise in the capabilities of one nation can translate into a major advantage in terms of power balance, as seen in the historical example of the United States and Soviet Union.

The study of international relations has significant real-world implications, as it enables students and professionals to understand the complexities of the global arena and the impact of international transactions on their lives. The limits to how international relations will continue to shape individual experiences and transactions are tremendous. As the world becomes increasingly interconnected, the role of international relations in shaping global politics, economies, and cultures will only continue to grow. Understanding international relations is essential for navigating the complexities of the global system and for making informed decisions about international interactions and transactions.
