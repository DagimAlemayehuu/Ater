# 1. The Intuitive Analogy

Imagine a vast, intricate clockwork system, where nation-states are like interlocking gears, each with its own rhythm and movement. The [[International_System]] is like the framework that holds these gears in place, influencing how they interact, mesh, and sometimes clash, ultimately shaping the global rhythm. Just as the clockwork's structure and gear movements determine the time and its changes, the **international system**'s structure and the interactions of its key actors - like powerful nations, international organizations, and non-state actors - determine the dynamics and outcomes of global events.

# 2. The Core Execution

### Global Strategy

International relations is essentially about actors' [[Power_Relations]] in the supra-national domain. It determines the relative influence of actors and it shapes the structure of the [[International_System]]. This field of study enables students and professionals to understand the dynamics at play in the global arena.

The underlying mechanism of international relations revolves around the concept of power, which is the lifeblood of international relations. Power can be defined in terms of both relations and material aspects. A relational definition of power, formulated by Robert Dahl, understands power as "A's" ability to get "B" to do something it would not otherwise do. This definition implies that power is not solely based on capabilities, but also on the ability to influence others. The balance of power between nations can shift with even a small change in capabilities, as seen in the historical example of the United States and Soviet Union.

The real-world significance of international relations lies in its impact on our daily lives. We live in a world where it is impossible to isolate our experiences and transactions from an international dimension. The limits to how international relations will continue to impact our lives are tremendous. Understanding international relations helps us navigate the complexities of global politics, where nationalism, culture, and ethnicity are potent forces. As Hans Morgenthau, a famous thinker of [[Realism]] theory in IR, argues that international politics, like all other politics, is a struggle for power. This struggle plays out in the **international system**, which operates like a constitution, setting out the consensus of its constituent actors on the distribution of authority.
