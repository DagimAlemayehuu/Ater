# 1. The Intuitive Analogy

Here is a mechanical analogy for **Sovereignty**:

Imagine a set of interconnected, hermetically sealed pressure vessels, each representing a sovereign state. Each vessel has its own pressure regulator, allowing it to control its internal environment (domestic affairs) independently, while also having a unique interface to connect with other vessels, free to negotiate and interact with them as it sees fit (foreign policy). Just as the vessels are equal in design and function, with no single vessel having overriding control over the others, states are formally equal, with **sovereignty** and autonomy to govern themselves and engage with others on their own terms.

# 2. The Core Execution

### Global Strategy

**Sovereignty** is defined as a state's autonomy in foreign policy and independence in domestic affairs. This concept is foundational to the modern [[International_System]], where states interact with each other as the primary political units. The definition of **sovereignty** encompasses two key aspects: internal sovereignty, which refers to a state's ultimate authority within its territorial entity, and external sovereignty, which pertains to a state's involvement in the international community.

The underlying mechanism of sovereignty is rooted in the idea that states have the exclusive right to rule their own territories and act as they see fit in relation to other states. This leads to a system where all states are formally equal, with the same rights and obligations. However, this equality also means that there is no overarching power in the **international system**, resulting in a decentralized structure where power is not concentrated in a single entity. In this context, sovereignty and formal equality lead to the problem of [[Anarchy]], which is characterized by the absence of authority and the lack of shared institutions with the right to enforce common rules.

Ultimately, sovereignty shapes the [[International_System]] into one where states must navigate complex relationships and balance their power to achieve their goals.

**Incomplete Territorial Control**: A state's **sovereignty** is compromised when it lacks effective control over parts of its claimed territory, leading to challenges in enforcing its authority.
**External Interference**: The principle of sovereignty is threatened when external actors interfere in a state's domestic affairs, undermining its independence.
**Power Imbalance**: The existence of significant power disparities among states can limit the effectiveness of sovereignty, as smaller or weaker states may be coerced or influenced by more powerful ones.
