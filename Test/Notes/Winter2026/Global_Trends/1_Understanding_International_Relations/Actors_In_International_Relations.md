# 2. The Core Execution

### Global Strategy

**Actors in International Relations** refer to entities that interact with each other in the global arena, influencing the structure and dynamics of international politics. These actors include a vast array of political entities, such as international organizations, multinational corporations, societies, and citizens, in addition to the traditional focus on states. The concept of **actors in international relations** has evolved over time, broadening the conventional view to encompass relationships between all sorts of political entities.

The underlying mechanism that defines actors in international relations is rooted in their ability to exert influence and shape the [[International_System]]. States, for instance, have a territorial extension, borders, capitals, armies, foreign ministries, flags, and national anthems, and claim the exclusive right to govern their respective territories in their own fashion, which they refer to as [[Sovereignty]]. This **sovereignty** allows states to interact with each other on the world stage, with international politics defined in terms of these interactions. The relative influence of actors is determined by their power, which in turn shapes the structure of the **international system**.

The real-world significance of actors in international relations lies in their impact on global politics and the lives of individuals. The interactions between actors can have far-reaching consequences, from shaping international policies and agreements to influencing economic outcomes and social norms. As the international landscape continues to evolve, understanding the complex relationships between actors in international relations is crucial for navigating the intricacies of global politics and addressing pressing global challenges. The study of actors in international relations provides valuable insights into the dynamics of international politics, enabling scholars and policymakers to better comprehend the complexities of the global arena.
