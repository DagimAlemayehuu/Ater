## Mental Model

Imagine a grand, intricately designed cathedral with stunning stained glass windows, where the shared values and interests of the community are the architectural blueprints and the interactions between individuals are the skilled craftsmen laying the stones. Just as the cathedral's beauty and structure depend on the interplay of its various components, such as the foundation, arches, and vaults, Constructivism sees international relations as emerging from the dynamic interactions and shared understandings among individuals, which can lead to the creation of new, sometimes unconventional, "architectural" forms of global politics. As new craftsmen with innovative ideas and tools, like digital platforms, arrive on the scene, they can reshape the global landscape, creating novel structures and patterns that transcend traditional boundaries.

## Global Strategy

The **Constructivist Perspective** is defined as a theoretical approach that highlights the importance of values and shared interests between individuals who interact on the global stage, and posits that international relations exist in the interactions between people. This perspective is commonly viewed as a middle ground between mainstream theories and [[Critical_Theories]].

The underlying mechanism of Constructivism is based on the relationship between agents, such as individuals, and structures, such as the state. According to Alexander Wendt, the essence of international relations is that it is agents of states, like politicians and diplomats, who interact, not the states themselves. This perspective argues that international [[Anarchy]] is not an inherent fact, but rather "**anarchy** is what states make of it," implying that the nature of international relations can change if a critical mass of individuals, and by proxy the states they represent, accept new ideas and norms.

The real-world significance of the **Constructivist Perspective** lies in its implications for understanding the dynamics of international relations. If international relations are shaped by the interactions of individuals and the norms they create, then it is possible for individuals to effect change and create new systems of international relations. This perspective suggests that international relations are in a constant state of flux, with emerging norms of the future replacing the accepted norms of the past. As a result, Constructivism offers a nuanced understanding of the complex and ever-changing nature of international relations, and highlights the agency of individuals in shaping the global order.
