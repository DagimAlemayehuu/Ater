## Global Strategy

**Human Nature In International Relations** refers to the role of human characteristics, behaviors, and tendencies in shaping interactions between nations and international organizations. Realists do not typically believe that human beings are inherently good, or have the potential for good, as liberals do. This fundamental difference in perspective on human nature underlies distinct approaches to understanding international relations.

The underlying mechanism of **Human Nature In International Relations** involves the assumption that human behavior is driven by certain innate characteristics, which in turn influence state behavior and international interactions. Liberals, for instance, view human beings as innately good and believe that peace and harmony between nations is not only achievable but also desirable. This perspective is often associated with idealistic or utopian theories of international relations, which emphasize the potential for cooperation and peaceful resolution of conflicts. In contrast, realists are more skeptical about human nature, implying that self-interest, power, and coercion are more reliable drivers of state behavior.

The real-world significance of Human Nature In International Relations lies in its implications for understanding and predicting international interactions, cooperation, and conflict. If human beings are indeed innately good, then international relations can be expected to trend towards greater cooperation, peace, and harmony. Conversely, if human nature is driven by self-interest and a desire for power, then international relations are likely to be characterized by competition, conflict, and the pursuit of national interests. This has significant consequences for policymakers, international organizations, and other actors seeking to promote peace, stability, and cooperation in the [[International_System]].
