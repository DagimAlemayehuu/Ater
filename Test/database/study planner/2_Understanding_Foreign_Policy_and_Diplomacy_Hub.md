## Overview

The study of foreign policy and diplomacy revolves around understanding the actions, decisions, and goals that states pursue to achieve and promote their national interests. At its core, national interest is a set of values, orientation, goals, and objectives a country seeks to achieve in its international relations, which can vary depending on one's perspective, such as realist or idealist views. This concept is crucial as it guides foreign policy decisions, which can be influenced by various criteria including ideological, moral, legal, pragmatic, and professional advancement considerations.

A key aspect of foreign policy is the scope of a country's activities and interests, which can range from global to regional or narrow isolationist approaches. Countries can operate multilaterally through diplomatic forums or unilaterally through individual actions. Foreign policy objectives are categorized into short-term, middle-term, and long-term goals, reflecting a country's priorities and strategies for achieving its national interests. Core interests and values, such as sovereignty, independence, and self-preservation, are fundamental short-range objectives.

The intersection of foreign policy and diplomacy involves the actions states take towards each other, tied to larger purposes and objectives. Diplomacy can involve bargaining, economic muscle, and other forms of influence to achieve strategic and symbolic objectives. Through economic diplomacy, states use rewards and punishments to affect the behavior of others. Maintaining the status quo or seeking to change it through various means, including foreign aid and diplomatic engagement, are also critical components. By synthesizing these concepts, it becomes clear that foreign policy and diplomacy are complex, multifaceted fields that require an understanding of national interests, strategic objectives, and the dynamic interactions between states.
